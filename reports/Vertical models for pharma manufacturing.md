# LLMs own the paperwork, not the process

Existing autoregressive language models, scaled and wrapped in harnesses, are already sufficient for the text layer of pharmaceutical manufacturing (deviations, CAPA, batch-record digitisation, SOP retrieval, natural-language data access) as long as a qualified human owns the output, and they are not sufficient as the numeric engine for process dynamics, spectra, fault detection or control: in every published system that works on those tasks, the numbers come from a non-LLM component. That does not make the second half of the thesis true. No pharma or bioprocess foundation model with a published benchmark exists; generic numeric foundation models tie with one-line detectors on fault detection and lose to a 256-sample ARX fit on input-response; a generic tabular model beats Raman-specific networks; hybrid mechanistic models already work with tens of runs; and a general LLM writing forecasting code inside a search loop has topped a snapshot of a time-series foundation-model leaderboard. The version of the thesis that survives is narrower and rests on data, not architecture: **a small, numeric-native, simulator-pretrained process model that adapts in context to a site's few batches, emits typed outputs with validated uncertainty, and sits inside a hybrid where the language model is replaceable**. Its moat is data rights, simulators and verified test sets, and accumulated validation evidence. That is also what Y Combinator's request literally asks for: its named hard parts are verifiable rewards, data rights and a win over existing specialist models, which in pharma means PLS, hybrid models and MPC, not GPT. The "Jev" it credits is TypeSafe AI's typed-decision model, an interface precedent that takes text input and is documented as weak at arithmetic; it is not JEPA. Two cautions frame everything below: EU GMP Annex 22 is still a consultation draft as of 19 November 2026, and the most decision-relevant experiment (frontier LLM with tools versus specialist numeric model versus local hybrid or classical baseline on bioprocess tasks) has not been published by anyone, so the founder can run it first.

**How to read this report.** The researchers' shared web-search budget ran out part-way through, so verification is uneven. Claims are tagged where it matters: **[vendor]** for company-reported figures, **[snippet]** for facts seen only in a search-result summary, **[abstract]** where only the abstract was read, **[preprint]** for work that is not peer reviewed, **[draft]** for regulatory text not in force, and **[synthesis]** for conclusions drawn by the researchers or by this report (these are not cited findings). Numbers described as "full text" in the notes were pulled by an automated reader from arXiv HTML and should be spot-checked against the PDFs before being quoted externally.

## A plant produces three kinds of information: token-like, re-encodable and unrecorded

Start from what a manufacturing process is. A bioreactor, a chromatography column or a tablet press is a physical system with dynamics, observed through sensors at different rates, steered by setpoints and manipulated variables, and judged by a small number of late laboratory results. A cell-culture run lasts about two weeks, with nutrients and metabolites measured offline roughly once a day or every 12 to 24 hours, and new products have few runs because each is expensive ([Khuat et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)). A Raman or infrared probe adds thousands of variables per time point against a handful of calibration samples, the "Low-N" problem in which training samples are far fewer than data dimensions ([Peng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12322v2)). The reference measurements are themselves noisy (viable-cell counts are described as "notoriously bad" late in a run), online instruments are scarce in downstream steps such as chromatography, and moving a product to a new site resets the historical record ([Khuat et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)). Small-molecule production is denser but no richer in labels: the one large public dataset from a commercial tablet line holds **1,005 batches over about 30 months, with press signals logged every 10 seconds and one row of laboratory results per batch** ([Žagar and Mihelič 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01203-x)).

Wrapped around that physical core is a quality system that is discrete and written in language. Recipes, equipment capabilities, procedures and batch production records have standard hierarchical schemas in ISA-88, and the levels from sensing to ERP are defined in ISA-95 ([ISA-88](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-88-standards); [ISA-95](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-95-standard)). Deviations are free text in volumes "too many to manually review for hidden patterns", in the words of Eli Lilly's quality group ([Lilly, TCD QP Forum 2026](https://www.tcd.ie/media/tcd/pharmacy/images/qp-forums/qp-forum-2026/presentations/3.-Innovation--Advancing-GMP---Applying-AIML-to-Deviation-Trend-Analysis.pdf)), and FDA itself lists mining complaints and deviation reports as a manufacturing use of AI ([FDA discussion paper 2023](https://www.fda.gov/media/165743/download)). Many batch records are still paper or scans: a vendor-authored study of three real records reports handwriting read at about 85% accuracy and step-level accuracy of 75 to 83%, with every extracted record requiring qualified review ([MasterControl, EACL 2026 Industry](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04368v1)) **[vendor, n=3]**.

The table below sorts the modalities. The properties come from the sources above; the classification is **[synthesis]** by the researchers and this report, not a published taxonomy.

| Information | Natural structure | Verdict | What the evidence suggests as an encoding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deviations, CAPA, change control, SOPs, validation documents, filings | Natural language | Token-like | Text tokens; general LLM with retrieval |
| Recipes, master batch records, specifications | Hierarchical symbols (ISA-88) | Token-like | Schema-bound text with validation |
| MES and electronic batch record events, alarms, audit trails | Timestamped discrete events | Token-like, but as event tokens | Event vocabularies, as in medical-event models |
| Lot genealogy, equipment trains, P&IDs | Graphs | Serialisable, better kept as graphs | Property graphs queried by tools ([Alimin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18928)) |
| Historian trajectories (pH, DO, temperature, feeds) | Multivariate, multi-rate, causal continuous signals | Needs a different encoding | Normalised patches with typed channel roles for setpoint, manipulated variable, measured response and context ([FactoryNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09081)) |
| Raman, NIR, chromatograms | Continuous functions, thousands of channels | Needs a different encoding | Float vectors into tabular or spectral encoders; latent variables |
| Offline assays, CQAs, release tests | Sparse, delayed, noisy scalars | Needs a different encoding | Time-stamped values with explicit uncertainty; distributional output heads |
| Product, clone, scale, site identity | Categorical with hidden similarity | Needs a different encoding | Learned embeddings ([Hutter et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13863)) |
| Design space, residence-time behaviour | Continuous regions and operators | Needs a different encoding | Mechanistic or hybrid models; the regulatory object is a multidimensional region ([ICH Q8(R2)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q8%28R2%29%20Guideline.pdf)) |
| Response to a manipulated input | Counterfactual | Not in routine GMP data | Requires designed excitation or a simulator |
| Operator tacit knowledge | Unrecorded | Cannot be encoded until captured | Capture through corrections and review |
| Batch disposition, "most probable" root cause | Accountable human judgement | Recordable, not a pure function of data | Decision support with human authorisation |

Three points sharpen the middle of that table. First, "non-token" is too coarse a slogan. Subword tokenisation does damage numeracy: GPT-4o's float-addition accuracy falls from about 90% on short numbers to under 5% on very long ones, and fractions and scientific notation average below 20% ([Number Cookbook, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03766v3)). But the fixes are not uniformly continuous. xVal's single scaled number token wins on weather and orbit regression and recovers three significant figures on a lookup task only 6% of the time against 99.9% for a text encoding ([xVal](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02989v2)); a regression-style loss on ordinary digit tokens matches a dedicated regression head ([Zausinger et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02083v3)); and reasoning models have closed much of the gap on numeric retrieval, with o3-mini at 96.8 where GPT-4o scored 41.7 ([NumericBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11075v2)). Several celebrated specialists are token models with a different vocabulary: Google's 60M-parameter Regression Language Model, trained from scratch on cluster logs, reads text and decodes numbers as sign, mantissa and exponent tokens ([Akhauri et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21718)) **[preprint]**. The defensible claim is "domain-native encoding and output heads trained on domain data", not "no tokens".

Second, the forecasting community has already voted on how to encode dense signals. Amazon moved its Chronos line from quantised value tokens to continuous patches with direct quantile outputs, and the 2025 to 2026 leaders (Chronos-2, TimesFM-3, Timestral, t0) all use normalised patches, attention across variates and explicit covariate roles ([Chronos-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1); [Mistral Timestral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/)). None of them natively handles the multi-rate mix of second-level sensors, hourly spectra, daily assays and one batch-end result; a 2026 benchmark on irregular series finds no strategy dominates ([BITS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33303)) **[preprint]**. The representation of "N prior batches of this product as context for batch N+1" is an open design problem **[synthesis]**.

Third, some of what a founder would most like to encode is absent from the data. Regulators at the 2026 EMA workshop observed that GMP "often uses tacit knowledge and experience to create controls beyond formally identified risks" ([EMA workshop report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)). Routine batches hold setpoints inside validated ranges, so the plant's response to a changed input is barely observed; the control evidence below shows that models cannot recover it from unexcited history. Disposition is a legally accountable decision of the owner's quality unit, which is "ultimately responsible" for approving or rejecting product ([FDA quality agreements guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/86193/download)). And whatever representation a model learns is a derived record: data-integrity rules require that original time-stamped data and metadata stay traceable ([FDA data integrity Q&A](https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download)), which is architecture-neutral **[synthesis]**.

## Job by job, the numbers never come from the language model

Organising by what manufacturing needs a model to do gives a different answer for each job. The table is this report's **[synthesis]**; the evidence follows.

| Job | Does a general LLM with tools suffice today? | Is a vertical foundation model shown to be needed? | Incumbent a new model must beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read, draft, search and trend the quality record | Yes, with qualified human review | No; vertical text models have been overtaken repeatedly | General LLM plus retrieval |
| Forecast trajectories and quality attributes | Not as the numeric engine; yes as a code author when a metric and held-out data exist | Not shown; no bioprocess evaluation exists | Hybrid model, PLS, tuned linear model, zero-shot time-series model |
| Soft-sense from spectra | No | Not shown; a generic tabular model leads | PLS, TabPFN-2.5 |
| Detect and diagnose faults | No for detection; useful for explanation after a classical detector | Not shown; frozen foundation models tie one-line baselines | PCA and multivariate SPC, variance and difference baselines, small autoencoders |
| Answer "what if" and control | No for regulation; conditionally for supervision behind a gate | Generic models fail; simulator fine-tuning repairs them. The clearest opening | ARX identification, PID, MPC, residence-time models |
| Adapt to a new product, scale or site with few runs | Untested | Not shown; transfer evidence is small and mostly simulated | Hybrid model with product embedding, Gaussian processes, DoE |
| Propose model structure and experiments | Yes, as proposer around a numeric fitter and evaluator | No | Expert modeller, Bayesian optimisation |
| Return a typed decision with trustworthy uncertainty | Achievable with pinned open weights and logit scoring; hosted frontier APIs fail reproducibility by default | A calibration advantage for vertical models is untested | Supervised small classifier plus a conformal or threshold layer |

### The quality record is language, and vertical language models keep being overtaken

On the document layer the thesis fails. Merck & Co. tested GPT-3.5, GPT-4 and Claude-2 on a small set of real manufacturing deviations and reports "high accuracy" extracting root cause and finding similar records by embedding search, with no metrics in the abstract ([Salami et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15578)) **[preprint, abstract]**. Lilly clusters deviation text with embeddings and has an LLM label the clusters, leaving the reviewer to judge whether a cluster is a real trend ([Lilly slides](https://www.tcd.ie/media/tcd/pharmacy/images/qp-forums/qp-forum-2026/presentations/3.-Innovation--Advancing-GMP---Applying-AIML-to-Deviation-Trend-Analysis.pdf)). The best adjacent numbers come from clinical-protocol deviations at Roche, where an open LLM reached at best 85.44% recall with 49.44% precision on 309 records while the experts themselves disagreed 20% of the time ([Zou et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12181094/)). That is useful, imperfect and firmly human-in-the-loop, which is also where regulators put it: FDA's April 2026 warning letter over unreviewed AI-generated specifications and master records states that AI-generated documents must be reviewed, as reported by a law firm (the recipient's identity was not confirmed against the letter itself) ([Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/fda-warns-against-over-reliance-on-ai-pharmaceutical-manufacturing-but-how-much-reliance-is-too-much)). Reported cycle-time gains such as a 40% reduction in deviation closure time come from a McKinsey article seen only as a search snippet ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/life-sciences/our-insights/gen-ai-a-game-changer-for-biopharma-operations)) **[snippet]** and should be treated as unverified.

Building a language model specialised on GMP text is the bet with the worst precedent. GPT-4 with general prompting beat specialist medical models on all nine MultiMedQA datasets, and the next model generation then beat that harness without any prompting technique, 96.0% to 90.2% on MedQA ([Nori et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16452); [Nori et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03590)). Medical LLMs beat their own general base models in only 12.1% of 3-shot comparisons ([Jeong et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.677/)). Harvey's custom legal model, once preferred by attorneys 97% of the time, was outperformed by seven general models within a year by Harvey's own account ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/anthropic-google-score-win-by-nabbing-openai-backed-harvey-as-a-user/)). The one pharma-manufacturing data point runs the same way: on text-to-SQL over a synthetic batch and MES database, code-tuned general 7B to 8B models produced queries for every task and the biomedical model Meditron failed on nearly all ([Bhetwal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01338)) **[preprint, small synthetic study]**.

### Forecasting and soft sensing reward numeric-native models, but the winners are generic or tiny

The cleanest evidence that a language backbone is the wrong numeric engine is an ablation. Removing the LLM from three popular LLM-based forecasters, or replacing it with one attention layer, matched or beat the original in **26 of 26, 22 of 26 and 19 of 26 cases**; Time-LLM carried 6,652M parameters against 0.55M for its ablation, and the LLM-based methods ran 28 times slower at inference on average ([Tan et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16964v2)). Purpose-built forecasters, by contrast, now clearly beat classical statistics on diverse public data: Chronos-2 scores a 47.3% skill score on fev-bench against 20.6% for AutoARIMA ([Chronos-2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)) **[vendor-lab preprint]**.

The evidence against stopping there is just as concrete. A Gemini-based system that searches over candidate programs built per-dataset solutions from scikit-learn, XGBoost and statsmodels that "outperformed the entire May 18, 2025 leaderboard" of time-series foundation models on GIFT-Eval, on a frozen snapshot with the five largest datasets excluded from search ([Aygün et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06503v3)) **[preprint; a Nature version is indicated by a search result and not confirmed]**. When the deciding information is textual, a directly prompted LLM beats the numeric specialists outright (0.159 against 0.326 for Chronos-Large on a context-dependent benchmark), while also producing occasional failures of 500% of range or more ([Context is Key, ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18959v4)). Batch context such as recipe changes, raw-material lots and deviation notes is text, and a numeric-only model cannot use it. And none of this was measured on a bioreactor: no evaluation of any LLM or foundation-model forecaster on real cell-culture, chromatography or batch-trajectory data was found. The one fed-batch-specific pretrained model located, Time-FLM, could not be opened ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705126003205)) **[snippet]**.

For spectra the 2026 state of the art is a horizontal model. On RamanBench (77 datasets with a median of 235 spectra, including bioprocess collections) tabular foundation models lead; TabPFN v2.5 has the lowest mean improvability at 19.0%, PLS ranks 17th of 28 yet still wins six individual targets, and Raman-specific architectures take 18 wins in total ([RamanBench, NeurIPS 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02003)). On 54 NIR regression tasks TabPFN significantly outperforms PLS, but its advantage shrinks on spectral outliers and extrapolated samples, where classical chemometrics stay competitive ([Reiter et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21544)) **[preprint]**. That weak spot is where real use lives. A multi-company Raman study pooled 61 runs from four industrial sites and built generic PLS models that did well on a dilution series and then overpredicted glucose with a 64% standard error of prediction on an independent CHO culture ([Yousefi-Darani et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9332195/)). Hybrid mechanistic models supply the other benchmark: a hybrid model inside an extended Kalman filter monitored cell culture at least 35% more accurately than PLS-based industrial tools and 50% better as a titre soft sensor ([Narayanan et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32436988/)), a result from the ETH and DataHow group that sells hybrid-model software.

### Fault detection: frozen foundation models tie with one-line baselines

Monitoring is where "pretrained beats classical" is most clearly false today. A peer-reviewed study of five time-series foundation-model families finds them no different from two one-line baselines, moving-window variance and squared differences ([Zhu et al., ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010437)). On turbofan degradation data a compact autoencoder scores AUROC 0.957 against 0.731 for a foundation model's reconstruction error ([Wen and Chen 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22968)) **[preprint]**. On the Tennessee Eastman chemical process, Chronos-Bolt residuals reportedly detect 19 to 22% of faulty samples at a 1% false-alarm rate against 69% for a naive temporal difference ([Raszka and Grycz](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7634341)) **[snippet; page returned 403]**. LLMs fare no better: deep-learning detectors remain about 30% ahead of LLM-based ones ([SigLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14755)), and on robot telemetry no frontier LLM exceeds 50% on structured questions or 18% on decisions ([FactoryBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07675)) **[preprint; discrete manufacturing]**.

Two results point at what does work. With only 1% of labels, pretrained time-series encoders roughly double conventional deep models on motor fault diagnosis ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23177)) **[preprint]**. And a small latent-predictive model, HEPA, is best or tied on 10 of 14 event-prediction sets against Chronos-2, PatchTST and a masked autoencoder, although it trains a separate encoder per dataset, is far worse than a supervised specialist on remaining-useful-life error, and its Tennessee Eastman score is a saturated 1.00 ([HEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130)) **[workshop preprint]**. The language model's proven role here is explanation after the fact: with PCA detecting 11 of 15 Tennessee Eastman faults and historical root causes supplied, o1-preview placed the correct cause in its top three for 9 of 11, alongside causal claims the authors call hallucinated ([FaultExplainer](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14492v1)).

### "What if" and control: a forecaster with covariates is not a model of the plant

This is the most thesis-relevant evidence in the notes, and it cuts both ways. A single-author October 2026 preprint tests whether covariate-aware forecasters can predict the effect of a changed input. TimesFM-2.5 and TabPFN-TS are memoryless through their default covariate interfaces; **Chronos-2 identifies dynamics in context but attenuates them to 0.33 to 0.80 of the true effect, and on an oscillator its error plateaus at 0.57 with 8,192 context samples while an ARX model fitted to 256 samples reaches 0.02**. A 26-minute fine-tune on synthetic forced systems restores response sensitivity to 0.83 to 0.96 ([Won 2026](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.08118v1)) **[preprint, read via the alphaXiv abstract]**. A workshop study using Chronos-2 inside MPC for heat pumps finds the same thing from another angle: response gain stays below 1 without input excitation in the context even as forecast error falls ([Amria et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06447)). A third finds frozen models do not consistently beat a history-validated linear baseline on noiseless linear processes ([Tu et al., NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175101)) **[listing only]**. GMP batches are unexcited by design, so an off-the-shelf model will forecast a bioreactor tolerably and answer "what if I raise the feed rate" wrongly **[synthesis]**. The repair is simulator-generated, control-structured training data, not a new architecture. Whether TimesFM-3, Timestral or t0 fix the attenuation is unknown; they were not in the study.

LLMs as controllers are worse placed. On Skogestad's distillation column an LLM supervisor beat tuned linear MPC on off-nominal target acquisition (error ratio 0.361) and was about ten times worse on disturbance rejection until a rule-based gate was added ([Rosenthal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27849)) **[single-author preprint, abstract]**. On a physical two-heater rig an LLM controller reached 1.03 K error against 0.76 K for PID, with a 34-second decision cycle ([Vyas and Mercangöz 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07115v1)). Wrapped in a deterministic validator, LLM agents do recover from faults in simulation, succeeding in 60 of 60 runs on a batch mixing module with one model ([Vyas et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28011)). Reinforcement learning is not a shortcut either: across standard process-control environments RL still trails nonlinear MPC ([PC-Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22093)).

What runs on real equipment is plainer than any of this. Google's data-centre cooling uses neural predictive models with eight safety mechanisms including uncertainty gating ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/safety-first-ai-for-autonomous-data-centre-cooling-and-industrial-control/)); a tokamak policy was trained entirely in a physics simulator ([Degrave et al., Nature 2022](https://www.doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04301-9)); Imubit reports more than 100 per-plant closed-loop controllers in refining and chemicals ([Imubit](https://imubit.com/)) **[vendor]**. In pharma the regulator-accepted model with published detail is a low-order residence-time model (plug flow plus two stirred tanks per blender): Merck's continuous tablet line predicts potency with **1.4% error against a 6% acceptance criterion** and converts that error directly into rejection limits ([Hurley et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8874656)). There is no accuracy headroom for a foundation model there. FDA researchers who trained a neural predictive controller on a digital twin of such a line note that standards for validating neural-network models are lacking ([FDA Science Forum 2023](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/fda-science-forum/development-artificial-intelligence-based-predictive-controller-continuous-pharmaceutical)). No learned world model, RL policy or LLM was found running a GMP line or a commercial mammalian bioreactor; that absence reflects a handful of searches, not an exhaustive review.

### New products, scales and sites: hybrids need tens of runs, and transfer is the open prize

Hybrid models (mass balances with a learned term for unknown rates) are the incumbent a vertical model has to beat, and they are data-frugal. Published training sets are tens of runs, 18 to 38 in the real-data studies found ([Bayer et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2021.740215/full); [Peng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26520)). Intensified DoE with a hybrid model described an E. coli process with one third of the full-factorial experiments ([Bayer et al. 2020](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/biot.202000121)) **[abstract-level]**. A hybrid chromatography model cut error two to three times against the standard lumped kinetic model ([Narayanan et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34087519/)), though the same group's later review calls that result "around 40% better", a discrepancy the notes could not resolve ([Narayanan et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/chemical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fceng.2023.1157889/full)). Most of these head-to-heads come from one commercially interested group and several use simulated data; an independent benchmark on real multi-product data does not appear to exist.

The nearest thing to a bioprocess foundation model in the literature is small-scale transfer. Representing product identity as a learned three-dimensional embedding feeding a Gaussian-process rate term, **four runs of a new product with transfer match eight to twelve without**, on emulator data, and the advantage disappears for titre once 16 local runs exist ([Hutter et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13863)). That bounds the prize: the marginal value of pretraining, as measured so far, is a halving of early experiments, not an order of magnitude **[synthesis]**. Scale is harder. A hybrid trained on shake flasks roughly doubles its error at 15 litres ([Bayer et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2021.740215/full)), and a 2026 review says negative transfer "is typically identified only after the modeling effort has been completed" and that the field lacks standard benchmark datasets ([Barón Díaz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.70186)).

Flexible physics-informed learning carries documented hazards. A systems-biology study of universal differential equations needed 10,000 optimisation restarts, found the neural term non-identifiable, and saw information criteria favour the 9-parameter ODE over the 153-parameter hybrid ([Philipps et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12398592/)). Physics priors help by more than an order of magnitude when data are imperfect and hurt when over-weighted on wrong physics ([Nai et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07184)) **[preprint, synthetic data]**. And 79% of ML-for-PDE papers claiming to beat a numerical method compared against a weak baseline ([McGreivy and Hakim 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218)). The lesson for architecture is "hard structure, learned residual, differentiable solver", and for benchmarking it is to include a tuned mechanistic model, PLS, a Gaussian process and a copy-the-context baseline before claiming anything **[synthesis]**.

General LLMs have a real role here as proposers of structure. LLM-SR has the LLM write equation skeletons while a numerical optimiser fits parameters; on E. coli growth it reaches normalised error of 0.0026 in domain and 0.0037 out of domain against 0.0376 and 1.0141 for PySR. Remove the optimiser and let the LLM write the numbers, and error on an oscillator problem rises from 2.12e-7 to 3.78e-1 ([LLM-SR, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18400v3)). On a benchmark built to defeat memorisation the best system reaches only 31.5% symbolic accuracy ([LLM-SRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10415)), which matters because an LLM will write Monod kinetics from recall and look competent. No study tests an LLM-built hybrid against an expert-built one on real bioprocess data.

### Typed decisions and determinism are engineering constraints with known fixes

A validated environment needs repeatable outputs, uncertainty that means something, and an audit trail. Hosted LLMs fail the first by default: at nominally deterministic settings accuracy varies by up to 15% across runs ([Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667)) **[preprint]**, and one 120B open model reproduced its own output 12.5% of the time at temperature zero while two 7B to 8B models reached 100% ([Khatchadourian and Franco 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07585)). Verbalised confidence is overconfident, with failure-prediction AUROC of 0.52 to 0.61 ([Xiong et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)). These have mitigations: pinned small models, logit scoring, temperature scaling. But calibration does not travel. Training with a Brier-score reward brings calibration error to 0.03 in domain, from 0.53 for the base model, and it rises to 0.21 out of domain ([Damani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)) **[preprint]**. No study measures the calibration of any model's numeric intervals on process data, so the claim that a vertical model is better calibrated than a general one is untested **[synthesis]**.

## Six candidate architectures, each with a result that cuts against it

No family below has a pharma-manufacturing proof point. The table gives the strongest evidence each way; the last column is **[synthesis]**.

Three observations organise that table. The strongest sub-claim in the thesis, that dense numeric signals should not be word-piece tokens, is supported from independent directions and does not depend on any one architecture. The JEPA material deserves a narrow reading: LeCun's argument that predicting in representation space lets a model ignore unpredictable detail has real support on noisy physical signals, while his error-compounding argument against autoregression is the best-rebutted part of the position and applies equally to JEPA's own multi-step latent rollouts, as its authors concede ([V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1); [Xing et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05169v3)). Batch data is exactly the low-diversity, slow-feature regime where latent prediction is documented to misbehave, so a naive "JEPA for batches" risks learning which reactor and which probe produced the data, not the biology **[synthesis]**. Finally, the winning pattern in adjacent physical domains is a hybrid: Waymo keeps a language model for rare-scene semantics next to a fast sensor encoder and an onboard validation layer ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2025/12/demonstrably-safe-ai-for-autonomous-driving)) **[vendor]**, and robotics moved from tokenised actions to continuous heads while keeping the language backbone.

## What the YC request actually asks for, and how pharma scores against it

The request is narrower than "LLMs are not enough". Diana Hu's entry says Jev "hit a nerve" by giving developers "a typed decision and a probability instead of generated text" with no classifier per task, asks for labs building specialised models for manufacturing, energy and biology that work zero-shot or few-shot, sets the bar at "matching or beating general-purpose models at orders of magnitude less inference compute", cites TimesFM-3, and states that "the hard part is getting high-quality, vertical-specific, verifiable rewards", alongside data rights and a clear win over existing specialist models ([YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)). A sibling entry in the same list expects companies to keep renting frontier models for their hardest problems while owning specialised ones.

**Jev is an interface precedent, not a representation precedent, and not JEPA.** It is TypeSafe AI's hosted "System One" decision model, launched 15 September 2026: it takes a text or JSON state plus typed questions and returns a choice, score or yes-no probability in one pass, with architecture, size and training data undisclosed ([TypeSafe](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)) **[vendor]**. The vendor's own evaluation has it matching GPT-5.6 Terra (67.8% vs 67.9%) at $0.0004 against $0.0304 per case, with ground truth taken from the consensus of two frontier LLMs instead of human labels ([TypeSafe evals](https://evals.typesafe.ai)) **[vendor]**. Independent tests are cooler: a tie with Claude Sonnet 5 at 91.7% on 60 hand-labelled cases at about 3.3 times lower latency and 40.6 times lower cost ([jev-benchmark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/themsquared/jev-benchmark/main/README.md)); 83% on Banking77 against 93% for a small supervised embedding classifier; and 45% correct at 74% mean stated probability on an out-of-distribution test ([Sophos](https://www.sophos.com/blog/jevs-paradox-hidden-cost-of-cheap-ai-decisions)). A preprint finds it violates the conjunction rule in 54% of responses ([arXiv 2610.19619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19619)). The vendor documents unreliable counting, arithmetic and date comparison, the context is 32,000 tokens, and no on-premises option is described ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)). Jev supports the "control over outputs" leg of the thesis and gives no support to "native numeric representations" **[synthesis]**. Stock open LLMs read out through logits reach similar calibration ([Dibia](https://newsletter.victordibia.com/p/how-jev-works-calibrated-decision)), so the interface is reproducible without a new model.

**TimesFM-3 is the representation precedent, and it is a hyperscaler's.** Google released it on 31 August 2026 as a 330M-parameter decoder pretrained on more than a trillion time points, with native multivariate and covariate support and nine quantiles in one forward pass; it claims the top average rank on three benchmarks and publishes no numeric scores or inference-cost figures ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/timesfm-3-a-zero-shot-foundation-model-for-multivariate-forecasting/)) **[vendor]**. Mistral's October 2026 snapshot puts its own Timestral and t0 ahead ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/)) **[vendor]**. The exemplar is free, generic and matched by Amazon and Mistral, which is the opposite of a moat, and its weights cannot go into production under their licence.

**"Orders of magnitude less compute" holds for one to two orders when measured independently.** A task-specific classifier emitted about 8 and 15 times less CO2 per query than two generative models on the same sentiment task ([Luccioni et al., FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16863v3)); independent Jev measurements land at roughly 10 to 40 times cheaper and 3 to 7 times faster than a single LLM call, against vendor claims of 444.6 and 193.6 times ([jev-benchmark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/themsquared/jev-benchmark/main/README.md); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)). Small is not automatically cheap: TabPFN takes about 0.2 s per prediction against 0.0002 s for CatBoost ([Hollmann et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711098/)). In a plant making a few hundred batches a year the compute argument is really about where the model can run: a model of a few hundred million parameters fits on one validated on-premises server, which a frontier LLM does not **[synthesis]**.

**Verifiable rewards are where pharma is both strongest and poorest.** The draft Annex 22 effectively defines a verifiable label: test-data labels verified by "independent verification by multiple experts, validated equipment or laboratory tests", with generation of test data or labels by generative AI "not recommended" ([Annex 22 draft](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)) **[draft]**. Validated assays and QA dispositions are trustworthy labels. They are also scarce and nearly constant: the tablet dataset's authors expect a process capability above 1 for drug release, meaning failures are rare ([Žagar and Mihelič](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01203-x)). The abundant, automatically verifiable signals are the future sensor trajectory, the paired soft-sensor and lab result, and simulator ground truth. The decision-relevant signals (disposition, confirmed root cause, out-of-specification outcome) are sparse or judgemental **[synthesis; the researchers' per-reward frequency and delay figures are their own estimates and are not reproduced here]**. ether0 shows both the payoff and the trap: verifiable chemistry rewards let a 24B general LLM beat frontier models and a purpose-built specialist with a tenth of the data, yet several of its "verifiers" were other models' predictions ([ether0](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17238v2)). The pharma equivalent of that trap is rewarding agreement with a soft sensor instead of the validated assay. No published work trains a model with a physically measured pharma-manufacturing outcome as the reward.

**Data rights are the requirement most likely to sink an entrant.** The only large proof that competing pharma companies will co-train a model is MELLODDY: ten companies, three years, €18.6M, and gains above 4% in AUC-PR for most partners on drug-discovery data with a shared molecular input space ([Heyndrickx et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005050/); [IHI factsheet](https://www.ihi.europa.eu/projects-results/project-factsheets/melloddy)). Manufacturing sites share no such input space. Synthetic pretraining sidesteps rights for the pretraining stage only; TabPFN saw no real tables at all.

**The stated bar is "existing specialist models", and regulation does not lower it.** A win over GPT alone does not meet the RFS. On regulation, the draft Annex 22 covers only static, deterministic models for critical GMP applications, says generative AI and LLMs "should not be used in critical GMP applications", and asks for logged confidence scores with an "undecided" outcome and recorded feature attribution ([Annex 22 draft](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)) **[draft]**. That reads like a specification for a typed, deterministic, attributable model. It is not law. The consultation closed on 7 October 2025; as fetched on 19 November 2026 the EudraLex Volume 4 page lists Annexes 1 to 21 only ([EudraLex Volume 4](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/eudralex/eudralex-volume-4_en)); and EMA's workshop report published 2 October 2026 records that the drafting group "has therefore discussed the scope of Annex 22 to allow dynamic or adaptive models, probabilistic models, and Generative AI/LLMs in GMP applications" under a documented risk-based control strategy, with a session consensus that the annex "should remain technology-neutral" ([EMA workshop report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)). That report is expert input, not a decision, and a Q4 2026 delivery target comes only from secondary sources. FDA has no binding rule; its January 2025 credibility guidance was still listed as draft on a page current to 1 May 2026 and scales the evidence burden with model influence ([FDA draft guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/184830/download)). What looks stable across regulators is architecture-neutral: defined intended use, pre-set acceptance criteria (the draft annex's wording is "at least as high as the performance of the process it replaces"), independent test data, change control, and supplier evidence access, since "if a third-party model or API cannot provide necessary outputs, rationale, logs or evidence needed for control, the manufacturer cannot measure and justify the system adequately" ([EMA workshop report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)). That favours models whose weights and versions the manufacturer controls, vertical or open-weight, over a rented API.

## The moat that survives is data, simulators and validation evidence

**Four versions of the thesis do not survive.** An architecture moat does not: parameter counts of the leading numeric models are mostly 5M to 330M, LeWorldModel trains in hours on one GPU, and inside specialist domains architectures are becoming more generic, with AlphaFold 3 dropping equivariance ([Abramson et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924)). A vertical language model does not, on the Harvey, medicine and finance record. "Regulators ban LLMs" does not, because the ban is a draft under reconsideration; as one practitioner put it, whether or not the exclusion survives, "both roads end in the same place" of human accountability ([PharmTech](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/europe-tried-to-ban-generative-ai-from-critical-gmp-the-ban-may-not-survive-it-does-not-matter-)). And a cross-company foundation model in the AlphaFold or GraphCast sense is not buildable by a startup from pooled real data today: every such win sat on a large shared corpus or a trusted simulator plus a public scorecard, and confirmed open pharma batch data amounts to one real tablet dataset and one 100-batch simulated fermentation dataset. The cautionary precedent is single-cell biology, where foundation models pretrained on large observational corpora fail to beat linear baselines on perturbation prediction ([Ahlmann-Eltze et al., Nature Methods 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12328236/)). Historical GMP batches are also observational and low-variance.

**The version that survives treats the model as the product of the moat.** Investor essays are close to unanimous that "AI is an extraordinary engine for product differentiation. But it's not a moat", placing defensibility in workflow ownership, system-of-record position and trust ([a16z](https://a16z.com/context-is-king/); [NFX](https://nfx.com/post/ai-defensibility)), with messy proprietary data as the one model-adjacent asset that lasts ([Insight Partners](https://insightpartners.com/ideas/building-a-moat-in-the-age-of-ai)). The heavily funded physical-AI companies fit that reading because they own the data-generating asset: robots, automated labs, or in Epic's case a records network across 310 health systems that made a 118-million-patient event model possible ([CoMET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12104)) **[preprint, vendor]**. In pharma that asset belongs to the manufacturers. A startup's durable position therefore consists of four things **[synthesis]**: contractual rights to learn from customer process data, negotiated customer by customer; a library of calibrated mechanistic and hybrid simulators that serve as both pretraining prior and reward environment; locked, expert- or lab-verified test sets per product and site, which draft rules would require anyway; and validated integration with existing systems of record. The specialist model is how those assets are expressed, and it should be built so the language layer can be swapped as general models improve. "Validation as a barrier to entry" is plausible and unevidenced: no source quantifies it.

The market looks open and unproven. Incumbent bioprocess vendors sell hybrid models plus LLM agents, and none of those checked describes a foundation model on its homepage; the one company claiming "foundation models for biomanufacturing", AbInitio Bio, says its first model beats standard hybrid models and gives no data, architecture or benchmark ([YC launch post](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/QS3-abinitio-bio-frontier-foundation-models-for-biomanufacturing)) **[vendor, unverified]**; the best-traction entrant found, Katalyze AI, is an integration and agent product on general models ([ERP Today](https://erp.today/katalyze-ai-pharma-manufacturing-agentic-ai-funding/)) **[press]**. Practitioners locate the bottleneck in data readiness more than model class: DataHow's CEO says of siloed, inconsistent process data that "no model can compensate for that" ([AIChE ChEnected](https://chenected.aiche.org/2026/04/data-driven-bioprocessing-conversation-datahows-alessandro-butte)) **[sponsored]**. A product that solves ingestion and provenance first becomes the aggregation point from which a data moat is possible; that sequencing also matches this project's own governing principles of preserving systems of record and provenance, keeping models decoupled from connectors and interface, and not presenting confidence numbers as probabilities unless they are calibrated and validated.

A public benchmark with tuned baselines would itself be a contribution, and a precondition for any credible "beats general models" claim.

## Ten experiments, starting with the head-to-head nobody has published

These are proposals **[synthesis]** assembled from protocols in the cited papers. Shared rules: split by held-out conditions (new product, scale, instrument, outside the DoE box), never by random time points; include a tuned mechanistic or hybrid model, PLS, a Gaussian process, tuned linear regression and a copy-the-context baseline every time; use post-cutoff, private or freshly simulated data for any LLM arm, because LLMs have memorised popular public tables ([Bordt et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06209)); and log cost, latency and run-to-run repeatability alongside accuracy.

## What was not verified, and where the evidence is thin

**Process limits.** The researchers' shared web-search budget ran out part-way through every workstream. After that only direct fetches of known URLs were possible, so coverage of named-company deployments, industry consortia, the classic multivariate SPC literature, reviewer criticism on OpenReview and 2026 leaderboards is thinner than intended. Numbers marked "full text" in the notes came from an automated reader of arXiv HTML and need a spot-check against the PDFs. Many numeracy and control benchmarks used 2024-era models, which overstates the deficit of current frontier models; ControlBench and most tabular head-to-heads have not been re-run.

**Evidence that does not exist publicly, on either side.** No benchmark compares frontier LLMs with tools against specialist numeric models and classical or hybrid baselines on bioprocess forecasting, soft sensing, anomaly detection or optimisation. No foundation-model study on mammalian cell culture, perfusion, chromatography or GMP batch data was found, and no pretrained model for chromatography traces. No study measures calibration of numeric prediction intervals on process data. No hybrid model was found in a filed biologics control strategy or release decision. No federated or consortium effort on manufacturing process data, no JEPA or latent world model on bioprocess or spectroscopy data, no reward-based training on a physically measured manufacturing outcome, and no quantification of validation effort as a barrier to entry were found. No source gives typical batches per product per year or historian tag counts; statements that manufacturers hold "tens to low hundreds" of batches per product and site are the researchers' domain judgement. Absence here reflects truncated searches and is weak evidence of absence.

**Regulatory status.** All Annex 22 quotations are from the July 2025 consultation draft. Whether the drafting group delivered final text to the Commission in Q4 2026 could not be confirmed, and that target comes from secondary sources. ## Conclusion

The framing "general LLM versus vertical foundation model" turns out to be the wrong axis. The evidence sorts along a different one: whether the information needed for a decision is already in the record as language, is in the record as numbers that need a native encoding, or is not in the record at all. Language models win the first, small numeric models and mechanistic structure win the second, and the third, which contains the plant's response to an untried input and most of what makes a new product hard, is supplied only by designed experiments and calibrated simulators. That last category is the binding scarcity. More observational batch data does not create it, a new architecture does not create it, and the single-cell and input-response results show what happens when a model is pretrained without it. A company that owns calibrated simulators, a designed-experiment loop and locked, lab-verified test sets owns the reward signal YC names as the hard part.

Two practical consequences follow. The honest pitch is not "LLMs are not enough" but "an owned, small, calibrated process model that saves a measurable number of runs per new product against PLS and hybrid baselines, at a size that runs inside the customer's validated environment". And there is a cheap test of whether a model moat exists at all: if a frontier agent that is allowed to call open specialists matches the bespoke model on the first experiment above, the model is a commodity and the value sits in data access, integration and evidence; if it does not, the gap is the first benchmark result in a field that has none. Three developments would change this verdict: a published cross-site, cross-product transfer result on real batches, the final scope of Annex 22, and evidence that the newest covariate-aware forecasters pass a paired-counterfactual test without domain fine-tuning.
