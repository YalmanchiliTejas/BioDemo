## 1. Hybrid (mechanistic + ML) bioprocess models: head-to-head results, data requirements, and transfer across products/scales/cell lines

### Takeaway
Hybrid models (mass balances with an ML term for unknown rates) repeatedly beat PLS/statistical baselines by roughly 35-50% and beat simple mechanistic baselines by 2-3x in the published ETH/DataHow comparisons, and they do it with tens of runs, not thousands. The closest thing to a bioprocess foundation model that exists in the literature is small-scale knowledge transfer (cell-line embeddings, meta-learning, multi-fidelity GPs) evaluated mostly on simulated data, where 4 runs with transfer match 8-12 runs without; no peer-reviewed, cross-company pretrained bioprocess model was found.

### Inferences
- The published evidence for "structure beats generic learners" in bioprocess is consistent in direction but narrow in provenance: most head-to-head numbers come from one group (ETH/DataHow, which sells hybrid-model software) and several use in-silico data. An independent, pre-registered benchmark on real multi-product data does not appear to exist.
- The same evidence cuts against the "foundation" half of the thesis: hybrid models win precisely because 20-80 runs are enough once mass balances are imposed. The marginal value of a pretrained model is therefore the difference between about 4 and about 8-12 runs for a new product (Hutter), not an order-of-magnitude step.
- The Hutter design is a direct architectural seed for a process foundation model: fixed conservation-law skeleton, a learned rate function shared across products, and a learned low-dimensional product/cell-line/scale embedding. Scaling it means replacing the GP with an amortised (in-context or meta-learned) rate model and extending embeddings to media, clone and reactor.
- A buildable experiment: reproduce Hutter's protocol on real data (N historic products, k = 2/4/8/16 runs on a held-out product) with four arms: (a) local hybrid, (b) hybrid + product embedding, (c) a general LLM given the same runs in context, (d) a general time-series foundation model. Report error vs k and the k at which (a) catches (b). The titer result at 16 runs suggests the crossover is low.
- Scale transfer roughly doubles NRMSE even with a hybrid model (Bayer 2021), so cross-scale generalisation, not within-scale interpolation, is where a vertical model has room to demonstrate value.

### Gaps
- The exact run count and percentage improvements in Narayanan et al. 2019 could not be verified (publisher pages blocked; the abstract has no numbers). My recollection that the study used 81 fed-batch experiments was specifically not confirmed by search.
- Smiatek et al. ("Towards a digital bioprocess replica") was not found beyond a second-hand citation to "Smiatek et al., 2020"; no title, venue or result was confirmed.
- Sokolov et al. on hybrid models as an enabler of digital twins, von Stosch et al. 2016 (industrial E. coli QbD/intensified DoE), and Narayanan et al.'s "increasing degree of process knowledge" papers (IECR) were seen only as citations inside other papers; details not verified.
- The full text of the 2026 transfer-learning review could not be parsed (binary PDF), so its table of per-study run savings is not captured here.
- No peer-reviewed bioprocess foundation model (pretrained across companies/products and evaluated on held-out products) was found as of this search. Absence of evidence in a truncated search is not proof of absence.

## 2. Small-molecule and drug-product unit operations: hybrid/PINN/surrogate models, digital twins on GMP lines, and what is actually used for MPC and real-time release

### Takeaway
What runs in commercial continuous manufacturing control strategies is deliberately simple and physically transparent: residence-time-distribution models (tanks-in-series plus dead time) combined with NIR/PLS, with regulator-accepted diversion limits derived from model error. Hybrid/PINN work on crystallisation, granulation and lyophilisation shows order-of-magnitude robustness gains over pure data-driven models under model mismatch, but almost entirely on synthetic or lab data.

### Inferences
- For continuous drug-product lines the binding constraint is validation and regulatory acceptance, not predictive accuracy: a three-parameter RTD model with a 1.4% RMSEP already clears a 6% criterion. A vertical foundation model has no accuracy headroom to win on here; its opening is in harder, multi-attribute problems (dissolution, CQAs dependent on material properties, cross-formulation transfer) where the Merck paper notes it covers one formulation on one rig.
- The crystallisation result is a clean statement of the hybrid trade-off: physics priors give 10x robustness when data are imperfect, but hard-coding wrong physics hurts. Any process foundation model should treat mechanistic terms as soft, weightable priors with learnable corrections, not hard constraints.
- Differentiable solvers (JAX PBE, PyTorch DG chromatography) are the practical route to hybrid models now; they are also the cheapest synthetic-data generators for pretraining.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed description was found of the models inside Vertex (Orkambi/Symdeko/Trikafta), Janssen (Prezista) or Pfizer (PCMM) commercial control strategies; only slides and vendor case studies. Whether any approved line uses MPC rather than PID plus RTD-based diversion could not be established.
- Mascia et al. 2013 (end-to-end plant), Lakerveld's plant-wide control work and Mesbah et al. 2017 (MPC of the integrated pilot plant, Org. Process Res. Dev.) were seen only as titles in reference lists or not at all; not verified.
- ICH Q13's exact wording on model impact categories and validation could not be extracted (PDF not machine-readable in this session).
- No hybrid/PINN studies were found for tableting/compaction or powder blending with quantitative head-to-heads, and no flow-chemistry reactor hybrid/neural-ODE study was verified.
- A 2025 AIChE Journal highlight on hybrid AI-physics crystal size prediction and a 2026 IJP review on AI-enabled lyophilisation were seen as titles only. — pointers: [AIChE CEP highlight](https://www.aiche.org/resources/publications/cep/2025/october/aiche-journal-highlight-hybrid-ai-physics-models-accelerate-crystal-property-prediction-fast-drug); [Strathclyde preprint](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/97141/7/Liu-etal-IJP-2026-AI-enabled-lyophilization-of-advanced-biologics.pdf)

## 3. PINNs, neural ODEs, universal differential equations, SINDy/symbolic regression and GP models on process systems: best results and failure modes

### Takeaway
Embedding known structure and learning only the residual works; asking a network to satisfy stiff physics through a soft loss frequently does not. The documented pathologies (stiffness-driven gradient imbalance, ill-conditioned loss landscapes, non-identifiable neural terms, multi-modal objectives needing thousands of restarts) are directly relevant to bioprocess kinetics, and the ML-for-PDE literature overstates gains because of weak baselines.

### Cited Findings
- **[UNDERMINES naive physics-informed learning]** "Characterizing possible failure modes in physics-informed neural networks", Krishnapriyan, Gholami, Zhe, Kirby, Mahoney, NeurIPS 2021. Setup: convection, reaction and reaction-diffusion problems. Result: vanilla PINNs fail beyond trivial regimes because the soft PDE loss makes the landscape hard to optimise, not for lack of capacity; curriculum regularisation and sequence-to-sequence training cut error by up to 1-2 orders of magnitude. — [arXiv 2109.01050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01050)
- **[UNDERMINES naive PINNs]** "Understanding and mitigating gradient pathologies in physics-informed neural networks", Wang, Teng, Perdikaris, 2020. Result: numerical stiffness causes unbalanced back-propagated gradients; learning-rate annealing plus a modified architecture improves accuracy by 50-100x. — [arXiv 2001.04536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04536)
- **[UNDERMINES for kinetics]** "Stiff-PINN: Physics-Informed Neural Network for Stiff Chemical Kinetics", Ji, Qiu, Shi, Pan, Deng, 2020/2021 (published DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.1c05102). Result: standard PINNs fail on stiff kinetic ODE systems; they work only after quasi-steady-state assumptions remove the stiffness. — [arXiv 2011.04520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04520)
- **[MIXED]** "Stiff neural ordinary differential equations", Kim, Ji, Deng, Ma, Rackauckas, 2021, Chaos 31(9). Setup: ROBER and POLLU stiff benchmarks. Result: learning stiff neural ODEs is possible but needs specific techniques (output and loss scaling, suitable activations, stabilised gradient calculation), because the adjoint method becomes unstable. — [arXiv 2103.15341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15341)
- **[SUPPORTS as architecture]** "Universal Differential Equations for Scientific Machine Learning", Rackauckas, Ma, Martensen, Warner, Zubov, Supekar, Skinner, Ramadhan, Edelman, 2020. The UDE framework (known terms plus neural terms inside a differentiable solver) with training methods claimed stable on stiff equations. No headline numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2001.04385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04385)
- **[SUPPORTS, lab scale]** "Physics-informed neural networks for hybrid modeling of lab-scale batch fermentation for β-carotene production using Saccharomyces cerevisiae", Bangi, Kao, Kwon, 2022, Chemical Engineering Research and Design 179:415-423. UDE hybrid where a neural ODE learns unknown rates alongside kinetic equations; no numbers in the abstract. — [DOI 10.1016/j.cherd.2022.01.041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cherd.2022.01.041)
- **[UNDERMINES / cautionary]** "Current state and open problems in universal differential equations for systems biology", Philipps, Schmid, Hasenauer, 2025, npj Systems Biology and Applications 11:101. Setup: glycolysis oscillator and STAT5 signalling UDEs; 20 settings spanning 8-61 data points per observable and 5-35% noise. Results: single-start optimisation often failed even on the observed species; 10,000 multi-starts were used and only a fraction reached the test-loss threshold; successful fits were over 4x more likely with weight decay 1 < λ < 10 than with none; training loss did not predict test performance; the neural term was not identifiable and symbolic regression recovered 3.96e-4·x + 0.8 instead of the true 6.17e-2·x; some UDEs produced negative concentrations; AIC/BIC favoured the 9-parameter ODE (294/311) over the 153-parameter UDE (480/766). — [PMC12398592](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12398592/)
- **[MIXED]** Symbolic discovery of bioprocess kinetics: a KAN-guided symbolic regression benchmark on an in-silico batch fermentation recovered kinetics structurally similar to the reference but missed the biomass-dependent inhibition term. — [arXiv 2509.10089](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10089)
- **[MIXED]** SINDy vs symbolic regression for kinetic rate laws: a 2026 PSE paper frames SINDy as limited to a predefined candidate library while genetic-programming symbolic regression searches a wider space, with AICc for selection. — [LAPSE 2026.0309](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2606/LAPSE-2026.0309-1v1.pdf); also "Machine learning uncovers analytical kinetic models of bioprocesses" (2024, Chemical Engineering Science) — [repository record](https://www.recercat.cat/handle/20.500.14342/4620)
- **[MIXED]** A 2026 multi-level soft sensor (Boskabadi et al., Systems & Control Transactions) compares a baseline EKF, a bias-augmented EKF and a hybrid EKF with physics-constrained learned residuals driven by sparse assays, on a lovastatin benchmark. — [DTU record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/a-multi-level-hybrid-ekfmachine-learning-soft-sensor-for-robust-b/)
- **[UNDERMINES the reliability of the whole literature]** "Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related partial differential equations", McGreivy and Hakim, 2024, Nature Machine Intelligence. Result: of ML-for-PDE articles claiming to beat a standard numerical method, 79% (60/76) compared against a weak baseline; outcome-reporting and publication bias are widespread. — [arXiv 2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218); [DOE PAGES record](https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2475825-weak-baselines-reporting-biases-lead-overoptimism-machine-learning-fluid-related-partial-differential-equations)

### Inferences
- The robust pattern across sections 1-3 is "hard structure, learned residual, differentiable solver" (hybrid ODE/UDE, solver-in-the-loop). The fragile pattern is "soft physics loss on a free-form network" (vanilla PINN). A process foundation model should inherit the former.
- Bioprocess kinetics are stiff, sparsely sampled (daily offline assays) and noisy, which is exactly the regime where the UDE study shows non-identifiability and multi-modality. Reported hybrid successes probably depend on strong restrictions (small networks, serial structure, intensive DoE), which is information a founder should carry into architecture choices.
- Given the 79% weak-baseline finding, any internal benchmark should include a tuned mechanistic model, PLS, a GP, and a simple copy/persistence baseline before any claim of superiority.

### Gaps
- Gaussian-process state-space models and GP-based MPC for fed-batch processes (e.g., work associated with del Rio-Chanona, Bradford, Zhang) could not be searched before the budget ran out; only GP-as-kinetic-term hybrids (Hutter, Rogers) are covered above.
- No verified head-to-head of SINDy vs neural hybrid vs mechanistic on real bioprocess data was found.
- The original neural ODE paper (Chen et al., 2018) was not separately verified in this session.

## 4. Neural operators and PDE/physics/dynamical-system foundation models: scale, what transfers zero-shot, and relevance to chemical engineering

### Takeaway
Physics foundation models now exist at 1-4.5B parameters trained on terabytes of simulation, and pretraining gives real sample-efficiency gains on related PDEs (Poseidon: about 20 samples to match an FNO trained on 1,024). But independent 2026 evaluations describe them as "conditional rather than universal generalists", none of the models found targets reactors or bioreactors, and for low-dimensional dynamical systems a 10k-parameter structured model or a copy-the-context baseline beats large time-series foundation models.

### Cited Findings

**Operator learning foundations**
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations", Li, Kovachki, Azizzadenesheli, Liu, Bhattacharya, Stuart, Anandkumar, 2020. Up to three orders of magnitude faster than traditional solvers; zero-shot super-resolution on turbulent flow. — [arXiv 2010.08895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "DeepONet: Learning nonlinear operators for identifying differential equations based on the universal approximation theorem of operators", Lu, Jin, Karniadakis, 2019 (published DOI 10.1038/s42256-021-00302-5). Branch/trunk architecture; much better generalisation than fully connected nets on small datasets. — [arXiv 1910.03193](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03193)

**PDE foundation models**
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Multiple Physics Pretraining for Spatiotemporal Surrogate Models" (MPP), McCabe et al. (Polymathic AI), NeurIPS 2024. One transformer pretrained autoregressively on several heterogeneous fluid systems with shared embedding/normalisation matches or beats task-specific baselines without fine-tuning, and when fine-tuned beats training from scratch and video-foundation-model initialisation on unseen physics. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2310.02994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02994); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/d7cb9db5ade2db7814fbd01ee59f4c7b-Abstract.html)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "DPOT: Auto-Regressive Denoising Operator Transformer for Large-Scale PDE Pre-Training", Hao et al., ICML 2024. Fourier-attention transformer, up to 1B parameters (0.5B in earlier versions), 10+ PDE datasets, over 100k trajectories. — [arXiv 2403.03542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03542v3); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hao24d.html)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Poseidon: Efficient Foundation Models for PDEs", Herde et al. (ETH), NeurIPS 2024. Multiscale operator transformer with time-conditioned layer norms, pretrained on fluid-dynamics equations only; 15 downstream tasks, 9 with PDEs unseen in pretraining; on average needs about 20 samples to match the error of an FNO trained on 1,024. Authors' own evaluation. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/84e1b1ec17bb11c57234e96433022a9a-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2405.19101](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19101v2)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "PDEformer: Towards a Foundation Model for One-Dimensional Partial Differential Equations", Ye, Huang, Chen, Liu, Wang, Dong, 2024. The PDE is given to the model as a computational graph (symbolic + numeric), with a graph transformer and implicit neural representation; zero-shot accuracy reported as comparable to specialist models. No numbers in the abstract. — [arXiv 2402.12652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12652)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Unisolver: PDE-Conditional Transformers Towards Universal Neural PDE Solvers", Zhou, Ma, Wu, Wang, Long, ICML 2025. Transformer conditioned on a complete set of PDE components (equation symbols, coefficients, boundary conditions) as domain-wise and point-wise conditions; state of the art on three large benchmarks. — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25r.html); [arXiv 2405.17527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17527)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "In-Context Operator Learning with Data Prompts for Differential Equation Problems" (ICON), Yang, Liu, Meng, Osher, PNAS 2023. One network learns operators from a few prompted input-output examples with no weight update, across forward/inverse ODE, PDE and mean-field control problems, and generalises to operators beyond the training distribution. — [arXiv 2304.07993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07993)
- **[SUPPORTS on data infrastructure]** "The Well: a Large-Scale Collection of Diverse Physics Simulations for Machine Learning", Ohana et al. (Polymathic AI), NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks. 15 TB, 16 datasets (biological systems, fluid dynamics, acoustic scattering, MHD, supernovae), 6.9 GB to 5.1 TB each, uniform grids. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4f9a5acd91ac76569f2fe291b1f4772b-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2412.00568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00568v2)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Walrus: A Cross-Domain Foundation Model for Continuum Dynamics", McCabe, Mukhopadhyay, Marwah et al. (Polymathic AI), arXiv Nov 2025, ICML 2026 spotlight. 1.3B-parameter transformer trained on The Well plus FlowBench: 19 scenarios, 63 state variables, 2D and 3D, spanning astrophysics, geoscience, rheology, plasma physics, acoustics and classical fluids; outperforms prior foundation models on short and long horizons. Chemical engineering, reactors and bioreactors are not mentioned in the abstract. — [arXiv 2511.15684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15684); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/spotlight/84667); [model card](https://huggingface.co/polymathic-ai/walrus/blob/main/README.md)
- **[MIXED: native representation, but tokenised and bootstrapped from video]** "PhysiX: A Foundation Model for Physics Simulations", Nguyen, Koneru, Li, Grover (UCLA), NeurIPS 2025. 4.5B-parameter autoregressive transformer over a universal discrete tokenizer plus a refinement module; outperforms baselines on The Well; initialised from video-generation checkpoints because the largest physics datasets have only tens of thousands of samples. — [arXiv 2506.17774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17774v1)
- **[MIXED]** "Towards a Physics Foundation Model" (GPhyT), Wiesner, Wessling, Baek, arXiv Sept 2025. Trained on 1.8 TB of simulation data with in-context learning. The headline claim changed across versions: v1 reported up to 29x over specialised architectures and zero-shot generalisation; later versions report more than 7x over multi-physics architectures and "plausible" zero-shot generalisation. — [v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13805v1); [later version](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13805v4)

**Independent evaluations and limits**
- **[UNDERMINES]** "Do Physics Foundation Models Learn Generalizable Physics? A Bias-Aware Benchmark Across Physical Regimes and Distribution Shifts", Chu, Liu, Biswas, Shen, May 2026 (rev. July 2026). 8 dynamics, 3 training mixtures, 25 test regimes, 60,000 measurements, 5 architectures each trained from scratch and pretrained at three sizes. Conclusion: these models are "conditional rather than universal generalists"; better training distributions only partly help, and pretraining and scale do not reliably remove regime-specific biases. — [arXiv 2605.29283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283)
- **[MIXED]** "Out-of-distribution transfer of PDE foundation models to material dynamics under extreme loading", Rautela et al. (LANL), March 2026. Poseidon and MORPH fine-tuned vs trained from scratch on shock/interface and fracture problems at several training-set sizes; the abstract does not state which won. — [arXiv 2603.04354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04354)
- **[MIXED]** "Breakeven complexity: A new perspective on neural partial differential equation solvers", Zhang, Roberts, Marwah, Khodak, 2026. Counts how many solves a learned solver needs before data-generation and training costs are recovered against a classical solver at matched error; neural solvers pay off only as problems get harder (higher dimension, longer rollouts, higher Reynolds number). — [arXiv 2605.15399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15399)
- **[SUPPORTS]** A NASA preprint (Feb 2026) reports that fine-tuning Poseidon gives skilful Martian-atmosphere emulators, i.e. transfer to a domain far from pretraining. — [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260001452/downloads/mars_pdefm_arxiv_v1.pdf)

**Zero-shot foundation models for (low-dimensional) dynamical systems**
- **[SUPPORTS: structured tiny model beats generic TS foundation models]** "True Zero-Shot Inference of Dynamical Systems Preserving Long-Term Statistics" (DynaMix), Hemmer and Durstewitz, NeurIPS 2025. Mixture-of-experts of almost-linear RNNs, about 10k parameters (about 0.1% of Chronos), pretrained on 34 systems; zero-shot reconstruction of out-of-domain systems from a short context, beating time-series foundation models on long-term statistics and often on short-term forecasts, including real traffic and weather series. — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1419d8554191a65ea4f2d8e1057973e4-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2505.13192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13192v2)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Panda: A pretrained forecast model for chaotic dynamics", Lai, Bao, Gilpin, ICLR 2026. PatchTST-style model with channel attention trained purely on a synthetic dataset of 20,000 chaotic dynamical systems found by an evolutionary algorithm; zero-shot it outperforms Chronos, TimeMoE and TimesFM even when those are fine-tuned on Panda's data. A later paper reports its long-horizon predictions can drift toward fixed points. — [arXiv 2505.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13755); [limitation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04024)
- **[MIXED]** "Zero-shot forecasting of chaotic systems", Zhang and Gilpin, ICLR 2025. 135 chaotic systems, 10^8 timepoints: generic time-series foundation models are competitive with custom-trained NBEATS/TiDE when data are limited. — [arXiv 2409.15771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15771)
- **[UNDERMINES generic sequence foundation models]** "Context parroting: A simple but tough-to-beat baseline for foundation models in scientific machine learning", Zhang and Gilpin, ICLR 2026. Time-series foundation models largely forecast by copying context; a naive copy-from-context baseline beats Chronos, TimesFM and TimeMoE on low-dimensional chaos, turbulence, coupled oscillators and ECG at a fraction of the cost; the behaviour is linked to induction heads in LLMs. — [arXiv 2505.11349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11349)

### Inferences
- Three conditioning strategies are available as templates for a process model: (i) explicit equation/graph conditioning (PDEformer, Unisolver) maps naturally onto "known stoichiometry + mass balances + unit-operation topology"; (ii) in-context operator learning (ICON, GPhyT, DynaMix) maps onto "a few runs of a new product as the prompt"; (iii) entity embeddings (Hutter) for product/clone/scale. A process foundation model could combine (i) and (ii).
- Bioprocess unit operations are mostly low-dimensional ODE/DAE systems with exogenous inputs (feeds, setpoints), which is the DynaMix/Panda regime, not the PDE regime. Those results suggest small, dynamics-structured models pretrained on tens to thousands of synthetic systems can beat billion-parameter generic sequence models zero-shot. This is the most direct architectural support for the thesis found.
- None of DynaMix, Panda or the PDE models, as described in their abstracts, handle controlled systems (exogenous inputs, discrete feeding events, irregular sparse sampling). That gap is where a vertical model would have to add something new.
- The PDE-foundation-model evidence does not transfer automatically: all pretraining corpora found are fluid/continuum simulations; no reactor, crystalliser, chromatography or bioreactor-CFD data are included in any corpus identified.

### Gaps
- No neural-operator (FNO/DeepONet) or PDE-foundation-model study on bioreactor CFD, kLa/oxygen transfer or scale-up was found; the only stirred-tank result is a 2D PINN study (section 2).
- Pretraining corpus sizes for MPP, Poseidon and PDEformer, and per-task numbers for Walrus and Unisolver, are not in the abstracts and were not extracted.
- The specific architectures tested in the May 2026 OOD benchmark are not named in its abstract.
- Whether any time-series foundation model has been evaluated on real bioprocess runs was not established; a search for chemical-process foundation models returned only general time-series models and Tennessee-Eastman benchmarks.

## 5. Precedents for vertical scientific foundation models beating general models, and whether pharma manufacturing has equivalent data

### Takeaway
The clear wins (weather, protein structure, materials) all had a large, standardised, public corpus or a trusted simulator able to generate one, and an objective benchmark with an entrenched incumbent to beat. Single-cell foundation models are the counter-example: with large but observational data and no such benchmark discipline, they fail to beat linear baselines. Pharma manufacturing currently resembles the second case on data availability: public data are a handful of simulators and one 100-batch simulated dataset.

### Cited Findings

**Wins**
- **[SUPPORTS]** "GraphCast: Learning skillful medium-range global weather forecasting", Lam et al., 2022/2023. Trained directly on reanalysis data at 0.25° resolution; outperforms the most accurate operational deterministic system on 90% of 1,380 verification targets; 10-day forecast in under one minute. — [arXiv 2212.12794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "GenCast: Diffusion-based ensemble forecasting for medium-range weather", Price et al., 2023 (Nature 2024). Beats ECMWF ENS on 97.4% of 1,320 targets (arXiv v2; secondary sources quote 97.2% for the Nature version); 15-day ensemble in about 8 minutes; trained on decades of reanalysis. — [arXiv 2312.15796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15796)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "A Foundation Model for the Earth System" (Aurora), Bodnar et al., 2024 (Nature 2025). Trained on over a million hours of diverse Earth-system data; outperforms operational forecasts in air quality, ocean waves, tropical cyclone tracks and high-resolution weather at orders of magnitude lower compute. — [arXiv 2405.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13063)
- **[MIXED]** Independent observation-based assessment during the South Asian monsoon finds differing regional biases across AI weather models (GraphCast, AIFS and GenCast with the smallest systematic biases; FourCastNet variants and Aurora with larger regional errors), a reminder that headline scorecards are evaluation-design dependent. — [arXiv 2509.01879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01879)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold", Jumper et al., Nature 2021. CASP14 median backbone accuracy 0.96 Å vs 2.8 Å for the next best method; trained on the PDB (about 100,000 unique experimental structures noted) with MSAs from databases including BFD at about 2.2 billion sequences, plus self-distillation on about 356,000 sequences. — [PMC8371605](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8371605/)
- **[SUPPORTS]** GNoME (DeepMind; figures from the company announcement, the journal paper page could not be fetched): 2.2 million new crystal structures predicted, about 380,000 on the stable hull, 736 independently synthesised; stability hit rate raised from about 50% to 80%; trained by active learning with DFT verification in the loop. — [DeepMind announcement](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/millions-of-new-materials-discovered-with-deep-learning/)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "MatterGen: a generative model for inorganic materials design", Zeni et al. (Microsoft), arXiv 2023 (journal version not verified in this session). Generated structures more than twice as likely to be novel and stable, and more than 15x closer to the local energy minimum, than prior generative models. — [arXiv 2312.03687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03687)
- **[SUPPORTS]** "A foundation model for atomistic materials chemistry" (MACE-MP-0), Batatia, Csányi et al., 2023. Trained on about 150k inorganic crystals from the public Materials Project (DFT simulator output); stable molecular dynamics out of the box across molecules and materials; fine-tunable to ab initio accuracy with a handful of data points. — [arXiv 2401.00096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00096v3)

**Cautionary tale: single-cell foundation models**
- **[UNDERMINES]** "Deep-learning-based gene perturbation effect prediction does not yet outperform simple linear baselines", Ahlmann-Eltze, Huber, Anders, Nature Methods 22:1657-1661, 2025. Five foundation models and two other deep models vs "no change", additive and simple linear baselines on single and double perturbation prediction: none beat the baselines. The preprint hypothesises this is partly because pretraining data are observational. — [Nature Index record](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6); [PMC12328236](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12328236/)
- **[UNDERMINES]** "Zero-shot evaluation reveals limitations of single-cell foundation models", Kedzierska, Crawford, Amini, Lu, Genome Biology 26:101, 2025. Geneformer and scGPT zero-shot underperform simpler methods (highly variable genes, scVI, Harmony); zero-shot testing exposes weaknesses hidden by fine-tuning-only evaluation. — [Genome Biology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-025-03574-x)

**Does pharma manufacturing have equivalent data?**
- **[UNDERMINES feasibility via public real data]** IndPenSim (Goldrick et al., 2015; extended 2019 in Computers & Chemical Engineering): simulation of a 100,000 L penicillin fermentation with a simulated Raman probe; the public dataset is 100 batches, about 2.5 GB (recipe-driven, operator-controlled, Raman-APC and fault batches); paper licensed CC BY-NC-ND. — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/pdnjz7zz5x/2); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10082538/)
- **[MIXED]** The Tennessee Eastman process has been the standard public chemical-process benchmark for nearly three decades (simulated; 33 process channels used in a 2026 virtual-sensing benchmark). — [arXiv 2303.05904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05904); [arXiv 2603.24602](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24602)
- **[SUPPORTS feasibility via simulators]** Open simulators exist to generate synthetic corpora: PharmaPy (dynamic drug-substance/drug-product flowsheets), BioSTEAM (design, simulation, techno-economic analysis and LCA of biorefineries under uncertainty; Cortés-Peña et al., 2020, ACS Sustainable Chem. Eng.), a differentiable JAX population-balance solver, and custom CHO emulators used in the DataHow/Imperial papers. — [PharmaPy](https://github.com/CryPTSys/PharmaPy); [BioSTEAM](https://github.com/BioSTEAMDevelopmentGroup/biosteam); [JAX PBE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00742); [CHO simulator for BO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10970)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Reviews describe upstream bioprocess data as small because cultivation is costly and slow, and call for standardised benchmark datasets that do not yet exist. — [arXiv 2506.12322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12322); [B&B 2026 review](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.70186)
- **[MIXED]** PhysiX's authors say the largest physics simulation datasets hold only tens of thousands of samples and resort to video-model initialisation; even the best-resourced physics domain is data-poor relative to language. — [arXiv 2506.17774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17774v1)

### Inferences
- Common factors in the wins: (a) one shared, standardised corpus (ERA5 reanalysis, PDB, Materials Project DFT); (b) a trusted generator (numerical weather prediction via reanalysis, DFT) that can label or create data on demand; (c) domain structure encoded in the architecture (sphere/mesh, equivariance, MSAs); (d) a public scorecard against an incumbent (HRES/ENS, CASP, Matbench Discovery). Pharma manufacturing has (c) available through mass balances and unit-operation structure, partially (b) through mechanistic simulators of uncertain fidelity, and essentially none of (a) or (d).
- The single-cell failure pattern (large observational corpus, no interventional coverage, weak baselines omitted) is the specific risk for a model pretrained on historical batch records: GMP runs are tightly controlled around setpoints, so they are observational and low-variance, whereas the hybrid-model literature succeeds because of deliberately designed (DoE/iDoE) excitation.
- The most realistic data strategy implied by the precedents is the Panda/MACE route: pretrain on a very large family of simulated processes (randomised kinetics, stoichiometries, scales, feeding policies) and fine-tune or prompt on a few real runs; the GNoME route (active learning with the simulator or lab in the loop) is the analogue for experiment design.
- A public benchmark with tuned baselines would be a contribution in itself and a precondition for any credible "beats general models" claim.

### Gaps
- Pangu-Weather and ESM were not verified in this session and are omitted. Aurora's parameter count was not confirmed (the abstract does not state it).
- gPROMS and other commercial simulators were not researched; no information on licensing for synthetic-data generation.
- No inventory of real, open CHO fed-batch datasets was obtained; the search specifically for public CHO benchmark data returned nothing, which is weak evidence that none of significant size exists.
- Fidelity of mechanistic simulators relative to real CHO processes (the sim-to-real gap) is not quantified in any source found.

## 6. Combining LLMs with mechanistic models: does the LLM layer help or harm?

### Takeaway
LLMs add measurable value as proposers of model structure (equations, hybrid architectures, flowsheets) when wrapped around numerical fitting and evaluation, but the best system on a memorisation-resistant equation-discovery benchmark reaches only 31.5% symbolic accuracy, and as numerical forecasters LLM components add nothing over simple attention layers. No study was found that tests an LLM-built model against an expert-built hybrid model on real bioprocess data.

### Cited Findings
- **[UNDERMINES LLMs as the numerical core; SUPPORTS the thesis]** "Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?", Tan, Merrill, Gupta, Althoff, Hartvigsen, NeurIPS 2024 (spotlight). Ablations on three LLM-based forecasters over eight datasets: removing the LLM or replacing it with a basic attention layer does not hurt and often improves results; pretrained LLMs do no better than randomly initialised ones and do not help few-shot. — [arXiv 2406.16964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract.html)
- **[UNDERMINES the thesis: LLM + harness works for structure]** "LLM-SR: Scientific Equation Discovery via Programming with Large Language Models", Shojaee, Meidani, Gupta, Barati Farimani, Reddy, ICLR 2025 (oral). LLM proposes equation skeletons as programs, parameters are fitted numerically, evolutionary search iterates; on four benchmarks designed to resist recall it finds physically plausible equations that beat symbolic-regression baselines, especially out of domain. — [arXiv 2404.18400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18400)
- **[MIXED]** "LLM-SRBench: A New Benchmark for Scientific Equation Discovery with Large Language Models", Shojaee et al., ICML 2025 (oral). 239 problems (chemistry 36, biology 24, physics 43, materials 25) built to defeat memorisation; evidence that LLMs recall Feynman-benchmark equations; the best system reaches only 31.5% symbolic accuracy (28.1% on the synthetic discovery subset). — [arXiv 2504.10415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10415)
- **[UNDERMINES the thesis]** "Automatically Learning Hybrid Digital Twins of Dynamical Systems" (HDTwinGen), Holt, Liu, van der Schaar, NeurIPS 2024 (spotlight). An LLM proposes and iteratively refines hybrid (mechanistic + neural) model specifications; parameters are optimised offline; evaluation feedback drives evolution. Reported to generalise better and be more sample-efficient than baselines (no numbers in abstract). — [arXiv 2410.23691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23691)
- **[MIXED]** "Automatic Ordinary Differential Equations Discovery For Biological Systems Using Large Language Model Powered Agentic System" (MEDA), Krongauz, Zulti, Segal, Lazebnik, July 2026. LLM + symbolic regression agent; ablations show knowledge-guided formalisation and mechanistic constraints are essential, and numerical fitting alone yields trajectory-compatible but biologically wrong equations. No metrics in the abstract. — [arXiv 2607.13608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13608)
- **[MIXED]** Related agentic ODE/hybrid discovery systems outside bioprocess: LEADS (LLM agent searching a structured action space of hybrid cardiac-electrophysiology components with gradient-based fitting), AgentODE (LLM proposes ODE structures from aggregate clinical data), and Model Discovery Agent (LLM-assisted Bayesian experiment design). — [arXiv 2606.18154](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18154); [arXiv 2607.00733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00733); [arXiv 2608.09696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09696)
- **[MIXED]** "Automatic Generation of SBML Kinetic Models from Natural Language Texts Using GPT" (KinModGPT), 2023: text-to-kinetic-model via GPT and Tellurium. — [PMC10138937](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10138937/)
- **[MIXED]** "Using (Not-so) Large Language Models to Generate Simulation Models in a Formal DSL: A Study on Reaction Networks", Kreikemeyer, Jankowski, Wilsdorf, Uhrmacher, ACM TOMACS 35(4), 2025. A fine-tuned 7B open model reproduces the reference reaction-network model from a natural-language description in up to 84.5% of cases, still below large commercial LLMs. — [arXiv 2503.01675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01675)
- **[UNDERMINES the thesis for flowsheet-level tasks]** Process-simulation agents (2026): "Text-to-flowsheet" (ETH, Digital Discovery) converts process descriptions into Aspen Plus simulations and is evaluated against 101 expert-drawn flowsheets and 50 chemical-engineering experts; Sketch2Simulation builds Aspen HYSYS models from diagrams across four case studies; CeProAgents is a hierarchical multi-agent system with a six-task benchmark (CeProBench), whose authors say the results show both the potential and the current limits of LLMs for industrial chemical engineering; an AVEVA Process Simulation agent is evaluated only qualitatively. — [Text-to-flowsheet](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/dd/d6dd00060f); [Sketch2Simulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24629); [CeProAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01654); [AVEVA agent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11650)
- **[MIXED]** A non-LLM route to the same goal exists: Rogers et al. 2025 transfer kinetic-model structure with ANN corrections, feature attribution and symbolic regression (80% structure recovery with multiple passes; section 1). — [PMC12417796](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12417796/)
- **[MIXED]** An August 2026 industry conference programme lists a talk on AI-assisted CHO modelling including hybrid models and "LLM-assisted workflow automation"; no published evaluation. — [Bioprocessing Summit programme](https://www.bioprocessingsummit.com/Bioproduction)

### Inferences
- The evidence separates two roles. As a *structure proposer and tool orchestrator* around a numerical core, the LLM helps (LLM-SR, HDTwinGen, flowsheet agents). As the *numerical model itself*, it does not (Tan et al.; context parroting in section 4). The defensible form of the thesis is therefore narrower: a native-representation numerical core is needed, and an LLM harness around it is complementary, not a competitor.
- This is also the strongest counter-argument to the thesis: "general LLM + harness + mechanistic simulator + numerical fitter" is a viable architecture that already produces hybrid models automatically, without a vertical foundation model. The open question is whether a pretrained process model beats that pipeline on new-product data efficiency.
- LLM-SRBench's memorisation finding matters for bioprocess: an LLM will readily write Monod/Contois/Haldane kinetics from memory, which looks competent but is recall; performance on genuinely novel mechanisms is about 30%.
- A decisive, buildable experiment: on held-out products, compare (a) expert hybrid model, (b) LLM-agent-built hybrid (HDTwinGen-style) with the same data, (c) pretrained embedding/in-context process model, (d) LLM given raw runs in context; measure error vs number of runs, extrapolation outside the DoE box, and expert hours.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found in which an LLM agent constructs and validates a cell-culture or fermentation kinetic/hybrid model on real data, and none quantifying harm (e.g., hallucinated mechanisms accepted into a GMP model).
- Quantitative results for Text-to-flowsheet and CeProBench were not retrievable (publisher page blocked; abstract has no numbers).
- Evidence on LLM in-context regression over tabular process data (as opposed to time-series forecasting) was not searched before the budget ran out.

## 7. Uncertainty quantification, extrapolation guarantees and interpretability across model classes (GMP validation relevance)

### Takeaway
No model class offers extrapolation guarantees; what exists is empirical. The accepted pattern in regulated use is a low-parameter, physically interpretable model whose prediction error is measured and converted into conservative acceptance limits. Hybrid models with GP or ensemble components give calibrated-looking uncertainty and comparable accuracy to full kinetic models, while free-form neural terms are frequently non-identifiable, and regulators themselves note there is no standard for validating neural-network process models.

### Cited Findings
- **[UNDERMINES complex models for GMP]** Regulator-accepted practice converts model error directly into the control limit: Merck's RTD strategy uses RMSEP (1.4% vs a 6% criterion) and tablet-weight variance with a one-sided 95% factor to tighten the 85-115% rejection limits. — [PMC8874656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8874656)
- **[UNDERMINES learned controllers]** FDA/CDER researchers state that standardised mechanisms for neural-network model verification and validation are lacking and that establishing regulatory standards for AI-model credibility is challenging. — [FDA Science Forum](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/fda-science-forum/development-artificial-intelligence-based-predictive-controller-continuous-pharmaceutical); [summary](https://www.europeanpharmaceuticalreview.com/news/270194/ai-framework-continuous-manufacturing-fda-cder-research/)
- **[SUPPORTS hybrid + probabilistic components]** GP-embedded hybrids: Rogers et al. 2022 report much improved predictive confidence at similar accuracy to the full kinetic model; Hutter et al. 2021 use a GP as the kinetic term and select embedding dimension by log marginal likelihood. — [DOI 10.1016/j.bej.2022.108761](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bej.2022.108761); [arXiv 2011.13863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13863)
- **[MIXED]** Uncertainty widens on transfer: in Bayer et al. 2021 the iDoE-trained hybrid had higher errors and wider confidence intervals than the shaker-trained model, and test NRMSE at 15 L was about double training NRMSE. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2021.740215/full)
- **[MIXED]** "About Model Validation in Bioprocessing" (MDPI Processes, 2021) classifies models as statistical, mechanistic or hybrid and reviews bootstrapped ensembles and validation profile likelihood for uncertainty, with a section on health-authority recommendations (content not extracted). — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-9717/9/6/961)
- **[MIXED]** Bayesian treatment of hybrid bioprocess models: sequential importance sampling with approximate Bayesian computation to obtain posteriors for knowledge-graph hybrid models; and an AIChE 2021 abstract on hierarchical Bayesian quantification of the uncertainty of hybrid-model uncertainty estimates (the likelihood is often not known exactly). — [arXiv 2205.02410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.02410); [AIChE 2021](https://proceedings.aiche.org/conferences/aiche-annual-meeting/2021/proceeding/paper/415f-general-strategy-quantification-uncertainty-on-hybrid-models-uncertainty-estimates)
- **[UNDERMINES free-form neural terms]** Identifiability and plausibility failures in UDEs: non-identifiable neural terms, wrong recovered mechanisms, negative concentrations without constraints, training loss uninformative about predictive quality, and information criteria favouring the 9-parameter ODE over the 153-parameter UDE. — [PMC12398592](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12398592/)
- **[SUPPORTS interpretable hybrids]** Interpretability by construction: Functional-Hybrid models replace the network with ranked domain functional forms (2x accuracy, half the experiments, in silico); the oligonucleotide solver-in-the-loop model is about 99.4% mechanistic with the network correcting only the isotherm. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.06.09.447664v1); [PMC12235242](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12235242/)
- **[MIXED]** Physics priors help only when weighted correctly: under systematic solubility-model error, over-weighting the physics term raises error. — [arXiv 2602.07184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07184)
- **[UNDERMINES foundation models on extrapolation]** Out-of-distribution behaviour of physics foundation models is regime-dependent and not fixed by scale or pretraining; generic time-series foundation models drift toward the mean when they stop copying context; Panda can drift to fixed points on long horizons. — [arXiv 2605.29283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29283); [arXiv 2505.11349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11349); [arXiv 2605.04024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04024)
- **[MIXED]** The ML-for-PDE literature's accuracy claims are unreliable as a basis for validation arguments: 79% of superiority claims used weak baselines. — [arXiv 2407.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07218)
- **[MIXED]** ICH Q13 includes a dedicated process-models section and expects characterisation of process dynamics (e.g., RTD) for transient events, applying to both chemical entities and therapeutic proteins. — [FDA Q13](https://www.fda.gov/media/165775/download)

### Inferences
- Ranking by GMP validation tractability, from the evidence above: low-order mechanistic (RTD, lumped kinetic) > hybrid with a constrained, small or symbolic learned term and GP/ensemble uncertainty > UDE/neural-ODE with free-form networks > pretrained foundation models, for which no validation precedent or OOD guarantee was found.
- For a vertical model to "win on control", its uncertainty must be converted into acceptance limits the way the Merck strategy does. That argues for an architecture that outputs a validated-domain flag and predictive intervals (GP head, ensemble, or conformal layer) and for claims scoped to decision support and development rather than GMP release until validation precedent exists.
- A foundation model can still fit this regime if the deployed artefact is a small, product-specific hybrid model distilled or instantiated from it, with the large model used offline for structure and prior selection. That keeps the validated object interpretable.
- Because training loss does not predict predictive quality for flexible hybrids, validation protocols need held-out *conditions* (outside the DoE box, different scale), not held-out time points or random run splits.

### Gaps
- No source was found giving formal extrapolation guarantees for any of these model classes in a process setting; conformal prediction for bioprocess models was not searched.
- The specific regulatory expectations for hybrid or ML models (ICH Q8/Q9/Q10 points-to-consider model-impact categories, EMA/FDA AI discussion papers, ICH Q13 section text) were not extracted; the Q13 PDF could not be parsed and searches on this were cut short by the search budget.
- No published case was found of a hybrid (mechanistic + ML) model used in a GMP release decision or filed control strategy for a biologic; the only regulator-accepted model with published detail is the purely mechanistic RTD model.
- Head-to-head calibration comparisons (coverage of prediction intervals) between hybrid, GP, PLS and neural models on the same bioprocess data were not found.
