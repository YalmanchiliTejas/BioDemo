## 1. What is "Jev"?

### Takeaway
Jev is confidently identified: a hosted, proprietary "System One" decision model from TypeSafe AI (San Francisco), launched 15 September 2026, that takes a text/JSON state plus typed questions and returns a choice/score/yes-no probability in one non-autoregressive pass, with no text generation. Its architecture, size, training data and calibration metrics are undisclosed, and independent tests show it is much cheaper than LLMs but neither more accurate than frontier LLMs nor reliably better calibrated than a logistic regression; it is also a text-input model that is documented as weak on arithmetic, so it is not evidence for "native numeric representations".

### Cited Findings

**Primary sources (all [VENDOR])**
- Launch post "Introducing System One Models and Jev" (TypeSafe AI, Sept 2026). Describes a "new model architecture" with a hardware-aware "parallel sampler" that "generates all outputs in a single query"; outputs are typed values with schemas defined in advance; choice cardinality "up to 255" (above that, two-stage score-then-choose); the model "gives up string generation". No parameter count, layer count, training data or hardware disclosed. — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- Training method is named "Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)", rewarded for "epistemically honest probabilities", and explicitly contrasted with RLHF (human preference) and RLVR (programmatically verifiable outputs). The method is unpublished; the FAQ lists questions about reward definition and data without answers. — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- Latency 70–500 ms end to end; claimed 40x–200x faster than frontier LLMs on System-One-shaped queries; homepage claims 193.6x faster and 444.6x cheaper on its workflow evals. Price $0.042 per million input tokens, output free. TypeSafe says it cannot prove the price is not subsidised. — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- Vendor eval (four TypeSafe-written workflows: security incidents, agent-trace observability, invoice processing, customer service), workflow mode, mean accuracy / cost per case / time per case: Jev 67.8% / $0.0004 / 0.4 s; GPT-5.6 Terra 67.9% / $0.0304 / 10.1 s; GPT-5.6 Sol 74.1% / $0.0836 / 23.3 s; Claude Opus 5 73.1% / $0.1761 / 37.8 s; Sonnet 5 67.8% / $0.1174 / 78.1 s; Haiku 4.5 53.6% / $0.0195 / 12.5 s. Ground truth is "consensus labels" from averaging GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 at high thinking, not human labels. No ECE or Brier reported; number of cases not stated. — [evals.typesafe.ai](https://evals.typesafe.ai)
- Docs: question types Choice, Score, Noul (0–1 yes/no), Box; state may be text, JSON or images; multiple questions evaluated in parallel and independently; multi-factor judgements should be split into atomic questions and combined in the caller's code. No on-prem, self-hosted, VPC or open-weights option is described. — [TypeSafe docs](https://docs.typesafe.ai)

**Who built it, access, adoption ([MEDIA/OPINION])**
- InfoQ (1 Oct 2026), "TypeSafe AI Releases Jev: a Decision-Only Model That Returns Typed Probabilities Instead of Text": founders named as Diogo Almeida, Erik Gafni and Sasha Sheng; Almeida described as a former OpenAI researcher. Context window 32,000 tokens. Available by API, Vercel AI Gateway, Netlify, and a LangChain `TypeSafeClassifier`. Vercel said Jev reached nearly 13% of paid teams within 24 hours. Documented "jaggedness": unreliable counting, arithmetic and date comparison, accuracy loss on large noisy state, with advice to keep mathematics in application code. Users are told to pin versions such as `jev-1.13.0`. No funding information. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)
- DataCamp (16 Sept 2026): announced 15 Sept 2026; early access by waitlist; endpoint `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone`; vendor reliability claims of 0% structured-output and tool-call error vs 0.58% (OpenAI luna/terra), 5.73% (Opus 5), 45.5% (Haiku 4.5); notes that returning a probability with no rationale is a problem for audits in regulated domains. — [DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/system-one-models-jev)
- XenoSpectrum (Y. Kobayashi, 20 Sept 2026): no architecture details are public; cannot tell whether Jev is encoder-type or decoder-type; a fork of LLaDA (a diffusion language model) sits in TypeSafe's GitHub organisation, which the author says is not evidence of lineage. — [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/jev-typesafe-bert-classifier-decomposition/)
- Victor Dibia, "How Jev works: calibrated decision models" (28 Sept 2026): TypeSafe's only published calibration evidence is one figure, 27 of 30 answers correct above 0.9 confidence on an older model (95% interval 74%–97%); the author's best guess is a logit-scoring readout over a fixed option set. — [Designing with AI](https://newsletter.victordibia.com/p/how-jev-works-calibrated-decision)

**Independent tests and criticism**
- **MIXED [INDEPENDENT TEST]** Tool-call risk benchmark (webofmike, 60 hand-labelled cases, 17 and 24 Sept 2026): jev-latest, jev-preview and Claude Sonnet 5 all scored 91.7%. Latency p50 421.6 ms vs 1,371 ms (about 3.3x, vs the vendor's 193.6x). Cost per call ~$0.0000173 vs ~$0.0007035 (about 40.6x, vs the vendor's 444.6x). ECE (10 bins) 0.0712 (jev-latest), 0.0505 (jev-preview), 0.0633 (Sonnet 5). Jev returned confidence exactly 1.000 on 40 of 60 cases. — [jev-benchmark README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/themsquared/jev-benchmark/main/README.md)
- **UNDERMINES [INDEPENDENT TEST]** Phishing, 2,000 emails, single question: Jev 62.6% vs Claude Haiku 4.5 81.3%; AUROC 0.689 vs 0.837; ECE 0.154 vs 0.097; median latency 239 ms vs 687 ms; cost per 1,000 emails $0.038 vs $0.462. On a held-out 1,000: Jev single signal 89.4%, a two-line regex 91.8%, Haiku 94.2%, Jev five-question composite with fitted weights 95.0% vs Haiku composite 93.2% (McNemar p=0.063). A spam test had Jev roughly tied with TF-IDF logistic regression (98.33% vs 98.39%). — [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/jev-typesafe-bert-classifier-decomposition/)
- **UNDERMINES [INDEPENDENT TEST / OPINION]** Nash Borges (Sophos SVP AI), "Jev's paradox": CLINC150 (200 requests) Jev 87% vs gpt-5.4-nano 80% vs GPT-5.6 Terra 92%; Banking77 (208 requests) Jev 83% vs 93% for a supervised bge-small + logistic regression; confidence exactly 1.0 on 102 of 200 CLINC150 items, six of them wrong; on a 300-question out-of-distribution urgency test Jev was 45% correct with 74% mean stated probability; thresholds tuned on one half of the data missed targets on the other half. — [Sophos](https://www.sophos.com/blog/jevs-paradox-hidden-cost-of-cheap-ai-decisions)
- **UNDERMINES [PREPRINT]** "Are AI Decision Models Biased? A Case Study of Jev" (Imperatrice, Kameneva, Greenberg, Romano; arXiv 2610.19619, 14 Oct 2026): 41,500 responses plus 15,000 on 30 conjunction tasks. Jev does very well on cognitive-reflection items, but bias manipulations shift returned probabilities even when the selected answer is unchanged; it violates the conjunction rule in 54% of responses; outputs are sensitive to irrelevant numbers and option order. — [arXiv 2610.19619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19619)
- **UNDERMINES [PREPRINT]** "Labels Override Definitions in Jev-Style Typed Decision Models" (Azizi, Baghaei Potraghloo, Pedram; arXiv 2610.02586, 1 Oct 2026). Studies open-weight implementations of the interface (not Jev itself), including reading methods on a Qwen2.5 backbone, on eleven tasks plus a synthetic PolicyBench. Deleting all option definitions left accuracy essentially unchanged (0.8559 vs 0.8487); renaming options to A/B raised accuracy by +0.1511; in a second model a contradictory label dropped accuracy from 0.8511 to 0.2281. Cause traced to prompt rendering, not the decision head. — [arXiv 2610.02586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02586)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** "Calibrated Decision Models for Autonomous Penetration-Testing Harnesses: JEV and Laya as System One Decision Layers…" (J. A. dos Santos Barbosa; arXiv 2609.28940, 24 Sept 2026): exploratory case study; reviews Jev, Jev-Ultrafast and the open-source Laya; results "do not establish statistical significance". — [arXiv 2609.28940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28940)
- **[MEDIA/OPINION]** Armin Ronacher (via TechCrunch, as reported by InfoQ): the design "delegates the hallucination problem a little bit to the user", who must decide whether a 50% probability is worth acting on. Hacker News commenters (unnamed, via InfoQ): "can't hallucinate" is wrong because a wrong-but-valid value is still possible, and LLMs can also be constrained to structured output. An OpenChamber analysis of 12,759 launch tweets found a median user-reported speedup of 7x and median cost saving of 30x. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/typesafe-ai-jev-released/)

### Inferences
- The RFS cites Jev for its **interface** (typed decision + probability, zero-shot, no per-task classifier), not for its architecture. Nothing public shows Jev uses non-text representations: it is priced per input token, has a 32k-token context, and open "Jev-style" clones are built on LLM backbones with a decision head. So Jev **supports** the "control/output" leg of the thesis and **does not support** the "native non-text representation" leg.
- Jev is unsuitable as-is for numeric process data: the vendor itself documents weak counting, arithmetic and date comparison and degradation on large noisy state. In the RFS, TimesFM-3 (not Jev) is the example of a native-numeric model.
- The honest independent read is "LLM-class accuracy at roughly 10–40x lower cost and 3–7x lower latency", not the vendor's 200–400x. A pharma pitch should use the independently measured multipliers.
- Hosted-API-only availability conflicts with GMP on-prem expectations and with the sibling YC "Air-Gapped Economy" request.

### Gaps
- Parameter count, architecture family, training data, reward definition and vendor calibration curves are **not public**. Funding is undisclosed in every source read.
- A UCLA eScholarship item (https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4t4449jv, CC BY 4.0) was described in search results as an audit of Jev 1.13.0 over 3,244 evaluations finding task- and question-form-dependent calibration; the PDF would not parse, so title, authors and numbers are unverified.
- A Towards Data Science post (URL slug "scikit-learn-matched-or-beat-jevs-calibration-on-banking77") returned 403. The search snippet reported ECE 0.022 (temperature-scaled logistic regression) vs 0.014 (Jev) on a 7-intent BANKING77 slice, statistically indistinguishable. Snippet only.
- A search snippet attributed "vendor-reported ECE of 0.246 on TypeSafe's own evaluation set" to a paper; I could not find it in the abstract I read. Treat as unverified.
- The original TechCrunch piece (18 Sept 2026) and the Hacker News thread were not located directly; they are quoted only via InfoQ.
- "Gemini Decision 1" appears in DataCamp's related links as a competitor; not verified.

## 2. TimesFM-3

### Takeaway
TimesFM-3 exists exactly as the RFS describes: Google Research released it on 31 August 2026 as a 330M-parameter decoder-only model natively pretrained for multivariate forecasting with covariates and single-pass decoding. Google claims the top average rank on GIFT-Eval, FEV-Bench and TIME, but publishes no numeric scores or inference-cost figures, compares only against Chronos-2, Toto 2.0 and TimesFM-2.5, and ships the weights under a non-commercial licence.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR]** "TimesFM-3: a zero-shot foundation model for multivariate forecasting" (Ayush Jain, Rajat Sen; Google Research blog, 31 Aug 2026). 330M parameters; pretrained on a real plus synthetic corpus of more than 1 trillion time points; decoder-only transformer with 32-step patches; alternating causal temporal attention and full variate attention (a 2D grid); future-known covariates handled by concatenating lookahead patches; non-autoregressive decoding via Contiguous Patch Masking (masked horizon filled in one forward pass; method linked to arXiv 2505.23719); nine quantiles (10th–90th). — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/timesfm-3-a-zero-shot-foundation-model-for-multivariate-forecasting/)
- **[VENDOR]** Benchmarks: claimed first by average rank among pretrained foundation models on GIFT-Eval, FEV-Bench and TIME for both point and probabilistic metrics; univariate mode matches or beats competitors. Baselines named: Chronos-2, the Toto 2.0 family, TimesFM-2.5. Results are in charts only; no MASE/CRPS values in text; a chart of inference time vs horizon has no numbers. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/timesfm-3-a-zero-shot-foundation-model-for-multivariate-forecasting/)
- **[VENDOR]** Model card: 0.3B parameters, 20 transformer layers, model dimension 1280, 16 heads; context patch 32 and horizon patch 64; "TimesFM Non-Commercial License v1.0"; no benchmark numbers; the linked paper is still the original TimesFM paper (arXiv 2310.10688), so there is no dedicated TimesFM-3 paper. — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch)
- **[MEDIA]** Checkpoint is about 1.32 GB; up to 32 variates in the published configuration; weights restricted to non-commercial, non-production use while repository code is Apache-2.0; BigQuery integration promised "in the coming weeks". — [DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/google-releases-timesfm-3)
- **[MEDIA]** TimesFM-2.5 for comparison: 200M parameters, 16K context, mainly univariate, optional quantiles. Caveats listed: quantile intervals must be checked on your own data; a tuned domain model can still win on unusual seasonality, structural breaks or special constraints; no first-party metered API or price. — [Build Fast with AI](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/google-timesfm-3-review)
- **[PREPRINT]** The main rival, "Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting" (Ansari, Shchur et al., Amazon; arXiv 2510.15821, 17 Oct 2025), uses group attention for in-context learning across related series, variates and covariates, is trained on synthetic data that imposes multivariate structure on univariate series, and reports state of the art on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II. — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821)
- The RFS's own wording about TimesFM-3: forecasts related series together with no task-specific fine-tuning in one forward pass; "Training happens once, and each new user brings their own history as context." — [YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

### Inferences
- **SUPPORTS** the representation leg of the thesis: the state of the art in numeric forecasting is a 330M patch-based model with native variate attention, not a text-token LLM.
- **UNDERMINES** the moat leg: the exemplar comes from Google, is trained on generic time series, and a second hyperscaler (Amazon) ships an equivalent. A pharma startup would compete against free horizontal models unless process-specific data produces a measurable gap.
- The non-commercial licence means the RFS's flagship example cannot legally be put into a production GMP system by default; only the BigQuery route or a competitor model is commercially usable.
- "Each user brings their own history as context" is the template for pharma: a model pretrained across processes, conditioned at inference on a site's golden batches. For batch processes the open question is whether a context of tens of historical batches is enough.

### Gaps
- No numeric GIFT-Eval/FEV-Bench/TIME scores were found in text; the leaderboards were not read directly. No published comparison against Moirai 2, TiRex, TabPFN-TS, or against LLMs was found. No inference-cost, throughput or hardware figures are published.
- Maximum context length and horizon are not stated on the blog or model card. The "32 variates" limit comes from one secondary source only.
- Whether BigQuery multivariate support has shipped is unclear (one November article says "integrated into BigQuery"; launch coverage said "coming weeks").
- MarkTechPost's launch article returned 403.

## 3. "Typed decision + probability" as a model class

### Takeaway
The class is real and older than Jev: in-context tabular models, zero-shot encoders and LLM logit-scoring all return a distribution over caller-defined options in one pass. The strongest pharma-relevant fact is regulatory: the EU's draft GMP Annex 22 confines critical GMP use to static, deterministic models, excludes generative AI and LLMs, and asks for logged confidence scores with an "undecided" threshold. Calibration, however, is not free: it degrades out of distribution for every method checked, including Jev.

### Cited Findings

**Regulatory definition of the acceptable model class**
- **SUPPORTS [REGULATOR]** EudraLex Volume 4, draft Annex 22 "Artificial Intelligence" (European Commission DG SANTE, drafted by the EMA GMDP Inspectors Working Group with PIC/S; consultation 7 July–7 October 2025). Scope: applies to "static models"; dynamic models that learn during use "should not be used in critical GMP applications"; applies to "models with a deterministic output"; models that may give different outputs for identical inputs "should not be used in critical GMP applications"; consequently the annex "does not apply to Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLM), and such models should not be used in critical GMP applications". In non-critical use an LLM needs a qualified human in the loop. — [Annex 22 draft PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf); [consultation page](https://health.ec.europa.eu/consultations/stakeholders-consultation-eudralex-volume-4-good-manufacturing-practice-guidelines-chapter-4-annex_en)
- **SUPPORTS [REGULATOR]** Same draft, section 9: the system should "log the confidence score" for each prediction and, when confidence is very low, consider flagging the outcome as "undecided". Section 8: record feature attribution (SHAP, LIME, heat maps) for decisions such as rejection. Section 4.3: acceptance criteria must be "at least as high as the performance of the process it replaces". Section 10: change control, configuration control, performance monitoring and input-space drift monitoring. — [Annex 22 draft PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)

**In-context tabular models (distribution out, no per-task training)**
- **SUPPORTS [PEER-REVIEWED]** "Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model" (Hollmann, Müller, Purucker, Krishnakumar, Körfer, Hoo, Schirrmeister, Hutter; Nature 637:319–326, 2025). TabPFN targets up to 10,000 samples and 500 features; one forward pass takes about 2.8 s (classification) and beats baselines tuned for 4 hours (about 5,140x speedup); normalised ROC AUC 0.939 vs CatBoost 0.752 at defaults; regression returns a full piecewise-constant predictive distribution; pretrained on roughly 100–130 million synthetic datasets from structural causal models. Limits: at most 10 classes; inference about 0.2 s per sample at 10,000 rows vs 0.0002 s for CatBoost. — [PMC11711098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711098/)
- **SUPPORTS [PREPRINT, vendor-authored]** "TabPFN-2.5: Advancing the State of the Art in Tabular Foundation Models" (Grinsztajn et al., Prior Labs; arXiv 2511.08667, Nov 2025): scales to 50,000 rows and 2,000 features; matches AutoGluon 1.4 (a four-hour tuned ensemble) on TabArena; 100% win rate vs default XGBoost on small classification sets; adds distillation into an MLP or tree ensemble for low-latency deployment. — [arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667)

**Zero-shot encoders for text decisions**
- **SUPPORTS [PREPRINT]** "GLiNER: Generalist Model for Named Entity Recognition using Bidirectional Transformer" (Zaratiana, Tomeh, Holat, Charnois; arXiv 2311.08526, 2023): a compact bidirectional encoder that extracts arbitrary entity types in parallel and is reported to outperform ChatGPT and fine-tuned LLMs zero-shot on NER benchmarks. — [arXiv 2311.08526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08526)
- **SUPPORTS [PREPRINT]** "GLiClass: Generalist Lightweight Model for Sequence Classification Tasks" (Stepanov et al.; arXiv 2508.07662, Aug 2025): adapts GLiNER to zero/few-shot classification with efficiency "comparable to embedding-based methods"; argues generative LLMs are inconsistent and costly while cross-encoders scale poorly with label count; adapts PPO for training from sparse data or human feedback. — [arXiv 2508.07662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07662)

**LLM-based routes to the same output (the main counter-argument)**
- **UNDERMINES [INDEPENDENT TEST]** Dibia's experiments on open Qwen2.5 models (one A10G GPU): reading option probabilities from logits ("scoring") was 7x–54x faster than generating on the 7B model; base models were near-calibrated (ECE 0.02 at 10 options), instruct models overconfident (ECE 0.10), and temperature scaling brought that to 0.03 given labelled data; a stock Qwen2.5-7B instruct reached 93.0% (ECE 0.061) on a 662-message prompt-injection set where Jev scored 96.5% (ECE 0.0588), with the author warning that prompts differed. — [Designing with AI](https://newsletter.victordibia.com/p/how-jev-works-calibrated-decision)
- **MIXED [PEER-REVIEWED]** "Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs" (Xiong, Hu, Lu, Li, Fu, He, Hooi; ICLR 2024): LLMs that verbalise confidence "tend to be overconfident"; white-box methods beat black-box ones only narrowly (AUROC 0.522 to 0.605); all methods struggle on tasks needing professional knowledge. — [arXiv 2306.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** "Beyond Binary Rewards: Training LMs to Reason About Their Uncertainty" (Damani, Puri, Slocum, Shenfeld, Choshen, Kim, Andreas, MIT; arXiv 2507.16806, 2025, revised May 2026). Reward = correctness plus Brier score (RLCR), on Qwen2.5-7B. HotpotQA in-domain: base ECE 0.53, ordinary RLVR 0.37, RLCR 0.03 at unchanged accuracy (about 62–63%). Out of domain: RLVR made calibration **worse** than the base model (ECE 0.40 to 0.46), and RLCR's ECE rose to 0.21. — [arXiv 2507.16806 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)

**Jev head-to-head (from section 1)**
- **MIXED** ECE 0.051–0.071 vs Sonnet 5's 0.063 on 60 tool calls; **UNDERMINES** ECE 0.154 vs Haiku 4.5's 0.097 on phishing; **UNDERMINES** 83% vs 93% for bge-small + logistic regression on Banking77. — [jev-benchmark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/themsquared/jev-benchmark/main/README.md); [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/jev-typesafe-bert-classifier-decomposition/); [Sophos](https://www.sophos.com/blog/jevs-paradox-hidden-cost-of-cheap-ai-decisions)

**Conformal prediction in a pharma consortium**
- **SUPPORTS [PEER-REVIEWED]** MELLODDY evaluated applicability domain with conformal efficiency; federation raised median conformal efficiency by at least 12% for most partners and above 20% for one. This is an industrial pharma precedent for conformal wrappers as the confidence layer. — [PMC11005050](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005050/)

### Inferences
- Annex 22 is the single strongest argument for "typed decision + probability + determinism" in pharma: an autoregressive LLM sampled at non-zero temperature fails the scope test on its face, while a fixed-weight model with a deterministic forward pass and a logged confidence score fits it. This favours the thesis on **control**, not necessarily on accuracy.
- Careful reading: the draft's phrase "probabilistic output" means non-repeatable output for identical input, not "outputs a probability". A deterministic model that emits a calibrated probability is in scope. A pinned LLM read out through logits at temperature zero could arguably also be made deterministic, so the exclusion of LLMs is categorical in the text but technically contestable.
- The "no per-task classifier" promise competes with a very cheap incumbent: when a few hundred labels exist, a supervised small model beats Jev (93% vs 83%). Zero-shot typed models win only where labels are scarce, which is the common case for deviations and rare faults in a plant.
- Calibration does not transfer: RLCR (0.03 to 0.21), Jev's out-of-distribution test (45% correct at 74% confidence) and threshold instability all show that a probability is only trustworthy on the distribution it was validated on. In pharma this implies per-site, per-product calibration checks and a conformal or threshold layer, which Annex 22 section 10.4 (input-space monitoring) already requires.
- Jev's lack of rationale conflicts with Annex 22 section 8 (feature attribution). A pharma decision model needs attribution as a first-class output.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study of typed-decision models vs frontier LLMs on manufacturing or pharma decisions was found.
- Not verified this session (known from background, no URL given): TabICL, TabDPT, TabPFN-TS; NLI-based zero-shot classifiers; Guo et al. 2017 on neural-network calibration; Kadavath et al. 2022 on LLM self-knowledge; conformal prediction tutorials (Angelopoulos and Bates) and conformal language modelling. The report writer should treat these as leads only.
- The final adopted text and date of Annex 22 were not checked; all quotes are from the July 2025 consultation draft.
- GLiNER and GLiClass abstracts give no accuracy or throughput numbers.

## 4. Compute economics

### Takeaway
"Orders of magnitude less compute" holds for one to two orders in independent measurements (roughly 8x–40x cheaper, 3x–7x faster), and for two to three orders only in vendor figures or when comparing against multi-step LLM agent workflows. Small specialised models are not automatically the cheapest option either: TabPFN is about 1,000x slower per prediction than CatBoost.

### Cited Findings
- **SUPPORTS [PEER-REVIEWED]** "Power Hungry Processing: Watts Driving the Cost of AI Deployment?" (Luccioni, Jernite, Strubell; ACM FAccT 2024). 88 models, 10 tasks, 30 datasets, measured on A100 GPUs with CodeCarbon. Mean energy per 1,000 inferences: text classification 0.002 kWh, extractive QA 0.003 kWh, text generation 0.047 kWh, summarisation 0.049 kWh, image generation 2.907 kWh. On the same sentiment task a BERT-based classifier emitted 0.32 g CO2eq per 1,000 queries vs 2.66 g for Flan-T5-XL and 4.67 g for BLOOMz-7B (about 8x and 15x). The paper concludes multi-purpose generative models are "orders of magnitude more expensive than task-specific systems" even controlling for parameter count. — [arXiv 2311.16863](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.16863v3)
- **MIXED [VENDOR vs INDEPENDENT]** Jev: vendor claims 444.6x cheaper and 193.6x faster, and $0.0004 per case vs $0.0033 (OpenAI luna) to $0.1761 (Opus 5) for LLMs in workflow mode (8x–440x; 76x vs GPT-5.6 Terra at equal accuracy). - **MIXED [INDEPENDENT TEST]** The speed advantage of scoring over generation depends on option count: about 9.8x faster with 2 options and 5 questions, but slower (0.5x) at 77 options unless one-token codes are used. — [Designing with AI](https://newsletter.victordibia.com/p/how-jev-works-calibrated-decision)
- **MIXED [PEER-REVIEWED]** TabPFN: 2.8 s total vs 4 hours of tuning (about 5,140x), but per-sample inference about 0.2 s vs 0.0002 s for CatBoost at 10,000 rows, with memory linear in dataset size. TabPFN-2.5 adds distillation to MLP/trees specifically to fix deployment latency. — [PMC11711098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711098/); [arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667)
- **SUPPORTS [VENDOR]** TimesFM-3 is a 1.32 GB, 330M-parameter checkpoint that returns a full multivariate quantile forecast in one forward pass. — [DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/google-releases-timesfm-3); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/timesfm-3-a-zero-shot-foundation-model-for-multivariate-forecasting/)
- **[MEDIA/OPINION]** IBM's CEO is quoted saying open-weight models deliver about 90% of frontier performance at 10%–25% of per-token cost. — [Postdigitalist](https://postdigitalist.xyz/yc-request-for-startups/)
- **SUPPORTS (deployment) [OPINION, investor]** YC's sibling request "AI for the Air-Gapped Economy" (Brad Flora): "The most valuable data in the world isn't in the cloud"; customers want products that run fully on premises with no internet; this is "where the largest budgets and the most defensible companies are". "Own Your Intelligence" (Jon Xu) says the need to own models is most pressing in regulated sectors with locked-down infrastructure. — [YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

### Inferences
- The defensible claim for a pharma deck is "10x–40x cheaper and 3x–7x faster than a single LLM call, and 100x+ cheaper than an agentic LLM workflow, measured independently". The 200x–400x figures compare a one-pass model against multi-step harnesses and come from the vendor.
- In pharma the compute argument is less about cloud bills than about **where the model can run**. A 330M-parameter, ~1.3 GB model fits on a single on-prem GPU or CPU server inside a validated, network-isolated environment; a frontier LLM does not. That matters more than dollars per call for a plant that produces a few hundred batches a year.
- "Cheap enough to run on everything" in a plant means scoring every sensor window, every batch record step and every deviation continuously. At plant data volumes even 10x matters little financially; the binding constraints are validation, determinism and on-prem deployment.
- Counterpoint for the thesis: classical supervised models (PLS, gradient-boosted trees, logistic regression) are cheaper still. The compute comparison a customer will make is vertical model vs their existing chemometrics, not vs GPT.

### Gaps
- No published inference cost, latency or energy comparison between a time-series foundation model and an LLM (or LLM agent) on the same forecasting task was found this session.
- No measurement of small-model deployment in an actual GMP, air-gapped setting was found.
- TypeSafe's hardware and unit economics are undisclosed; the price may be subsidised by its own admission.

## 5. Verifiable rewards in pharma manufacturing (the crux)

### Takeaway
Pharma manufacturing has unusually trustworthy labels (validated assays, QA-approved dispositions) but very few of them: a high-volume tablet product yielded 1,005 release-tested batches in about 30 months, and well-controlled processes almost never fail, so the outcome label is nearly constant. The only abundant, immediately verifiable signal is the future sensor/spectral trajectory itself, plus simulator ground truth; every sparse human-adjudicated label (root cause, deviation class) is slow, confounded and only partly verifiable.

### Cited Findings
- **SUPPORTS [REGULATOR]** Annex 22 draft, section 5.3, effectively defines a verifiable label for GMP: test-data labelling "should be verified following a process that ensures a very high degree of correctness", for example "independent verification by multiple experts, validated equipment or laboratory tests". Section 5.6: generating test data or labels with generative AI "is not recommended". Section 6: test data must be independent of training, access-controlled and audit-trailed. — [Annex 22 draft PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)
- **MIXED [PEER-REVIEWED]** "Big data collection in pharmaceutical manufacturing and its use for product quality predictions" (Žagar, Lek Pharmaceuticals; Mihelič, University of Ljubljana; Scientific Data 9:99, 2022). 1,005 production batches of a cholesterol-lowering film-coated tablet (direct compression, four strengths, nine batch sizes), November 2018–April 2021. One row of laboratory results per batch: incoming raw-material quality, in-process controls (weight, thickness, diameter, hardness, yield), and final release tests (drug release, assay, impurities, residual solvents). Tablet-press time series logged every 10 s. The authors state Ppk above 1 is expected for drug release, i.e. the process is capable and failures are rare. No model results are reported. — [Scientific Data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01203-x)
- **MIXED [PEER-REVIEWED dataset]** IndPenSim benchmark data (Goldrick et al.; Mendeley Data v2, 2019; linked papers in Computers and Chemical Engineering 2019 and Journal of Biotechnology 193:70–82, 2015). 100 simulated 100,000-litre penicillin batches with simulated Raman: batches 1–30 recipe-driven, 31–60 operator-controlled, 61–90 advanced process control using Raman, 91–100 with faults. So this gives simulator ground truth for titre, labelled faults, and three control policies for the same process. — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/pdnjz7zz5x/2)
- **MIXED [dataset]** "Additional Tennessee Eastman Process Simulation Data for Anomaly Detection Evaluation" (Rieth, Amsel, Tran, Cook; Harvard Dataverse, 2017): 500 simulation runs per set, 20 labelled fault types, 52 process variables sampled every 3 minutes. Fault labels are exact because they are injected in simulation. — [Harvard Dataverse](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/6C3JR1)
- **MIXED [dataset]** SECOM (McCann, Johnston; UCI, 2008): 1,567 semiconductor units, 591 sensor features, 104 fails (6.6%). A real pass/fail disposition label with heavy imbalance and noise, the closest public analogue to batch disposition. — [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/179/secom)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** ether0, "Training a Scientific Reasoning Model for Chemistry" (Narayanan, Braza, Griffiths, Bou, Wellawatte, Caldas Ramos, Mitchener, Rodriques, White; FutureHouse; arXiv 2506.17238, 2025). Rewards are programmatic checks (RDKit validity, exact match, Tanimoto ≥ 0.7, purchasability via a Bloom filter), but several are **model-predicted oracles**, not experiments (solubility scored by KDESol; retrosynthesis validated by Molecular Transformer), which the authors list as a limitation. — [arXiv 2506.17238 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17238v2)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** RLCR shows that a proper scoring rule (Brier) can itself be the reward and yields calibration in-domain, but calibration degrades out of domain (ECE 0.03 to 0.21). — [arXiv 2507.16806 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** "Reinforcement Learning for Batch Bioprocess Optimization" (Petsagkourakis, Sandoval, Bradford, Zhang, del Rio Chanona; arXiv 1904.07292, 2019): a policy is first trained on a preliminary process model and then updated batch-to-batch from true-plant measurements, explicitly to handle plant-model mismatch; three simulated case studies compared with NMPC. — [arXiv 1904.07292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07292)
- **UNDERMINES [REGULATOR]** FDA, "Contract Manufacturing Arrangements for Drugs: Quality Agreements" (Nov 2016): the owner's quality unit decides "whether to approve or reject product for release" and is "ultimately responsible". Disposition is therefore a human, legally accountable decision that can depend on investigations and judgement, not a pure function of process data. — [FDA guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/86193/download)

### Inferences
The table below is my assessment. Rows are anchored to the sources above where noted; frequency and label-count figures without an anchor are reasoned estimates, **not sourced**, and should be checked with the pharma-modalities researcher's notes.

| Candidate reward | Verifiable? | Frequency / labels per year | Delay | Noise and confounding | Used as a learning signal in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Future sensor / spectral values (self-supervised) | Yes, fully and automatically | Effectively unlimited (10 s logging in the tablet dataset) | None | Sensor drift, controller actions confound "natural" dynamics | TimesFM-3, Chronos-2 pretraining; tablet and IndPenSim time series |
| CQA assay vs specification (release tests) | Yes (validated methods; Annex 22 names lab tests) | One vector per batch; ~400 per year for one high-volume tablet (1,005 in ~30 months) | Days to weeks (estimate) | Assay variability; composite sampling; near-constant "pass" | Žagar and Mihelič dataset (proposed, no results) |
| In-process control results | Yes | Several per batch | Minutes to hours | Operator adjustments in response to IPC break independence | Žagar and Mihelič dataset |
| Batch disposition (release / reject) | Yes as a record, but a human decision | One per batch; rejects are rare when Ppk > 1 | Days to weeks (estimate) | Extreme imbalance; depends on investigation outcome | SECOM analogue (6.6% fails) |
| Yield / titre | Yes | One per batch (plus trajectory) | End of batch | Raw-material and seed-train effects | IndPenSim; RL bioprocess papers |
| RTRT / soft-sensor agreement with lab assay | Yes (paired measurement) | Per sample; can be thousands per batch with PAT | Hours to days | Calibration transfer across instruments | IndPenSim simulated Raman |
| Deviation occurrence | Yes as a record | Tens to hundreds per site-year (estimate) | Immediate to days | Reporting culture changes the base rate | No public dataset found |
| Confirmed root cause | Only partly: an expert conclusion, often "most probable" | A fraction of deviations | Weeks (estimate) | Hindsight, template-driven closure | No public dataset found |
| OOS / OOT outcome | Yes for the result; invalidation is judgemental | Rare | Days | Lab-error vs process-error attribution | No public dataset found |
| Process capability (Cpk/Ppk) | Yes, but an aggregate over many batches | A handful per product-year | Months | Cannot assign credit to single decisions | Reported descriptively in the tablet dataset |
| Simulator ground truth | Yes with respect to the simulator only | Unlimited | None | Sim-to-real gap | IndPenSim, Tennessee Eastman, PC-Gym |

- The decisive asymmetry: **dense signals are verifiable but not decision-relevant by themselves; decision-relevant signals are verifiable but sparse or judgemental.** A credible vertical lab would pretrain on dense self-supervised and simulated signals, then calibrate decision heads on the few hundred validated outcome labels a product generates per year.
- Because good processes rarely fail, "predict release" is a near-constant target. More informative rewards are continuous: assay value within specification, forecast error against the realised trajectory, soft-sensor error against the paired lab result. All are proper-scoring-rule compatible, which is what RLCR-style and TimesFM-style training need.
- ether0 shows the trap to avoid: once a reward is a model's prediction rather than a measurement, "verifiable" quietly becomes "consistent with another model". In pharma the equivalent would be rewarding agreement with a soft sensor or a simulator instead of the validated assay.
- Annex 22's test-data rules (independent, access-controlled, expert- or lab-verified labels, no generative labels) mean the scarce verified labels are needed twice: for calibration and for a locked acceptance test. That halves what is available for learning.
- For the thesis: this is **supportive of "more data + harness is not enough"** (there is not more labelled data to be had, and an LLM judge cannot manufacture verified labels) but **also a warning for any vertical model**, which faces the same scarcity.

### Gaps
- No paper was found this session that trains a foundation-style model with batch disposition, deviation root cause, OOS outcome or Cpk as the reward. I could not search for one after the budget ran out, so absence here is weak evidence.
- Delay, deviation counts and biologics batch counts per year are uncited estimates. A typical commercial biologics product is commonly said to run tens of batches per year; I have no source confirmed this session.
- Real-time release testing papers (PAT/NIR/Raman agreement with lab assay), golden-batch monitoring literature and DoE counterfactual datasets were not verified this session.
- PharmaPy is confirmed to exist as an open simulator; gPROMS and BioSTEAM were not checked.

## 7. Data rights and pooling

### Takeaway
The only large-scale proof that competing pharma companies will co-train a model is MELLODDY, and it cost €18.6M over three years to deliver single-digit median gains on drug-discovery data, with nothing equivalent for manufacturing. In manufacturing the regulated product owner, not the software or equipment vendor, is accountable for the records, so a startup's data rights have to be negotiated customer by customer.

### Cited Findings
- **MIXED [PEER-REVIEWED]** "MELLODDY: Cross-pharma Federated Learning at Unprecedented Scale Unlocks Benefits in QSAR without Compromising Proprietary Information" (Heyndrickx … Ceulemans, 49 authors; J. Chem. Inf. Model. 64(7):2331–2344, 2024). Ten companies; more than 2.6 billion activity data points, 21 million molecules, 40,000 assays. Gains over each partner's own model (relative improvement of proximity to perfection): above 4% in AUC-PR and 2% in R² for most partners, with at least one partner above 12.5% and 4.8%; each partner's best 10% of models gained 20% or more. Gains saturate; a 2-fold increase in comparable, overlapping data is suggested as the minimum worth a model update. Obstacles: units and scales made regression hard to federate; cross-compound federation of safety panels did not materialise; federation "comes at non-negligible cost and nonzero risk". Privacy design: shared trunk only, private heads, shuffled ECFP fingerprints, secure aggregation, audited platform. — [PMC11005050](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005050/)
- **[Project record]** MELLODDY ran 1 June 2019–31 May 2022 with a total budget of €18,635,465 (€8M EU, €10M EFPIA). Partners: Amgen, Astellas, AstraZeneca, Bayer, Boehringer Ingelheim, GSK, Servier, Janssen, Merck KGaA, Novartis, plus Owkin (coordinator), Substra, Iktos, Kubermatic, Nvidia, KU Leuven and BME. — [IHI factsheet](https://www.ihi.europa.eu/projects-results/project-factsheets/melloddy)
- **UNDERMINES [REGULATOR]** FDA quality-agreements guidance (2016): the owner's quality unit is "ultimately responsible" for approving or rejecting product; the agreement "should define" each party's role in making and maintaining original records and how records are made available; both parties "should share relevant information". The guidance allocates responsibility and access by contract and does not grant any third party rights to reuse the data. — [FDA guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/86193/download)
- **MIXED [REGULATOR]** Annex 22 draft, section 2.2: model documentation must be "available and reviewed by the regulated user" whether the model is built in-house or "provided by a supplier or service provider". Section 6: test data must be kept from anyone involved in training. — [Annex 22 draft PDF](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)
- **UNDERMINES [MEDIA/OPINION]** Postdigitalist (16 Nov 2026): manufacturing and energy data sit with Siemens, GE Vernova and utilities, which can train their own models; "without that [exclusive data access], Google can ship an equivalent model for free". — [Postdigitalist](https://postdigitalist.xyz/yc-request-for-startups/)
- **UNDERMINES [VENDOR]** Even Google restricts its exemplar: TimesFM-3 weights are non-commercial. — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch)
- **SUPPORTS [PEER-REVIEWED]** Synthetic pretraining sidesteps data rights for the pretraining stage (TabPFN used no real data). — [PMC11711098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711098/)

### Inferences
- MELLODDY translated to manufacturing is harder, not easier: QSAR partners shared a common input space (molecular fingerprints) and overlapping assays. Manufacturing sites have different equipment, tags, recipes and units, and MELLODDY already found unit and scale mismatches damaging for regression. The analogue of a "shared trunk, private heads" design would need a common process representation first, which is itself the research problem.
- Documented obstacles to pooling, in order of likely severity: (1) the legally accountable owner controls the records and can refuse reuse; (2) CDMO batch data is governed by quality and supply agreements between two other parties; (3) heterogeneity of tags and units; (4) the validation regime needs per-customer locked test data; (5) cost and time (three years and €18.6M for ten willing partners).
- Realistic legitimate routes: per-customer licences for model improvement with contractual carve-outs, federated or on-prem training with a shared trunk, synthetic and simulator pretraining, and partnerships with data-holding incumbents. Each trades moat strength against sales friction.
- The RFS line "You'll need the rights to the data" is the requirement most likely to sink a pharma entrant. Commentators converge on the same point (Tottman argues a team that already holds data rights starts well ahead of one with only a better model idea).

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: BioPhorum data-sharing programmes; CDMO contract norms on who owns batch data; EMA or FDA statements specifically on cross-company data pooling; positions of Sartorius, Cytiva, Thermo Fisher, Siemens, Emerson, Körber (PAS-X) or AVEVA (PI) on customer data reuse; any federated-learning project on manufacturing data; post-MELLODDY consortia. These need a follow-up search.
- The FDA 2023 discussion paper on AI in drug manufacturing was downloaded but its text could not be extracted, so it is not cited.
- The FDA guidance does not use the word "ownership"; I found no regulator text assigning data ownership as such.

## 8. RL and reward-based training with domain-verifiable rewards

### Takeaway
In the 2025–2026 literature a "verifiable reward" is one computed programmatically from the output, with no human or model judge; vertical labs have built them from deterministic checkers, simulators and proper scoring rules. The best scientific example, ether0, is itself a fine-tuned 24B autoregressive LLM that beat frontier models on its trained tasks, which supports "vertical plus verifiable rewards" but cuts against "non-text representations are required".

### Cited Findings
- **[PREPRINT]** The term originates with "Tulu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training" (Lambert et al., Allen Institute for AI; arXiv 2411.15124, Nov 2024), which introduces "a novel method we call Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)" alongside SFT and DPO. — [arXiv 2411.15124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** ether0 (FutureHouse; arXiv 2506.17238): Mistral-Small-24B post-trained with RL on 640,730 experimentally grounded chemistry problems across 375 tasks, with no domain pretraining. Highest accuracy on all open-answer categories vs frontier and chemistry-specific models; on multiple choice 50.1% zero-shot vs 52.2% for o1. Data efficiency: reached 70% on reaction prediction after 46,000 examples, whereas Molecular Transformer got 64.1% from about 480,000 reactions and under 30% when retrained on about 60,000. Limitations: weak generalisation beyond the training distribution; RL degraded general instruction following; some verifiers are predictive models. — [arXiv 2506.17238 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17238v2)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** RLCR (MIT; arXiv 2507.16806): rewarding correctness plus Brier score gives calibration without accuracy loss in-domain, while plain RLVR worsens out-of-domain calibration (ECE 0.40 to 0.46). — [arXiv 2507.16806 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)
- **[VENDOR]** TypeSafe positions its unpublished RLCD as a third category after RLHF and RLVR, rewarding "epistemically honest probabilities". — [TypeSafe blog](https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev)
- **MIXED [PREPRINT]** RL for batch bioprocesses: train a recurrent policy on a preliminary model, then adapt batch-to-batch on the real plant; compared with NMPC on three simulated cases. — [arXiv 1904.07292](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07292)
- **UNDERMINES [PREPRINT]** PC-Gym benchmark: across CSTR, extraction and crystallisation environments, RL algorithms show performance gaps to NMPC oracles. — [arXiv 2410.22093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22093)
- **[PREPRINT]** "A Guide to Bayesian Optimization in Bioprocess Engineering" (Siska, Pajak, Rosenthal, del Rio Chanona, von Lieres, Helleckes; arXiv 2508.10642, Aug 2025): BO suits noisy, small-data experimental optimisation and has recently gained traction in bioprocessing but remains early-stage and needs extensions for biological uncertainty. BO is the incumbent for experiment selection. — [arXiv 2508.10642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10642)
- **[PREPRINT]** GLiClass adapts PPO to train a zero-shot classifier from sparse data or human feedback, a small-model precedent for reward-based training of typed-decision models. — [arXiv 2508.07662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07662)

### Inferences
- Three reward constructions recur and all map onto pharma: (a) deterministic checkers (specification limits, mass balance, alarm logic); (b) simulators (IndPenSim, PharmaPy, kinetic models); (c) proper scoring rules against later-realised measurements (forecast vs actual trajectory, predicted vs measured assay). Type (c) is the pharma-native one and is exactly what TimesFM-style quantile training and RLCR/RLCD-style calibration training use.
- ether0 is the most important **counter-example** to the strong form of the thesis: a general LLM backbone plus verifiable rewards beat both frontier LLMs and a purpose-built specialist with ten times less data. The lesson a sceptic will draw is "the moat is the reward and data, not the architecture".
- ether0 also carries the main **supporting** lesson: frontier general models did not match it without those rewards, and it generalised poorly outside its trained tasks, so vertical reward construction is real, defensible work.
- For closed-loop control, published evidence still favours model-based control (NMPC) over RL, and plant deployment needs batch-to-batch correction for model mismatch. A pharma startup claiming "control" should expect to be compared with MPC and BO, not with LLMs.
- In GMP, online RL on the plant is a "dynamic model" under Annex 22 and is excluded from critical use; reward-based training must happen offline, with a frozen model deployed under change control.

### Gaps

### Inferences
- YC's framing is narrower than the founder's thesis. YC asks for (1) a typed, probabilistic output interface, (2) zero/few-shot use at much lower compute, (3) verifiable rewards and data rights. It does **not** claim general LLMs fail, and its sibling request explicitly expects frontier models to stay in use for the hardest problems. Pitching "LLMs are not enough" overstates what the RFS says; pitching "an owned, cheap, calibrated decision model with verified rewards" matches it.
- The RFS's final bar is against "existing specialist models". In pharma that means multivariate statistical process control, PLS chemometrics, mechanistic and hybrid models and MPC, plus TimesFM-3/Chronos-2 zero-shot. A win over GPT-class models alone would not meet the stated bar.
- How the evidence in these notes splits on the thesis:
  - **Supports:** Annex 22's exclusion of LLMs from critical GMP use and its demand for deterministic output, confidence and attribution; native-numeric forecasting models leading benchmarks at 330M parameters; one to two orders of magnitude measured cost advantage; on-prem demand; scarcity of verified labels (more data is not available).
  - **Undermines:** ether0 (an LLM backbone won with verifiable rewards); Jev-style typed models are plausibly LLM-backed and take text input; logit-scored open LLMs reach similar calibration; supervised small classifiers beat zero-shot typed models when labels exist; BloombergGPT precedent; exemplar models come from hyperscalers and are free or licence-restricted; RL lags MPC in process control.
  - **Mixed:** calibration claims (hold in-domain, fail out of domain for everyone); MELLODDY (pooling works but is slow, costly and modest in gain).
