## 1. The bitter-lesson side: where general models overtook specialised ones, and what happened to vertical text-model bets

### Takeaway
Wherever the domain's raw material is text (medicine QA, finance NLP, law), domain-pretrained or domain-fine-tuned language models were matched or beaten by the next general frontier model within roughly 6 to 24 months, and the best-documented vertical text company (Harvey) publicly moved from a custom model to orchestrating general models. The counter-current is narrow: small task-specific models still win on cost-per-page tasks such as document parsing, and even there general VLMs lead on messy real-world inputs.

### Cited Findings

**Core argument**
- **[UNDERMINES | opinion]** "The Bitter Lesson", Rich Sutton, 13 March 2019, personal essay (incompleteideas.net). Claim: across 70 years of AI research, general methods that leverage computation (search and learning) ultimately beat approaches that build in human domain knowledge; researchers should build in meta-methods that can discover structure, not the structure itself. Examples in the essay are chess, Go, speech and vision. The canonical URL is `http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html` (fetch failed on a certificate error in this session; wording confirmed through mirrors). — [UT Austin course mirror](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~eunsol/courses/data/bitter_lesson.pdf)

**Medicine**
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, Microsoft/OpenAI authors]** "Can Generalist Foundation Models Outcompete Special-Purpose Tuning? Case Study in Medicine", Nori et al. (Microsoft Research; one OpenAI author), Nov 2023, arXiv 2311.16452. Setup: GPT-4 with "Medprompt" (a composition of general prompting strategies, no domain fine-tuning) versus specialist models such as Med-PaLM 2 on the nine MultiMedQA datasets. Result: state of the art on all nine; first score above 90% on MedQA (USMLE); 27% error-rate reduction versus the best prior specialist results; "an order of magnitude fewer calls to the model" than Med-PaLM 2. The prompting methods were also tested on electrical engineering, law and nursing exams. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16452)
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint]** "From Medprompt to o1: Exploration of Run-Time Strategies for Medical Challenge Problems and Beyond", Nori et al., Nov 2024, arXiv 2411.03590. Setup: o1-preview with no prompt engineering versus GPT-4 + Medprompt. Result: MedQA 96.0% (o1-preview) versus 90.2% (GPT-4 + Medprompt); few-shot prompting hurts o1; the authors say medical benchmarks are near saturation. Shows that even the harness (Medprompt) was absorbed by the next model generation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03590)
- **[UNDERMINES | peer-reviewed]** "Medical Adaptation of Large Language and Vision-Language Models: Are We Making Progress?", Jeong, Garg, Lipton, Oberst, EMNLP 2024 (oral), pp. 12143–12170. Setup: head-to-head of medical LLMs/VLMs versus their own general-domain base models with per-model prompt optimisation and statistical testing. Result: in the 3-shot setting medical LLMs beat their base models in only 12.1% of cases, tied in 49.8% and were significantly worse in 38.2%. The extended version (arXiv 2411.08870) finds task-specific fine-tuning can help, but gains did not transfer to clinical-note tasks. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.677/); [extended arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08870)

**Finance**
- **[SUPPORTS at the time, now historical | preprint, vendor authors]** "BloombergGPT: A Large Language Model for Finance", Wu et al. (Bloomberg), March 2023, arXiv 2303.17564. Setup: 50B-parameter model trained on 363B tokens of Bloomberg financial data plus 345B general tokens. Claim: outperforms existing (2023, open) models on financial tasks "by significant margins". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17564)

So a 50B bespoke model was overtaken by a general model within about two months of publication. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05862)

**Law (what happened to a company that bet on a vertical text model)**

I could not reconcile this timeline with the 2025 move in-session; it suggests the pendulum between custom and general models is still moving, driven partly by cost and dependency rather than accuracy. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/legal-tech-ai-firms-shift-away-from-anthropic-openai-reliance)

**Document parsing / OCR (general VLMs versus small specialised models)**
- **[SUPPORTS narrowly | preprint, vendor authors]** "PaddleOCR-VL: Boosting Multilingual Document Parsing via a 0.9B Ultra-Compact Vision-Language Model" (Baidu, Oct 2025, arXiv 2510.14528) and MinerU2.5 (Sept 2025, arXiv 2509.22186). Setup: sub-1B to small task-specific VLMs versus GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Qwen2.5-VL-72B on OmniDocBench. Claim: MinerU2.5 "consistently outperforms both general-purpose VLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, GPT-4o) and domain-specific models". Exact scores were not retrieved. — [PaddleOCR-VL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14528); [MinerU2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22186)
- **[MIXED | preprint, 2026]** "How Far Is Document Parsing from Solved? PureDocBench", Li et al., May 2026 (rev. Sept 2026), arXiv 2605.07492. Setup: 58 models (pipeline parsers, end-to-end parsers, general VLMs) on 1,475 pages in clean, digitally degraded and real-capture versions. Result: best three-track average 79.69/100; scores fall 11.61 points from clean to real. A specialist parser (TeleOCR) led on clean pages, while general models led on degraded and real inputs (GLM-5.3-Flash on digital, Gemini 3.6 Flash on real). Specialists win in-distribution; generalists are more robust off-distribution. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07492)

### Inferences
- The pattern in text domains is consistent across medicine, finance and law: domain pretraining buys a lead measured in months, and the harness around a general model (Medprompt) is itself absorbed by the next model. A pharma-manufacturing product whose differentiator is "an LLM fine-tuned on GMP text, batch records and deviations" sits squarely in the overtaken category.
- The surviving specialist advantage in text-adjacent work is cost and latency at volume (0.9B-parameter parsers), not peak accuracy on hard, messy cases. That matches the YC framing in section 5 (the bar is "orders of magnitude less inference compute").
- Harvey's trajectory is the clearest case study for a founder: the custom model was a real, measured advantage (97% attorney preference) and it still expired in under a year; the company's value moved to workflow, distribution and evaluation (BigLaw Bench).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm BloombergGPT's later fate (whether it was deployed, retired or replaced). No source found in-session.
- Code models: I did not retrieve a source in-session for the claim that code-specialised LLMs (Codex-era, CodeLlama, StarCoder) were overtaken by general frontier models on SWE-bench-style tasks. This is widely believed but unverified here.
- MedHELM (2025) and Med-Gemini comparisons were not retrieved.
- The date and substance of Harvey's "Tenet" model need a primary source.

## 2. The specialist side: where non-text structure made specialised models decisively better, and what predicts it

### Takeaway
Specialist models have won decisively, and stayed ahead, where the data is not language (3D coordinates, gridded fields, sensor/actuator streams, event codes), where there is a very large shared corpus or a trustworthy simulator, and where outputs must be continuous, calibrated or real-time. But two caveats cut against the "custom architecture" half of the thesis: within those domains the trend is toward generic transformers/diffusion with fewer hand-built priors, and in robotics and driving the winning design is a hybrid with a general VLM inside it.

### Cited Findings

**Structural biology and genomics**
- **[SUPPORTS on modality, UNDERMINES on hand-built priors | peer-reviewed]** "Accurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions with AlphaFold 3", Abramson et al. (Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs), Nature 630:493–500, May 2024. Setup: diffusion over raw atom coordinates with a simplified "pairformer" trunk; PoseBusters set of 428 protein–ligand structures, success = ligand RMSD < 2 Å. Result: significantly better than Vina (P = 2.27 × 10⁻¹³) and RoseTTAFold All-Atom (P = 4.45 × 10⁻²⁵) without structural inputs. Architecture note: the model has "no rotational or translational equivariance" and no stereochemical loss terms; MSA processing was cut back. Limitations stated by the authors: hallucinated structure in disordered regions, 4.4% chirality violations on PoseBusters, static structures only. Training used a PDB cutoff of 30 Sept 2021 plus cross-distillation from AlphaFold-Multimer predictions. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924)
- **[MIXED | peer-reviewed, TMLR]** "Does equivariance matter at scale?", Brehmer, Behrends, de Haan, Cohen, arXiv 2410.23179 (Oct 2024; TMLR 2025). Setup: equivariant versus plain transformers on a rigid-body benchmark, varying data, model size and compute. Result: equivariance improves data efficiency; non-equivariant models with augmentation can close the gap given enough epochs; but equivariant models outperform at every compute budget tested. Read: built-in symmetry matters most when data is scarce, which is the pharma-manufacturing regime. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179)
- **[SUPPORTS built-in symmetry | preprint, third-party vendor claim]** Pearl (arXiv 2510.24670) uses an SO(3)-equivariant diffusion module and reports 85.2% and 84.7% success on Runs N' Poses and PoseBusters, described as improvements over AlphaFold 3. A separate docking paper cautions that cross-method comparisons are confounded by training data, pocket definitions and post-processing. — [Pearl, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24670); [SigmaDock, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04854)
- **[SUPPORTS | vendor blog for a Science paper]** ESM3 (EvolutionaryScale), release post 25 June 2024. Setup: 98B-parameter generative protein model over sequence, structure and function tokens, trained on "billions of proteins" plus hundreds of millions of synthetic data points, >1×10²⁴ FLOPs. Result: generated esmGFP, a fluorescent protein 96 mutations (of 229 residues) from the nearest known one, 58% sequence similarity, framed as equivalent to "over 500 million years" of evolution. Note that it tokenises non-text biology rather than avoiding tokens. — [EvolutionaryScale](https://www.evolutionaryscale.ai/blog/esm3-release)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, 2026]** Evo 2 (Arc Institute), preprint Feb 2025, published in Nature, announced 4 March 2026. Setup: DNA language model at 7B and 40B parameters with context up to 1 million base pairs, trained on roughly 9.3 trillion base pairs from about 128,000 genomes (one summary gives ~8.8 trillion for OpenGenome2). Results from the one-year update: 16 of 285 designed bacteriophage genomes propagated and inhibited target strains; designed chromatin-accessibility patterns matched predictions with AUROC 0.92–0.95; an external farm-animal variant study reported AUROC 0.921. Adoption: >200 citations, ~88,000 GitHub downloads. — [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/news/evo-2-one-year-later); [GenomeWeb on data scale](https://www.genomeweb.com/node/635106)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, 2026]** "Advancing regulatory variant effect prediction with AlphaGenome", Avsec et al. (Google DeepMind), Nature 649:1206–1218, 28 Jan 2026. Setup: 1 Mb DNA input, thousands of functional genomic tracks at base-pair resolution. Result: matched or exceeded the strongest external models in 25 of 26 variant-effect evaluations. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41606153/)

**Weather and Earth system**
- **[SUPPORTS | preprint confirmed; widely cited journal version not confirmed in-session]** "GraphCast: Learning skillful medium-range global weather forecasting", Lam et al. - **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** GenCast (Google DeepMind), Nature 2024 (arXiv 2312.15796). Setup: diffusion ensemble model trained on 40 years of ERA5 (1979–2018). Result: beats ECMWF ENS on 97.4% of 1,320 targets (v1 abstract said >96%). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15796v2)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** "A foundation model for the Earth system" (Aurora), Bodnar et al. (Microsoft Research), Nature 641:1180–1187, 29 May 2025. **Materials and atomistic simulation**
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** "Scaling deep learning for materials discovery" (GNoME), Merchant et al. (Google DeepMind), Nature, 29 Nov 2023. Setup: graph networks trained through large-scale active learning with DFT in the loop. Result: 2.2 million new crystal structures, 381,000 newly stable; 736 independently synthesised by external labs; stability-prediction hit rate raised to above 80%. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06735-9); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/millions-of-new-materials-discovered-with-deep-learning/)

- **[SUPPORTS | preprint]** "LLM4Mat-Bench: Benchmarking Large Language Models for Materials Property Prediction" (arXiv 2411.00177, 2024). ### Inferences
Features that predict a durable specialist win, read across the cases above:
1. **Non-text native modality with structure text serialisation destroys** (3D coordinates, gridded fields, long nucleotide strings, multi-rate sensor streams). All decisive wins are here.
2. **A very large shared corpus or a trustworthy simulator** (PDB, ERA5, 128k genomes, DFT, FGE). Without one of these there is no specialist foundation model, only per-site models (Imubit).
3. **Continuous, calibrated or real-time outputs** (flow-matching actions, ensemble forecasts, control setpoints). This is where even LLM-centric systems bolt on a non-token head.
4. **Verifiable ground truth** (crystal structure, tomorrow's weather, plant yield), which lets specialists be trained and benchmarked objectively.
5. **Safety and latency constraints**, which push toward distilled, deterministic, certifiable controllers (Waymo students, Imubit) rather than a large model in the loop.

Counter-observations a founder should weigh:
- Inside specialist domains the architecture is getting more generic, not more bespoke (AlphaFold 3 dropped equivariance; ESM3 and Evo 2 are token models; CoMET is a plain decoder-only transformer over event tokens). The durable asset is the data and the tokenisation/representation choice, not exotic architecture. Brehmer et al. is the exception that matters for small-data regimes.
- "Native representation" in practice usually still means tokens, just not word tokens. The defensible claim is "domain-native tokenisation and output heads", not "non-token".
- In EHR, the closest analogue to batch-record event data, a general LLM encoder reading text-serialised records is within noise of a dedicated model in-distribution and better across sites. For a product sold to many manufacturers with different historians, MES schemas and naming conventions, portability may matter more than in-distribution accuracy.
- Robotics and driving show the winning pattern is a hybrid in which the general VLM is retained for semantics and a specialist head handles continuous output.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or preprint evidence found in-session for a foundation model over pharmaceutical manufacturing process data (bioreactor or downstream time series, batch records) with published benchmarks. The only pharma-manufacturing-specific "foundation model" claim found is a vendor launch post with no numbers (see section 5).
- Chip design (AlphaChip and its Nature addendum) was not retrieved; unverified here.
- ETHOS quantitative results, Foresight, and head-to-head CoMET-versus-LLM comparisons were not retrieved.
- π0 training-data hours (commonly quoted as ~10,000 hours) could not be confirmed; the Physical Intelligence blog returned HTTP 429.
- AlphaFold 3 headline PoseBusters percentages are in a figure and were not extracted.

## 3. Hybrid architectures as a middle path: concrete designs and results

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced designs keep a general language model for semantics, planning and explanation and add (a) a learned encoder/adapter for the non-text modality, (b) a continuous output head, or (c) tool calls into numeric models and simulators with a deterministic validation gate. Bolting a frozen LLM onto raw numeric series for forecasting has been shown not to help, so the split of labour matters more than the presence of an LLM.

**(d) Text-to-text regression (a "tokenise everything" hybrid that works on industrial systems)**
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, Google]** "Performance Prediction for Large Systems via Text-to-Text Regression", Akhauri et al., June 2025, arXiv 2506.21718. Setup: a 60M-parameter encoder-decoder trained from scratch reads raw configuration files and system logs as text and emits the numeric outcome as text, predicting resource efficiency on Google's Borg cluster. Result: up to 0.99 rank correlation (0.9 average) across the fleet and 100x lower MSE than tabular approaches; adapts to new tasks with 500 examples; captures outcome densities. Notable: it is a small specialised model trained on proprietary data, but with a pure text interface, so it undermines "non-text tokens are required" while supporting "train your own model on your own operational data". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21718)

### Inferences
- Split of labour that the evidence supports: numeric/continuous modelling by a purpose-trained model or simulator; semantics, context integration, planning and explanation by a general LLM; a deterministic validation layer before anything consequential. This is the Waymo, π0, Biomni and fault-tolerant-control pattern.
- Where hybrids fail: feeding raw numbers through a frozen LLM and expecting better forecasts (Tan et al.), and giving an agent tools for problems the base model can already reason through (ChemToolAgent).
- Synthetic paired data (ChatTS) and auto-captioning of sensor data (SensorLM) are the two documented ways to get modality-language alignment without labelled text. Both are feasible for process data, where events, alarms and batch-record entries are natural captions for sensor windows.
- For a pharma-manufacturing product the hybrid suggests the defensible engineering is in the modality encoder, the digital twin/mechanistic model, the ontology and the validation gate. The LLM itself is replaceable, which is a feature for adoption and a problem for a "model moat" claim.

### Gaps
- Coscientist (Boiko et al., Nature 2023), Health-LLM, and neurosymbolic or knowledge-graph-retrieval comparisons were not retrieved in-session. The fault-tolerant-control paper is the only Graph-RAG-over-plant-ontology evidence found, and it reports no baseline numbers in its abstract.
- No head-to-head was found comparing the three hybrid families (adapter, output head, tool-calling) on the same industrial task.

## 4. Direct head-to-heads between general LLM agents and specialised models, 2024–2026: has the gap closed?

### Takeaway
The gap has closed or reversed on tasks where the deciding information is textual or contextual, and on knowledge and reasoning benchmarks; it has not closed on pure numeric prediction, subtle anomaly detection, closed-loop regulation or industrial visual inspection. I found no rigorous public benchmark that pits 2026 frontier reasoning models with code execution against purpose-built models on bioprocess or pharma-manufacturing tasks.

### Cited Findings

**Forecasting**
- **[UNDERMINES | peer-reviewed, ICML 2025]** "Context is Key: A Benchmark for Forecasting with Essential Textual Information", Williams et al. (ServiceNow Research, Mila), arXiv 2410.18959. Setup: 71 hand-built tasks in seven domains where text context is required; statistical models, time-series foundation models and LLM forecasters compared on a region-of-interest CRPS (lower is better). Result: Direct Prompt with Llama-3.1-405B scores 0.159; GPT-4o 0.274; Chronos-Large 0.326; Lag-Llama 0.327; TimeGEN 0.353; Moirai-Large 0.520; ARIMA 0.475. Caveats in the paper: without context, LLM forecasters no longer dominate; LLMs produce occasional "significant failures" (errors of at least 500% of range); they run orders of magnitude slower; several are Pareto-dominated by Chronos and Lag-Llama on size versus accuracy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18959v4)
- **[UNDERMINES at the time | peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2023]** "Large Language Models Are Zero-Shot Time Series Forecasters", Gruver, Finzi, Qiu, Wilson, arXiv 2310.07820. Setup: series encoded as digit strings. Result: zero-shot forecasts comparable to or better than purpose-built models; but GPT-4 can be worse than GPT-3 because of number tokenisation, and calibration is poor. An explicit "tokenisation is the bottleneck" data point. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07820)
- **[SUPPORTS | preprint, Amazon]** "Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting", Ansari et al., Oct 2025, arXiv 2510.15821. Setup: pretrained model handling univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting zero-shot via group attention; multivariate/covariate behaviour learned from synthetic data. Claim: state of the art on fev-bench, GIFT-Eval and Chronos Benchmark II, with the largest margins on covariate-informed tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, Energy and AI 2026]** "Systematic Evaluation of TabPFN-TS and Chronos-2 for Zero-Shot Heat Load Forecasting in District Heating Networks", Spoek et al., arXiv 2608.20024. Setup: two German district-heating networks, full-year benchmark, zero-shot specialists versus trained baselines. Result: CVRMSE 12.48% (Chronos-2) and 13.07% (TabPFN-TS); both beat all trained baselines; a 12-week context with ambient temperature was enough. An industrial-utility example of small synthetic-pretrained specialists working with no site-specific training. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20024)
- **[MIXED | NeurIPS 2026 listing]** "What a Forecasting Leaderboard Can Establish: Resolution Reporting for Zero-Shot Time-Series Models". Finding: on GIFT-Eval (97 configurations from 28 datasets) no neighbouring pair in the top ten separates statistically. Read: specialist time-series models are converging among themselves; leaderboard position is not a moat. — [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175015)

**Anomaly detection**
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025]** "Can LLMs Understand Time Series Anomalies?", Zhou and Yu, arXiv 2410.05440. Result: LLMs detect trivial synthetic anomalies but there is no evidence they recognise subtle real-world ones; they do better when the series is given as an image than as text tokens; chain-of-thought prompting does not help and sometimes hurts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05440)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025]** "MMAD: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models in Industrial Anomaly Detection", Jiang et al., arXiv 2410.09453. **Chemistry, materials, biology**
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, April 2024, rev. Nov 2024; journal version not confirmed in-session]** "Are large language models superhuman chemists?" (ChemBench), Mirza et al., arXiv 2404.01475. Setup: >2,700 question-answer pairs, LLMs versus human chemists. **Process control**
- **[MIXED | preprints, 2025–2026]** LLM agents versus PID/MPC: GPT-4o-class agents reach 100% valid recovery paths within five reprompts on 180 random finite-state machines and perform "similarly to PID" on a two-heater lab rig (Vyas and Mercangöz, arXiv 2507.07115); an LLM supervisor beats tuned linear MPC on off-nominal target acquisition but is 10x or more worse on disturbance rejection unless gated (Rosenthal, arXiv 2607.27849, section 3). — [arXiv 2507.07115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07115); [arXiv 2607.27849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27849)
- **[SUPPORTS | preprint]** "Optimizing Operation Recipes with Reinforcement Learning for Safe and Interpretable Control of Chemical Processes", arXiv 2511.16297. Purpose-built optimisation remains the reference for batch control. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16297)

**Structured records and small tabular data**
- **[UNDERMINES / MIXED]** Hegselmann et al. and Gao et al. (section 2): general LLM encoders match a dedicated EHR model in-distribution and beat it across sites; simple count-based models stay competitive. — [arXiv 2502.17403](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17403v4); [arXiv 2511.00782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00782)

### Inferences
- A workable rule from these results: **if the answer depends on context that exists as language (campaign notes, deviations, change controls, SOPs, operator comments), a general LLM wins; if it depends on fine numeric structure in the signal, a purpose-trained model wins; the best systems give each its part.** Context is Key is the cleanest demonstration, since the same LLMs that win with context lose their edge without it.
- "Has the gap closed with reasoning models and code execution?" On knowledge and reasoning, yes (o1 over Medprompt; ChemBench). On numeric prediction the published evidence I could confirm mostly tests 2024-era models, so the honest answer is "not shown". A general agent with code execution can also simply call Chronos-2 or TabPFN, which converts the specialist into a commodity tool unless it is trained on data nobody else has.
- ether0 and Periodic Neon define the strongest current "vertical model" recipe: take an open general LLM, post-train with RL on verifiable domain rewards from proprietary experiments. That matches YC's stated hard part ("high-quality, vertical-specific, verifiable rewards") and is a data/reward moat, not an architecture moat.
- Reliability asymmetry matters for GMP settings: LLM forecasters show rare catastrophic failures and poor calibration, and LLM supervisors show "specification abandonment". A validated, bounded specialist plus a gate is easier to qualify than an open-ended agent.

### Gaps
- No benchmark found comparing o3/GPT-5-class or 2026 frontier models with tools against specialist models on bioprocess forecasting, soft sensing, batch anomaly detection or process optimisation. This is the single most decision-relevant missing experiment and the founder could run it.
- GIFT-Eval/fev-bench leaderboards were not checked live; I found no result for LLM agents on them.
- TxGemma and other therapeutics generalist-versus-specialist results were not retrieved.
- Exact OmniDocBench and MVTec comparisons for 2026 frontier VLMs were not retrieved.

### Takeaway
Investor writing in 2024–2026 is close to unanimous that models differentiate but do not defend; moats are placed in workflow ownership, system-of-record position, proprietary or hard-to-get data, and trust. ### Inferences
- There is a split between where money goes (billions into model-centric physical-AI and AI-for-science companies) and what investor essays say defends a business (workflow, system of record, data access, trust). The two are reconciled by noticing that the heavily funded model companies also own the data-generation asset: robots (Physical Intelligence), automated labs (Periodic, Lila), a clinical-records network (Epic). The model is the product of the moat rather than the moat.
- For pharma manufacturing, the data-generation asset belongs to the manufacturers. A startup's durable position therefore depends on data rights and aggregation (contracts, federated arrangements), validated integration into GMP systems of record, and accumulated evaluation evidence. This matches the governing product direction's emphasis on provenance, existing systems of record and not claiming validated results without evidence.
- "Regulatory validation as a moat" appears in investor writing only as "trust". I found no essay or data that quantifies validation (CSV/CSA, GAMP 5, Annex 11/22-style expectations) as a barrier to entry; treat it as a plausible but unevidenced moat.
- Orbital's pivot and Harvey's shift both show model-first companies migrating toward whatever layer captures value (products, workflow) when the model lead narrows.
- The pharma-manufacturing field is early and open: the one company claiming a biomanufacturing foundation model has published no benchmark, and the best-traction entrant found is an integration/agent product on general models.

## 6. Data-scale reality check, and how teams bootstrapped when data was scarce

### Takeaway
Every successful vertical foundation model sat on either a very large pooled corpus (decades of reanalysis, a hundred thousand genomes, a hundred million patients, a million robot trajectories) or a simulator that could manufacture data. Where neither existed, teams used synthetic priors, self-supervision on unlabelled streams with auto-generated captions, pretrain-then-fine-tune, or cross-company federated learning, and the federated pharma precedent delivered real but modest gains.

### Cited Findings

**How much data the winners had**
- **Weather:** GenCast trained on 40 years of ERA5 (1979–2018); Aurora pretrained on more than a million hours of Earth-system data, 1.3B parameters. — [GenCast](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15796v2); [Aurora](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13063v3)
- **Structural biology:** AlphaFold 3 trained on PDB structures to a 30 Sept 2021 cutoff, augmented by cross-distillation from AlphaFold-Multimer predictions (self-generated data) to reduce hallucination. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924)
- **Proteins:** ESM3: "billions of proteins" plus "hundreds of millions of synthetic data points"; 98B parameters; >10²⁴ FLOPs. — [EvolutionaryScale](https://www.evolutionaryscale.ai/blog/esm3-release)
- **Genomics:** Evo 2: ~9.3 trillion base pairs from ~128,000 genomes (alternative figure ~8.8 trillion). — [GenomeWeb](https://www.genomeweb.com/node/635106)
- **Materials:** MatterGen: ~608,000 stable structures; UMA: ~0.5 billion unique atomic structures (>30 billion atoms), all DFT-computed, i.e. simulator-generated. — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/mattergen-a-new-paradigm-of-materials-design-with-generative-ai/); [UMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23971)
- **Robotics:** Open X-Embodiment: 1M+ trajectories from 22 embodiments, pooled from 60 datasets across 34 labs (21 institutions per the paper), 527 skills. RT-1-X trained on the pooled data outperformed the original per-lab model by 50% on average. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08864); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/scaling-up-learning-across-many-different-robot-types/)
- **Driving:** Waymo: "well over 100 million fully autonomous miles", plus a learned simulator and critic. — [Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2025/12/demonstrably-safe-ai-for-autonomous-driving)
- **EHR:** CoMET: 118M patients, 115B events (only possible because Epic Cosmos pools 310 health systems); MOTOR: up to 55M patients, 9B events. — [CoMET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12104); [MOTOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03150)
- **Wearables:** SensorLM: 59.7M hours from 103,000+ people, with captions generated automatically. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09108)
- **Physical sensors:** Newton: 0.59B measurements from open datasets. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14724v1)

**Bootstrapping when data is scarce**
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed, Nature 2025]** Synthetic priors: "Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model" (TabPFN v2), Hollmann et al., Nature 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6. The model "trains on synthetic datasets and predicts on unseen real-world datasets in a single forward pass"; it is designed for small data (original limits in the thousands of rows; current releases accept far more). This is the most relevant existence proof for a domain where each customer has tens to hundreds of batches. — [PriorLabs repository](https://github.com/PriorLabs/TabPFN)
- **[SUPPORTS | preprints]** Synthetic time-series pretraining: Chronos-2 learns multivariate and covariate behaviour from synthetic data imposed on univariate generators; TempoPFN is pretrained exclusively on synthetic data and reports competitive zero-shot results on GIFT-Eval and fev-bench; a 2025 survey catalogues synthetic-data use across time-series foundation models (TimesFM mixed real and synthetic; original Chronos used Gaussian-process "KernelSynth"). — [Chronos-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821); [TempoPFN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25502); [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11411)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** Simulation: tokamak controllers trained entirely in the FGE simulator then deployed on hardware (Degrave et al.); Imubit trains RL controllers in simulation against per-plant learned process models (vendor). — [Nature](https://www.doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04301-9); [Imubit](https://imubit.com/)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** Synthetic paired data for language alignment: ChatTS trains time-series/LLM alignment on synthetic pairs because real paired data is scarce. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03104)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** Pretrain broadly, fine-tune narrowly: Aurora fine-tunes a general Earth-system model to air quality, waves and cyclones where task data is limited. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13063v3)
- **[SUPPORTS | preprint]** Built-in symmetry as a substitute for data: equivariant models are more data-efficient (Brehmer et al.). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23179)
- **[MIXED | peer-reviewed]** Federated pharma consortium: "MELLODDY: Cross-pharma Federated Learning at Unprecedented Scale Unlocks Benefits in QSAR without Compromising Proprietary Information", Heyndrickx et al., Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling, 2023. Setup: ten pharma companies (Amgen, Astellas, AstraZeneca, Bayer, Boehringer Ingelheim, GSK, Janssen, Merck KGaA, Novartis, Servier) jointly trained multitask models over ~2.6 billion data points, 21M+ compounds and 40k+ assays without sharing data. Result: every partner saw aggregate improvements and a wider applicability domain, with the clearest gains in pharmacokinetics and safety-panel assays; an interim assessment reported a median 5.5% improvement in a conformal-efficiency metric for federated over single-partner models (up to 9.7%). Real but single-digit-percent gains from a three-year, ten-company effort. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11005050/); [IHI summary](https://www.ihi.europa.eu/news-events/newsroom/federated-learning-model-allows-competitors-pool-data-identify-promising)
- **[SUPPORTS | vendor claim]** Proprietary experiment generation: Periodic Labs' stated thesis is that "experiments from our high-throughput labs provide the data to train increasingly capable scientific AI", while noting physical data is slow, not elastically scalable and often ambiguous. — [Periodic Labs](https://periodic.com/news/building-labs-that-learn)

### Inferences
- Pharma manufacturing lacks both prerequisites in their usual form: there is no public corpus of process data, and each manufacturer's dataset is small (batches per product per site are counted in tens to low hundreds) and heterogeneous. A cross-customer foundation model in the AlphaFold/GraphCast sense is therefore not currently buildable from pooled real data by a startup.
- The realistic bootstrapping stack, each element with a documented precedent: (1) synthetic priors from mechanistic and hybrid bioprocess models (TabPFN/Chronos-2 pattern; the domain has decades of first-principles kinetic and mass-balance models to sample from); (2) self-supervision on unlabelled historian streams with events, alarms and batch-record entries as automatic captions (SensorLM pattern); (3) zero-shot generic time-series and tabular specialists as a baseline that needs no customer training data (district-heating result); (4) per-site fine-tuning or in-context adaptation; (5) federated or consortium learning later (MELLODDY), expecting modest uplift and long timelines.
- CoMET shows who can build the pooled model: the party that already operates the system of record across hundreds of sites (Epic). In manufacturing the analogous positions are held by MES/historian/automation vendors and large CDMOs. For a startup, becoming a trusted aggregation point is the precondition for a data moat, which again points to workflow and integration first.
- Simulator quality is the other lever: tokamak and Imubit show that a credible process model plus RL yields deployable controllers without large pooled data. A validated library of mechanistic/hybrid unit-operation models is plausibly a more defensible asset than a neural architecture.

### Gaps
- Total PDB structure count (commonly cited as around 200,000+) was not confirmed in-session; the RCSB statistics page did not return numbers.
- The MELLODDY final-paper headline effect size should be checked against the full text (DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00799); the 5.5% figure is from an interim assessment surfaced in search.
- The AI Structural Biology (AISB) consortium / OpenFold3 federated fine-tuning on pharma-proprietary structures was not retrieved; unverified here.
- No published federated-learning or consortium effort on pharmaceutical manufacturing process data was found.
- No source quantifying typical batch counts per product/site; that statement in Inferences is domain judgement, not a cited fact.

## 7. "Tokenisation is the bottleneck" / "not everything is language": published arguments and counter-arguments

### Takeaway
The "language is not enough" camp (LeCun, Fei-Fei Li, quantitative-model vendors) argues that text is a narrow, ungrounded channel and that physical domains need different representations and objectives; the "everything can be tokenised" camp points to working unified-token systems (Gato, RT-2, Chameleon, text-to-text regression) and to evidence that representations converge across modalities. In practice the dispute is narrower than the rhetoric: almost all working specialist models still use tokens and transformers; what differs is what is tokenised, what is predicted, and whether outputs are discrete or continuous.

### Cited Findings

**"Not everything is language"**
- **[SUPPORTS | opinion]** "From Words to Worlds: Spatial Intelligence is AI's Next Frontier", Fei-Fei Li, Substack, 10 Nov 2025. Claims: LLMs are "wordsmiths in the dark; eloquent but inexperienced, knowledgeable but ungrounded"; current models "rarely perform better than chance on estimating distance, orientation, and size". On tokenisation specifically: current models "tokenize data into 1D or 2D sequences", making simple spatial tasks unnecessarily hard; she calls for 3D/4D-aware tokenisation, context and memory, and says a universal task function "as simple and elegant as next-token prediction" for world models is still missing. — [Substack](https://drfeifei.substack.com/p/from-words-to-worlds-spatial-intelligence)
- **[SUPPORTS | press on opinion + funding, 2026]** Yann LeCun / AMI Labs: argues LLMs learn only from text, a low-bandwidth representation of human knowledge, and that prediction should happen in latent space (JEPA) rather than token or pixel space; AMI raised ~$1.03B at a $3.5B valuation (March 2026). — [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/yann-lecuns-ami-raises-1bn-seed-round-is-the-world-model-era-finally-here); [heise](https://heise.de/-11206350)
- **[SUPPORTS | vendor framing]** SandboxAQ "Large Quantitative Models": LQMs "leverage numerical data and equations" for industries where language models fall short; the CEO argues most of the economy "is actually not based on language"; reported $5.6B valuation after a $300M round (single article; coverage is largely promotional and I found no independent critical analysis). — [AIM Research](https://aimresearch.co/?p=13048)
- **[SUPPORTS | vendor blog]** Waymo assigns the VLM a limited role (semantic reasoning for rare scenes) and relies on a separate fast sensor-fusion encoder and an onboard validation layer for real-time driving. — [Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2025/12/demonstrably-safe-ai-for-autonomous-driving)
- **[SUPPORTS | peer-reviewed]** Empirical tokenisation failures: GPT-4 forecast worse than GPT-3 because of how it tokenises numbers (Gruver et al., NeurIPS 2023); LLMs detect time-series anomalies better from images than from text tokens (Zhou and Yu, ICLR 2025); language pretraining adds nothing to numeric forecasting (Tan et al., NeurIPS 2024). — [Gruver](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07820); [Zhou and Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05440); [Tan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- **[SUPPORTS | vendor blog]** DeepMind frames world models (Genie 3: real-time 720p at 24 fps, consistency for a few minutes, announced 5 Aug 2025) as "a key stepping stone" to AGI because they can train embodied agents in unlimited simulated environments. — [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)

**"Everything can be tokenised"**
- **[UNDERMINES | DeepMind paper, 2022, arXiv 2205.06175]** "A Generalist Agent" (Gato), Reed et al. (DeepMind). One ~1.2B-parameter transformer with a single set of weights trained on 604 tasks, with text, images, proprioception and actions all "serialized into a flat sequence of tokens". — [DeepMind](https://www.deepmind.com/blog/a-generalist-agent); [arXiv](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2205.06175v3.pdf)
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint]** RT-2: robot actions emitted as text tokens by a co-fine-tuned VLM generalise better than robot-only models (6,000 trials). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818)
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, Meta FAIR]** "Chameleon: Mixed-Modal Early-Fusion Foundation Models", May 2024, arXiv 2405.09818. All modalities as tokens in one sequence from the start; state of the art in image captioning and competitive with Gemini Pro/GPT-4V on mixed-modal generation, with acknowledged training-stability challenges. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818)
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, MIT, May 2024]** "The Platonic Representation Hypothesis", Huh, Cheung, Wang, Isola, arXiv 2405.07987. Argues that models trained on different modalities and objectives converge toward a shared statistical model of reality as scale and task diversity grow. If true, a large enough general model eventually acquires the representation a specialist hand-builds. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07987v4)
- **[UNDERMINES | preprint, Google]** Text-to-text regression on Borg (section 3): raw logs and configs as text in, numbers as text out, 100x lower MSE than tabular baselines. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21718)
- **[UNDERMINES | peer-reviewed / vendor]** Specialist models that are themselves token models: ESM3 (sequence, structure and function tokens), Evo 2 (nucleotide tokens), CoMET (medical-event tokens), ETHOS (tokenised health timelines). — [ESM3](https://www.evolutionaryscale.ai/blog/esm3-release); [CoMET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12104); [ETHOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21124)

**Middle position**
- **[MIXED | peer-reviewed / preprint]** Robotics moved from tokenised actions (RT-2) to continuous flow-matching/diffusion heads on a VLM (π0, GR00T N1): the language backbone stayed, the discrete output did not. — [π0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164); [GR00T N1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14734)
- **[MIXED | peer-reviewed]** AlphaFold 3 operates on raw atom coordinates with a diffusion head (not word tokens), yet dropped equivariance and other hand-built geometric priors. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11168924)

### Inferences
- The defensible technical statement is not "tokens are the bottleneck" but "word-piece tokenisation of numbers and cross-entropy over a text vocabulary are poor fits for dense multi-rate signals, calibrated uncertainty and continuous control". The remedies in the literature are domain-native tokenisers/patch encoders and continuous output heads, both of which can be attached to a general model.
- If the Platonic Representation Hypothesis holds, representation advantage decays with general-model scale unless the specialist trains on data the general model will never see. For pharma manufacturing that data is private process history, so the representation argument collapses back into a data-access argument.
- The strongest form of the opposing (bitter-lesson) argument, assembled from this section and section 1: (1) every text-domain specialist was overtaken within two years; (2) even in physical domains, bespoke priors are being removed as scale grows; (3) unified token models already handle actions, images, logs and numbers; (4) general models with tools can call any open specialist; (5) therefore any advantage that is purely architectural or representational is temporary, and durable advantage must come from proprietary data, verifiable-reward environments, integration and trust.
- The strongest form of the thesis that survives this: in a domain with small, private, heterogeneous, multi-rate numeric data, strict safety and validation requirements, and no public corpus, a general lab cannot train the capability in, so a company that secures data rights, builds simulators/synthetic priors and accumulates validation evidence can hold a lead. That is a moat made of data, environments and qualification, expressed through a specialist model, and it should be designed as a hybrid so the language layer can be swapped as general models improve.

### Gaps
- No direct statement by Demis Hassabis on world models versus LLMs, or on AlphaFold "not being a language model", was retrieved in-session.
- LeCun's primary position paper and exact "low bandwidth" wording were not fetched; the claim is reported via secondary coverage.
- SandboxAQ's own LQM page could not be fetched (404 on the URL tried); only secondary, promotional coverage was found.
- No peer-reviewed study was found that isolates tokenisation (as opposed to data or objective) as the causal bottleneck on industrial process data.
