## 1. Architectures and representation choices: how each model encodes continuous signals, and the tokens-vs-patches-vs-flow debate

### Takeaway
The field has converged, between 2024 and late 2026, on **instance-normalised continuous patches fed to a transformer (or xLSTM) with a quantile-regression head**; the two early alternatives — discretised value tokens (Chronos v1, TOTEM) and LLM re-use — have been largely abandoned by their own authors, and generative flow/diffusion heads (Sundial) have not won the leaderboards. Tabular prior-data-fitted networks (TabPFN family), pretrained purely on synthetic data, are a distinct second lineage that is unusually strong in the small-data regime.

### Cited Findings

**Discrete value tokens**

- **Chronos** — "Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series", Ansari et al., 2024, TMLR. Scales each series and quantises values into a fixed vocabulary, then trains off-the-shelf T5 models (20M–710M parameters) with cross-entropy on public data plus Gaussian-process synthetic data. A secondary source describes mean scaling plus uniform binning into a 4,094-bin vocabulary with clipping to [-15, 15]; I did not confirm the bin count in the primary paper. [fetched via search summary] MIXED (numeric-native but uses an LLM-style discrete vocabulary). — [arXiv 2403.07815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)
- **TOTEM** — "TOTEM: TOkenized Time Series EMbeddings for General Time Series Analysis", Talukder, Yue, Gkioxari, 2024, TMLR. Learns a discrete tokeniser by self-supervision and reuses it across tasks; evaluated on imputation (12 datasets, 17 baselines), anomaly detection (25 datasets, 19 baselines) and forecasting (12 datasets, 14 baselines), "nearly 500 experiments"; claims to match or beat SOTA in specialist and generalist settings. The abstract does not name VQ-VAE (that detail is [memory]). [fetched] PEER-REVIEWED. — [arXiv 2402.16412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16412)

**Continuous patches + quantile / parametric heads (now the dominant design)**

- **Chronos-Bolt** — Amazon model card, 2024. Same team abandons value tokens: context is split into patches, T5 encoder-decoder, decoder emits multi-step quantiles directly. Sizes 9M / 21M / 48M / 205M. "Up to 250 times faster and 20 times more memory-efficient" than same-size Chronos; Bolt-Base is more accurate than Chronos-Large while over 600x faster; trained on nearly 100 billion observations. [fetched] VENDOR model card. — [Hugging Face: amazon/chronos-bolt-base](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-bolt-base)
- **Chronos-2** — "Chronos-2: From Univariate to Universal Forecasting", Ansari, Shchur, Küken, Auer et al. (AWS), Oct 2025, arXiv technical report. Encoder-only T5-style transformer, 120M parameters (28M small variant). Input: z-score standardisation then sinh⁻¹ transform; non-overlapping patches through a residual embedding, each patch carrying values, time index and missing-value mask. Alternates **time attention** (within a series, RoPE) with **group attention** (across series/covariates at the same patch index, no positional embedding). Output: direct multi-step forecasts for 21 quantiles (0.01–0.99), pinball loss. Context 2,048 then post-trained to 8,192. Multivariate and covariate training relies **entirely on synthetic data** ("multivariatizers" imposing dependencies on univariate generators). ~300 series/s on one A10G. [fetched, full HTML] PREPRINT (vendor lab). — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)
- **TimesFM** — "A decoder-only foundation model for time-series forecasting", Das, Kong, Sen, Zhou (Google), 2023/2024. "Patched-decoder style attention model"; zero-shot "comes close to the accuracy of state-of-the-art supervised forecasting models". ICML 2024 venue and ~200M parameter count are [memory]. [fetched abstract]. — [arXiv 2310.10688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10688)
- **TimesFM 2.5 / 3.0** — Google repo README. 2.5 (15 Sep 2025): 200M parameters, context up to 16k, optional 30M quantile head for horizons up to 1k, covariates via XReg (external regression, re-added 29 Oct 2025). 3.0 (Aug 2026): 330M parameters, context up to 15,360, 9 quantiles, **native multivariate and past/future covariates**, non-commercial weights licence, claims rank 1 on fev-bench, TIME and GIFT-Eval; rolled into BigQuery ML Sept 2026. [fetched] VENDOR. — [github.com/google-research/timesfm](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm)
- **Moirai** — "Unified Training of Universal Time Series Forecasting Transformers", Woo et al. (Salesforce), 2024. Masked-encoder transformer trained on LOTSA (over 27B observations, nine domains); "competitive or superior performance as a zero-shot forecaster when compared to full-shot models". Multi-patch-size projections, any-variate attention and mixture-distribution output are [memory] (not in the abstract); ICML 2024 venue is [memory]. [fetched abstract]. — [arXiv 2402.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02592)
- **Moirai-MoE** — "Moirai-MoE: Empowering Time Series Foundation Models with Sparse Mixture of Experts", Liu et al., Oct 2024. Replaces frequency-specific input/output projections with one projection plus sparse mixture-of-experts for token-level specialisation; claims wins on 39 datasets in-distribution and zero-shot (no numbers in abstract). [fetched] PREPRINT. — [arXiv 2410.10469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10469)
- **Moirai 2.0** — "Moirai 2.0: When Less Is More for Time Series Forecasting", Liu, Aksu et al., Nov 2025 (v3 Feb 2026). Switches to **decoder-only, single patch size, quantile loss** with multi-token prediction; new 36M-series corpus; about 2x faster and 30x smaller than Moirai-1.0-Large while more accurate; authors' evaluation placed it 5th on MASE and 6th on CRPS on GIFT-Eval. Stated limitations: performance **plateaus with parameter count and declines at longer horizons**. Whether any-variate/multivariate input was dropped is not confirmed from the abstract. [fetched] PREPRINT. — [arXiv 2511.11698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11698)
- **Lag-Llama** — "Lag-Llama: Towards Foundation Models for Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting", Rasul et al., 2023/2024. Decoder-only transformer using **lags as covariates**; univariate probabilistic only. [fetched abstract] PREPRINT. — [arXiv 2310.08278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08278)
- **TimeGPT-1** — Garza, Challu, Mergenthaler-Canseco (Nixtla), 2023. Closed API model; the abstract gives no architecture or training-data details. [fetched] VENDOR preprint. — [arXiv 2310.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03589)
- **MOMENT** — "MOMENT: A Family of Open Time-series Foundation Models", Goswami et al. (CMU AutonLab), ICML 2024. General-purpose (forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, imputation) model pretrained on the "Time Series Pile"; masked-patch reconstruction with a T5 encoder is [memory]. The Mantis report lists MOMENT as not supporting multivariate input. [fetched abstract] PEER-REVIEWED. — [arXiv 2402.03885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03885); [Mantis report](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15637v1)
- **Timer-XL** — "Timer-XL: Long-Context Transformers for Unified Time Series Forecasting", Liu, Qin, Huang, Wang, Long (Tsinghua), 2024/2025. Causal decoder-only; generalises next-token prediction to **multivariate next-token prediction** over flattened patch tokens with "TimeAttention"; handles univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed (exogenous) forecasting. ICLR 2025 venue is [memory]. [fetched abstract]. — [arXiv 2410.04803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04803)
- **TiRex** — "TiRex: Zero-Shot Forecasting Across Long and Short Horizons with Enhanced In-Context Learning", Auer, Podest, Klotz, Böck, Klambauer, Hochreiter (NXAI/JKU), NeurIPS 2025. **xLSTM**, 35M parameters, decoder-only, z-score instance normalisation, non-overlapping patch windows, contiguous patch masking (CPM) to avoid autoregressive re-feeding. Argues recurrent **state tracking** matters for long horizons where transformers underperform; claimed SOTA on GIFT-Eval and Chronos-ZS at release, beating much larger TabPFN-TS, Chronos-Bolt, TimesFM and Moirai. [fetched] PEER-REVIEWED. — [arXiv 2505.23719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23719); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/5356603f9c47399adfd372f77a677057-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Toto 1.0** — "This Time is Different: An Observability Perspective on Time Series Foundation Models", Cohen, Khwaja et al. (Datadog), May 2025 (NeurIPS 2025 per search listing). Decoder-only, next-patch prediction, patch 64, context 4,096, embedding 768, 151M parameters; **causal patch-wise instance normalisation**; factorised attention with an 11:1 ratio of time-wise to variate-wise blocks; **Student-T mixture head**; composite NLL + Cauchy robust loss. Trained on ~2.36 trillion points (43% anonymised Datadog metrics). [fetched, full HTML]. — [arXiv 2505.14766](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14766v2)
- **Toto 2.0** — "Toto 2.0: Time Series Forecasting Enters the Scaling Era", Khwaja, Lettieri, Woo et al. (Datadog), May 2026. Five sizes (4M, 22M, 313M, 1B, 2.5B) from one recipe; contiguous patch masking for single-pass forecasts; example code returns 9 quantiles; context 4,096; u-μP hyperparameter transfer. Claims quality gains scale reliably from 4M to 2.5B. Base models see **no public forecasting data** in pretraining (observability + synthetic only). [fetched] VENDOR preprint/blog. — [arXiv 2605.20119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20119); [Datadog blog](https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/ai/toto-2.md)
- **Timestral** (most recent, 15 Oct 2026) — Mistral. Causal decoders with learned forecast tokens ("Decoupled Horizon Forecasting"); covariate-aware attention assigns each series a role (target, past-only covariate, known-future covariate) with information flowing only covariate→target; pinball loss over 21 quantiles; context 32,768; sizes 5M/28M/123M/209M; pre-trained on **purely synthetic data**, then post-trained on a real+synthetic mix ("the majority of the training data was synthetic"). [fetched] VENDOR. — [mistral.ai/news/timestral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/)
- **t0** — "t0: A Time-Series Foundation Model for Forecasting with Context", Meyer, Sole, Xiang et al., 21 Sep 2026. Open-weights, t0-alpha 102M and t0-beta 256M; transformer layers alternate attention along time and across variates; conditions on target history, past covariates and known-future covariates; quantile outputs; synthetic generators built to include covariate-to-target dependencies. [fetched] PREPRINT. — [arXiv 2609.24559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24559)

**Continuous generative heads (flow matching)**

- **Sundial** — "Sundial: A Family of Highly Capable Time Series Foundation Models", Liu, Qin, Shi et al. (Tsinghua), Feb 2025 (ICML 2025 is [memory]). Pre-trained natively on continuous values with **no discrete tokenisation**; predicts next-patch distribution with **TimeFlow Loss** (flow matching), no prior distribution specified; trained on TimeBench (one trillion points); authors claim this **mitigates mode collapse**. [fetched abstract]. — [arXiv 2502.00816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00816)
- Counter-evidence on flow heads: in the Chronos-2 paper's fev-bench table Sundial scores 41.0% win rate / 33.4% skill versus 90.7% / 47.3% for Chronos-2 (patch + quantile head) and 80.8% / 42.6% for TiRex. [fetched]. — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)

**Tabular prior-data fitted networks (synthetic-prior pretraining, in-context learning)**

- **TabPFN v2** — "Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model", Hollmann et al., Nature, 2025. Transformer pretrained only on synthetic datasets drawn from a prior over structural causal models; predicts for a new table in one forward pass with the training set as context. The Nature page was paywalled/redirected in this session so details here are [memory]; the TabICL abstract confirms TabPFNv2 "excels up to about 10K samples" and uses alternating column- and row-wise attention. PEER-REVIEWED. — [Nature s41586-024-08328-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08328-6); [TabICL abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05564)
- **TabPFN-2.5** — "TabPFN-2.5: Advancing the State of the Art in Tabular Foundation Models", Grinsztajn, Hollmann, Hutter et al. (Prior Labs), Nov 2025 (v2 Feb 2026). Up to 50,000 rows and 2,000 features (20x more cells than v2); claims lead on TabArena, matching AutoGluon 1.4 tuned for four hours; default TabPFN-2.5 has a **100% win rate vs default XGBoost on classification datasets ≤10,000 rows / 500 features**, 87% on larger datasets, 85% on regression; a distillation engine converts it to an MLP or tree ensemble for low-latency deployment. [fetched] VENDOR preprint, self-reported. — [arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08667)
- **TabICL / TabICLv2** — "TabICL: A Tabular Foundation Model for In-Context Learning on Large Data", Qu, Holzmüller, Varoquaux, Le Morvan, ICML 2025: column-then-row attention builds row embeddings, then an ICL transformer; synthetic pretraining with up to 60K samples; scales to 500K rows; matches TabPFNv2 on 200 TALENT classification datasets while up to 10x faster, and beats TabPFNv2 and CatBoost on 53 datasets over 10K rows. TabICLv2 (ICML 2026) claims to surpass tuned, ensembled, real-data-fine-tuned RealTabPFN-2.5 on TabArena and TALENT without tuning. [fetched / snippet] PEER-REVIEWED. — [arXiv 2502.05564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05564); [PMLR v306 TabICLv2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/qu26a.html)
- **TabPFN-TS** — "From Tables to Time: Extending TabPFN-v2 to Time Series Forecasting", Hoo, Müller, Salinas, Hutter, Jan 2025 (v4 Jan 2026). Treats forecasting as tabular regression on calendar/time-index features with **no time-series pretraining at all**; 11M parameters; claims SOTA on covariate-informed forecasting and competitive univariate accuracy on GIFT-Eval and fev-bench. [fetched] PREPRINT. — [arXiv 2501.02945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02945)

**LLM (text-token) backbones for numeric series — evidence for Claim A**

- "Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?", Tan, Merrill, Gupta, Althoff, Hartvigsen, NeurIPS 2024 (Spotlight). Ablates three LLM-based forecasters (Time-LLM, OneFitsAll, CALF): removing the LLM or replacing it with a basic attention layer gives comparable or better results; pretrained LLMs do no better than randomly initialised ones, "do not represent the sequential dependencies in time series, and do not assist in few-shot settings"; a simple patch+attention baseline (PAttn) beats most LLM-based forecasters. Authors explicitly do not claim LLMs will never help. [snippet of proceedings/abstract] PEER-REVIEWED. **SUPPORTS Claim A.** — [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2406.16964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- Counter-example: "A Text-Based Knowledge-Embedded Soft Sensing Modeling Approach for General Industrial Process Tasks Based on Large Language Model" (LLM-TKESS), Tong et al., Jan 2025, arXiv. Fine-tunes a pretrained LLM into a "soft sensing foundation model" with adapters and text knowledge inputs; case study on air-preheater rotor thermal deformation; abstract reports "outstanding" small-sample performance but no numbers and no ablation of the LLM. [fetched] PREPRINT. **MIXED on Claim A** (an LLM-backbone soft sensor exists, but without the Tan-style ablation). — [arXiv 2501.05075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05075)

**Dissent from the dynamical-systems community**

- "Position: A Dynamical Systems Perspective is Needed to Advance Time Series Modeling", Durstewitz, Hemmer, Hess, Doll, Eisenmann, Feb 2026 (v3 Sep 2026), arXiv. Argues series from engineered systems "almost always originate from some underlying DS", that dynamical-systems reconstruction gives long-term statistics, generalisation to unseen regimes and control strategies at far lower compute, and questions how much progress current TSFM hype reflects. Argumentative, no benchmark numbers in the abstract. [fetched] PREPRINT. **SUPPORTS Claim B in spirit** (structure-aware vertical models), **UNDERMINES** the idea that scaling generic TSFMs is the route. — [arXiv 2602.16864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16864)

### Inferences
- The tokenisation debate is effectively settled *against* LLM-style discrete value vocabularies for forecasting: Amazon moved Chronos → Chronos-Bolt → Chronos-2 from quantised tokens to patches with quantile heads; Salesforce moved Moirai from mixture-distribution masked encoder to decoder-only quantile regression; Datadog moved from a Student-T mixture (Toto 1.0) to quantile outputs (Toto 2.0); Timestral, t0 and TimesFM 3.0 all emit quantiles. This is a consistent data point for Claim A: the groups with the most resources chose not to represent numbers as language-like tokens.
- Flow-matching/diffusion heads are an open minority position: Sundial's "no mode collapse" argument is plausible for multimodal futures, but its measured leaderboard position in an independent lab's table is well below quantile-head models. For batch processes (multimodal outcomes such as "contaminated vs clean" trajectories) the argument may matter more than on public benchmarks; I found no test of this.
- The two things every recent leader added in 2025–26 are (i) cross-series/variate attention and (ii) explicit covariate roles — both learned largely from **synthetic** multivariate structure. That is a transferable recipe for a vertical model: a process simulator could play the role of Chronos-2's "multivariatizers".
- Parameter counts are small by LLM standards (5M–330M for most leaders; only Toto 2.0 goes to 2.5B). Training a competitive numeric-native model is within reach of a start-up; the scarce asset is data and priors, not compute.

### Gaps
- Timer (the predecessor of Timer-XL) and Morpheus-2.0, Tabby, Falcon-2.0 and IBM Granite PatchTST-FM were seen only as leaderboard names; I could not open primary sources, so no architecture is given for them.
- I could not confirm from primary text the Chronos v1 bin count, Moirai's any-variate attention details, MOMENT's masking scheme, or TOTEM's VQ-VAE specifics (all [memory]).
- No source directly compared discrete tokens, continuous patches and flow heads on the *same* backbone and data; the "debate" is inferred from lineage changes and cross-paper tables.

## 2. Benchmarks: what GIFT-Eval, fev-bench, BOOM, TIME and TSB-AD show, and whether FMs beat tuned classical / small supervised baselines

### Takeaway
On aggregate forecasting leaderboards, current TSFMs beat classical statistical baselines by a wide margin (roughly 47–50% skill over seasonal naive versus ~20% for AutoARIMA on fev-bench) and the top models are now statistically hard to separate. But on **anomaly detection**, on **out-of-distribution domains**, and against **well-tuned simple models**, the evidence is frequently negative, and benchmark leakage is an acknowledged problem.

**Critiques: simple or small models beating TSFMs**

- "Performance of Zero-Shot Time Series Foundation Models on Cloud Data", Toner, Lee, Joosen, Singh, Asenov, ICLR 2025 "I Can't Believe It's Not Better" workshop. Many well-known FMs "fail to generate meaningful or accurate zero-shot forecasts" on cloud data and "are outperformed consistently by simple linear baselines"; per search summary, the naive seasonal baseline's MASE is typically half that of TimesFM, and the best FM (VisionTS) is near-identical to naive seasonal. [fetched abstract; numbers from snippet] WORKSHOP. **UNDERMINES generic TSFMs out of domain; SUPPORTS the need for vertical data.** — [arXiv 2502.12944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12944)

### Inferences
- "Do FMs beat classical baselines?" has different answers by task. **Forecasting on diverse public data: yes, clearly** (≈2x the skill score of AutoARIMA on fev-bench). **Anomaly/fault detection: no** — peer-reviewed 2026 evidence says frozen TSFMs equal one-line detectors. **Domains far from the pretraining mix (cloud, observability before Toto): no**, until someone pretrains on that domain's data.
- BOOM is the cleanest public demonstration of the vertical-data mechanism the thesis relies on: a domain whose statistics differ from public corpora (high cardinality, spiky, non-stationary) → general TSFMs degrade → a model pretrained on 43% in-domain data wins by ~13% MASE. Process data shares several of these properties (many correlated variates, regime changes, flat spots).
- The "tuned linear regression beats Chronos-Base" and "50K-parameter SAMFormer beats fine-tuned TimesFM" results concern 2024-era models and/or single datasets; the 2025–26 leaders have not, as far as I found, been subjected to the same tuned-linear test. Treat the critique as a warning about baselines, not as a current ranking.
- Top-of-leaderboard gaps are now ~1–2% on aggregate metrics and vendor-reported; they are not a basis for choosing a model for a pharma application. Covariate handling, licence (TimesFM 3.0 weights are non-commercial) and fine-tunability matter more.

- No primary source for the TIME benchmark's construction; "contamination-resistant" is Datadog's description.

- I found no benchmark that includes tuned DLinear/PatchTST-style supervised baselines alongside the 2026 leaders with equal tuning effort.

## 3. Multivariate, covariates, irregular sampling, long horizons and control inputs

### Takeaway
Since late 2025 the leading forecasters accept multivariate targets and past/known-future covariates natively, and covariates deliver their largest gains. But three 2026 studies aimed squarely at control show that covariate-aware TSFMs **do not reliably learn the causal response to a manipulated input**: they are memoryless or attenuate the effect, and classical system identification on a few hundred samples beats them. Irregular, multi-rate sampling remains mostly unsupported.

### Cited Findings

**Capability map (from the sources in section 1)**

- Univariate only: Lag-Llama; TiRex (treated as univariate in comparisons). Multivariate via cross-variate attention: Moirai 1.0 [memory], Toto 1.0/2.0, Timer-XL, Chronos-2, t0, Timestral, TimesFM 3.0. Explicit past-only and known-future covariates (numeric and categorical): Chronos-2, Timestral, t0, TimesFM 3.0; TimesFM 2.5 only through an external regression wrapper (XReg). Covariates as ordinary tabular features with a continuous time index: TabPFN-TS. — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1); [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral/); [arXiv 2609.24559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24559); [TimesFM repo](https://github.com/google-research/timesfm); [arXiv 2501.02945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02945)

[fetched]. MIXED. — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)

**Control inputs / manipulated variables (most recent, Oct–Nov 2026)**

- "Attenuated in-context identification in time-series foundation models: diagnosis under counterfactual inputs and repair by synthetic forced-system fine-tuning", Hong-In Won (KITECH / Hanyang), 6 Oct 2026, arXiv. Tests Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5 and TabPFN-TS for training-free "what-if" prediction on six synthetic forced-system classes and four measured plants. **TimesFM-2.5 and TabPFN-TS are memoryless through their default covariate interfaces** (predicted effect depends only on the same-time input, R² = 1.000). **Chronos-2 identifies dynamics in context but attenuates them to 0.33–0.80 of the true effect**, with a wrongly shaped impulse response; on an oscillator its error plateaus at 0.57 with 8,192 context samples while **ARX fitted to 256 samples reaches 0.02**. ### Inferences
- The control result is the most thesis-relevant finding in these notes. Covariate support in a forecaster is not the same as an input–output model: generic TSFMs learn correlation with a covariate channel, not the plant's response, unless the context is persistently excited — and GMP batch records are, by design, *not* excited (setpoints are held within validated ranges). This predicts that off-the-shelf TSFMs will forecast a bioreactor trajectory tolerably while giving wrong answers to "what if I raise the feed rate".
- The repair route demonstrated by Won (fine-tune on synthetic forced systems for 26 minutes) is direct evidence that **simulator-generated, control-structured pretraining data** is the missing ingredient rather than a new architecture. A vertical model built on mechanistic bioprocess simulators with randomised kinetics and control actions is the obvious analogue; nobody appears to have published it.
- Multi-rate data (seconds-level sensors, hourly PAT spectra, daily offline assays, one-off batch-end CQAs) is not natively handled by any leaderboard model; time-indexed tabular models (TabPFN-TS, MoTM) are the closest fit because they do not assume a grid.
- FactoryNet's S-E-F-C schema is a concrete representation idea for process data: separate setpoint, manipulated variable, measured response and context as typed channels rather than anonymous variates.

### Gaps
- No study combined irregular/multi-rate sampling, manipulated inputs and closed-loop evaluation on a chemical or biological process.
- The Tu et al. NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper's model list and numbers were not available on the listing page.
- No evidence was found on whether Timestral, t0 or TimesFM 3.0 (released after Chronos-2) fix the input-response attenuation; they were not in the Won study.

Direct evaluations of generic TSFMs on process-industry monitoring tasks are few and mostly **negative** (Tennessee Eastman, C-MAPSS, industrial anomaly detection), while small bespoke models pretrained on a single plant's own history (crude distillation, hydrotreating, fed-batch) report wins — but these are preprints, a master's thesis, or abstracts without independently reproducible baselines. ### Inferences
- The pattern across independent evaluations is consistent: frozen generic TSFMs used through forecast or reconstruction residuals are **worse than or equal to trivial detectors** for fault detection (TEP 19–22% vs 69%; C-MAPSS AUROC 0.73 vs 0.96; ICLR 2026 one-liners). Fault detection in a multivariate plant is about deviations from cross-variable correlation structure under a given operating regime — information a univariate, zero-shot forecaster does not have.
- Where pretrained numeric models *do* win in industry, it is (a) as **feature extractors with a few labels** (motor monitoring at 1% labels) or (b) as **small models pretrained on the same plant's own multi-year history** (Prometheus, IPM-FM). Neither is a cross-plant foundation model. Cross-site, cross-product transfer — the property a pharma vertical model would need — is undemonstrated in the process industries; FactoryNet's "transfer rarely across manufacturers" is a cautionary robotics analogue.
- The effect sizes reported by bespoke process models are modest where they are quantified against named-quality baselines (IPM-FM: 8.3% RMSE gain, R² 0.50). That is evidence for the thesis direction but not for a step-change.
- For the founder's thesis: the evidence supports "generic TSFMs + harness are not enough for process monitoring and control" more strongly than it supports "a vertical FM will win" — the latter currently rests on one thesis, two preprints and one unread journal article.

### Gaps
- **No results found for AspenTech, Yokogawa, Honeywell, Basetwo or Bosch** time-series/process foundation models (searched once collectively; the search budget ran out before per-vendor follow-ups).
- **No foundation-model study on mammalian cell culture, mAb fed-batch, perfusion or downstream chromatography process data** was found. Bioprocess evidence is limited to Time-FLM (unread), Raman soft-sensor work (section 6) and vendor statements.
- No semiconductor-fab evaluation of TSFMs on production virtual-metrology data was found.
- TEP paper details (authors' affiliations, date, MOMENT numbers, PCA/DPCA baselines) and Time-FLM results need to be read from the primary pages, which blocked automated access.
- No TSFM evaluation on batch-process monitoring formulations (MPCA/MPLS-style, batch-to-batch) was found.

## 5. Small-data reality: few-shot / zero-shot transfer and synthetic-prior pretraining

### Takeaway
The strongest positive evidence for numeric-native pretrained models in the tens-to-hundreds-of-samples regime comes from **tabular PFNs pretrained only on synthetic priors**, which now top independent small-data benchmarks in spectroscopy; synthetic pretraining is also how the leading forecasters learn multivariate and covariate structure. Evidence that this transfers to few-batch process trajectories is indirect, and simulation-to-real results warn that an uncalibrated simulator prior can be useless.

[fetched] — [arXiv 2608.13341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13341); [PMC12264939](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12264939/)

### Inferences
- The small-data regime is where the "more data" half of the thesis bites hardest: with tens of batches per product there is no route to a data-hungry model per product, so the only options are (i) priors learned elsewhere and applied in-context, or (ii) mechanistic/hybrid models. PFN-style in-context learners are the existing technology that most directly matches (i), and they are numeric-native — a point for Claim A.
- However, the tabular evidence cuts against Claim B: generic TabPFN, with no chemistry in its prior, beat Raman-specific networks and PLS on average. A vertical model has to beat *that*, not just beat an LLM. The NIR study's finding that the advantage disappears on outliers and extrapolation is exactly where a domain prior (spectral physics, mass balances, kinetics) could plausibly add value — and where GMP use cases live (new product, new scale, excursion).
- A credible build path suggested by the evidence: PFN-style or Chronos-2-style training on a **prior of simulated bioprocesses** (randomised kinetic models, feeding and control policies, sensor noise, multi-rate sampling), then post-training on pooled real batches. The Won and FactoryNet results say the simulator must be calibrated on the channels that carry the signal of interest; breadth alone is not enough.
- "Batch" is a small-n unit but each batch is a long multivariate trajectory; tabular PFNs treat rows as exchangeable and TSFMs treat series as independent contexts. Neither natively represents "N prior batches of this product as context for batch N+1". That representational gap is open.

### Gaps
- No published zero-/few-shot evaluation of Chronos-2, TimesFM, TabPFN or similar on cell-culture or fermentation batch trajectories with tens of batches was found (searches for this returned nothing before the budget ran out).
- No published PFN trained on a process-simulator prior was found.
- The MDPI PFN fault-diagnosis review's quantitative evidence was not retrievable.

## 6. Spectral data (Raman, NIR, IR, mass spectrometry, chromatography) for PAT

### Takeaway
There is still no public pretrained "Raman/NIR foundation model for bioprocess PAT"; the 2026 state of the art on small spectral calibration problems is a **generic tabular foundation model (TabPFN v2.5 / TabICL v2)**, with classical PLS remaining competitive on outliers and extrapolation. Genuine spectral foundation models exist for IR (simulation-pretrained) and tandem mass spectrometry, and vendors ship transfer-learned Raman calibrations for cell culture.

### Cited Findings

- **RamanBench** — "RamanBench: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Machine Learning on Raman Spectroscopy", Koddenbrock, Lange, Legner, Jäger, Kögler, Cruz Bournazou, Neubauer, Biessmann, Rodner, May 2026 (v4 Oct 2026), NeurIPS 2026. 77 datasets (17 new), 169 targets, 325,862 spectra, four domains including bioprocess monitoring (a "Bioprocess Analytes" collection measuring the same analytes on eight spectrometers; a time-gated E. coli fermentation set with 12 spectra); 28 models. ### Inferences
- For PAT soft sensors (glucose, lactate, titre, VCD from Raman/NIR), the practical 2026 recommendation implied by the evidence is: benchmark TabPFN-2.5/TabICLv2 against PLS before building anything bespoke. That is a numeric-native pretrained model — but a horizontal one.
- The opening for a vertical spectral model is where the horizontal one is documented to be weak: extrapolation to new cell lines/media/instruments/scales (NIR study; Peng et al.'s meta-features; RamanBench's eight-spectrometer collection). UltraIR's cross-instrument zero-shot claim and Merck's ready-to-use models suggest instrument- and matrix-invariance is learnable from pooled or simulated data; no public Raman model has shown it for bioprocess.
- Mass-spectrometry FMs show that self-supervised pretraining on raw spectra (peak lists as native tokens, not text) yields large identification gains — the clearest spectral-domain support for native representations — but these target analytical characterisation, not in-line process monitoring.

### Gaps
- No pretrained/foundation model specific to **chromatography traces** (HPLC/SEC/CEX chromatograms for product quality) was found; DreaMS only uses retention order as a pretext task.
- No public benchmark numbers for Raman calibration transfer across CHO cell lines/scales using pretrained models; the RamanBench per-dataset bioprocess scores were not in the portion of the paper I could read.
- The Merck ProCellics numbers come from a vendor application note seen only via search summary (number of runs, analytes and scales not retrieved).
- DreaMS publication details and quantitative results were not verifiable in this session.
