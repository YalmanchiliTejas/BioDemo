## 1. The core argument: what LeCun (2022) claims about autoregressive generative models, and the published rebuttals

### Takeaway
LeCun's position has four separable claims (predict in representation space, not observation space; drop unpredictable detail instead of modelling it; avoid collapse by regularisation instead of contrastive negatives; act by optimisation/MPC over a learned world model), plus a fifth claim made in talks rather than the paper (per-token errors compound as (1-e)^n). The fifth is the weakest and is widely rebutted; the first two have real head-to-head support on sensory/physical data, but "LLMs learn no world model" is contradicted by probing studies, and JEPA's own latent rollouts are autoregressive too.

### Cited Findings

**The claim itself**

- Paper: "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence", Yann LeCun, 2022, OpenReview position paper (v0.9.2). URL: [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf). NOTE: the OpenReview PDF could not be fetched in this session (browser-verification wall), so the points below are taken from Meta's official summary and from papers that quote it, not from the PDF text.
- Architecture: six differentiable modules (configurator, perception, world model, cost with intrinsic cost + trainable critic, actor, short-term memory); the actor minimises predicted future cost "similar to classical optimal control" — [Meta AI blog, 2022](https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-ai-research/).
- Predict in representation space: JEPA encodes x and y and trains a predictor to predict y's representation from x's; the encoder is allowed to drop hard-to-predict details, and a latent variable z yields a set of plausible predictions. The blog's example: "I don't need to predict the detailed position of individual leaves in the trees" — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-ai-research/).
- Collapse prevention: contrastive methods need many incompatible examples, which is "rather impractical" in high-dimensional representation spaces; regularised (non-contrastive) methods such as VICReg (variance, invariance, covariance regularisation) are preferred — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-ai-research/).
- Planning: hierarchical JEPA (H-JEPA) gives more abstract, longer-horizon predictions at higher levels; planning is top-down with higher-level actions as sub-goals and "resembles Model-Predictive Control"; gradient-based action optimisation if all modules are differentiable — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-ai-research/).
- Self-admitted open problems: how to train the critic and configurator and how to use short-term memory were "still to be defined" — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/yann-lecun-advances-in-ai-research/).
- The exponential error-compounding argument is attributed to LeCun's March 2023 NYU slide deck, not the 2022 paper: with per-token error probability e, P(correct at length n) = (1-e)^n; half-life is about log2/e tokens, i.e. roughly 70 tokens at e = 1% — [Brando Miranda blog, 26 May 2026](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/brando9/2026/05/26/ar-error-compounding-real-or-fiction.html).

**Rebuttals of the error-compounding argument** — all **[UNDERMINES]** the strong form "autoregression is doomed"

- (2026) Miranda accepts the algebra but lists three contestable assumptions: e constant across positions, errors independent, errors unrecoverable. Proposes a two-state recoverable-Markov model (on/off manifold with non-zero recovery probability); with a verifier that enables backtracking the exponent changes. No experiments reported; cites Dziri et al., "Faith and Fate" (NeurIPS 2023) as peer-reviewed evidence that compounding does occur in compositional tasks — [Miranda 2026](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/brando9/2026/05/26/ar-error-compounding-real-or-fiction.html); companion post on the toy AR-vs-EBM argument: [Miranda, 9 Jun 2026](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/brando9/2026/06/09/why-energy-based-models-the-toy-ar-vs-ebm-argument.html).
- "Contra LeCun on 'Autoregressive LLMs are doomed'", rotatingpaguro, LessWrong, 10 Apr 2023. Comment by gwern: LeCun is "trivially wrong" because inner-monologue/chain-of-thought shows error is not monotonic — a model can produce a wrong estimate and then correct it. Other commenters: errors are strongly dependent, long documents are modular, RL-style training escapes pure imitation — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zyPaqXgFzqHkQfccq/contra-lecun-on-autoregressive-llms-are-doomed).
- "The Pitfalls of Next-Token Prediction", Gregor Bachmann and Vaishnavh Nagarajan, ICML 2024. Says the compounding argument "crucially assumes that teacher-forcing has learned an accurate next-token predictor" and conflates inference-time autoregression with training-time teacher forcing. Their own (different) negative result: on a minimal planning task, teacher-forced Transformer and Mamba both fail "despite the task being straightforward to learn"; "teacherless" multi-token prediction fixes it. **[MIXED]**: weakens LeCun's specific argument but supplies a separate, experimentally demonstrated failure of next-token training on planning — [arXiv 2403.06963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963).
- "Critiques of World Models", Eric Xing, Mingkai Deng, Jinyu Hou, Zhiting Hu (MBZUAI/CMU/UCSD), arXiv Jul 2025: JEPA "remains fundamentally autoregressive and generative" because it recursively simulates latent states — so the compounding critique applies to JEPA rollouts as well — [arXiv 2507.05169](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05169v3).
- The JEPA camp concedes this in its own papers: V-JEPA 2-AC lists "autoregressive prediction accumulates error over longer rollouts" as a limitation — [V-JEPA 2, arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1); TS-JEPA reports "uncertainty amplification in long-term roll-out forecasting" for both JEPA and autoregressive baselines — [arXiv 2509.25449](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25449v1).

**"LLMs do learn world models"** — **[UNDERMINES]** the claim that token prediction cannot yield world structure

- "Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task" (Othello-GPT), Kenneth Li et al., ICLR 2023 (oral). GPT trained only on legal Othello move sequences develops an internal board-state representation recoverable by non-linear probes; interventions on it change the model's moves — [arXiv 2210.13382](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.13382v5). Follow-ups find the representation is linear: Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg — [arXiv 2309.00941](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00941v1); Hazineh et al. — [arXiv 2310.07582](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07582v2).
- "Language Models Represent Space and Time", Wes Gurnee and Max Tegmark, ICLR 2024. Linear probes on Llama-2 activations recover latitude/longitude and dates across six datasets; individual "space neurons" and "time neurons" found. Authors frame this as basic ingredients of a world model, not a complete one — [arXiv 2310.02207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02207).

**"…but the world models are incoherent"** — **[SUPPORTS]**

- "Evaluating the World Model Implicit in a Generative Model", Keyon Vafa, Justin Chen, Ashesh Rambachan, Jon Kleinberg, Sendhil Mullainathan, NeurIPS 2024. Transformers trained on NYC taxi turn sequences almost always predict a valid next turn, yet the street map reconstructed from them has impossible orientations and flyovers and "little resemblance" to Manhattan; performance breaks down when detours are added. Conclusion: good next-token metrics can coexist with an incoherent world model — [arXiv 2406.03689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03689).

**Critique of the JEPA programme itself** — **[UNDERMINES]** the "JEPA specifically" version of the thesis

- Xing et al. (2025), five critiques: (1) data — video is redundant and low in semantic content, privileging it over text "reflects a limitation or bias"; (2) representation — "Vocabulary-based tokens are thus not a liability, but an asset", propose mixed continuous + discrete; (3) architecture — JEPA is still autoregressive in latent space; (4) objective — Proposition 1: a latent-only loss can be trivially minimised "by mapping all observations to a constant vector"; Theorem 2: the latent loss is upper-bounded by the generative loss, so minimising it does not guarantee consistency with observations; a generative (reconstruction) loss has no such degenerate solution; (5) usage — MPC is costly at inference and short-horizon, RL in the world model can amortise long-horizon value. The paper contains no experiments; the proposed alternative (PAN, with "Generative Latent Prediction") is a design preview only — [arXiv 2507.05169](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05169v3).

### Inferences
- The defensible core of LeCun's position for a process-data founder is not "autoregression fails" but "what you choose to predict determines what the representation keeps". For sensor data with a lot of unpredictable high-variance content (measurement noise, valve chatter), a loss that must explain every sample spends capacity on that noise; a latent-prediction loss can ignore it. Sections 3 and 5 show that this claim has direct experimental support and some counter-evidence.
- The error-compounding argument should not be used in a pitch: it is the best-rebutted part of the thesis, and it applies equally to any multi-step latent rollout a JEPA world model would do for a 14-day fed-batch run.
- Probing results (Othello-GPT, Gurnee and Tegmark) show that sequence models form state representations when trained on the right sequences; Vafa et al. show that these can be good enough for next-step prediction and still wrong as a model of the system. For a regulated plant the second property (coherence under off-nominal conditions — "detours") is the one that matters, and it is not guaranteed by either paradigm.

### Gaps
- The 2022 paper's exact wording on energy-based vs probabilistic models, and on LLMs, could not be quoted because the PDF could not be fetched. The widely repeated summary (abandon normalised probabilistic models in favour of energy functions; LLM scaling is insufficient) is not independently verified here.
- No peer-reviewed experiment was found that fits geometric vs recoverable error models to success-vs-length data on real LLM systems; Miranda (2026) states it as a hypothesis to test.
- OpenReview reviewer comments on LeCun's position paper were not retrieved.

## 2. JEPA family results: setup, numbers, compute, stated limitations

### Takeaway
On images and video the JEPA line now has strong frozen-feature results and one real-robot zero-shot planning result (V-JEPA 2-AC: 65–80% pick-and-place from 62 hours of unlabeled robot video), and since late 2025 the EMA/stop-gradient heuristics can be replaced by a single regulariser (SIGReg) that trains small world models on one GPU. But planning remains short-horizon, deterministic, image-goal-only and slow (16 s per action), the best planning encoder is DINO rather than JEPA, and one-step latent prediction error turns out to be a poor proxy for planning quality.

### Cited Findings

**I-JEPA** — "Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture", Mahmoud Assran et al., ICCV 2023. Predicts representations of several target blocks from one context block, no hand-crafted augmentations. Compute: ViT-Huge/14 on ImageNet with 16 A100 GPUs in under 72 hours. Key design finding: target blocks must be large (semantic) and the context block spatially distributed. **[SUPPORTS]** (augmentation-free SSL is what a sensor domain needs) — [arXiv 2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243). ImageNet linear-probe figure not re-verified in this session.

**V-JEPA** — "Revisiting Feature Prediction for Learning Visual Representations from Video", Adrien Bardes et al., arXiv Feb 2024. Feature prediction as the only objective (no pretrained image encoder, text, negatives or reconstruction), 2 million public videos. Frozen ViT-H/16: 81.9% Kinetics-400, 72.2% Something-Something-v2, 77.9% ImageNet-1K. **[SUPPORTS]** — [arXiv 2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471).

**MC-JEPA** — "MC-JEPA: A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Self-Supervised Learning of Motion and Content Features", Adrien Bardes, Jean Ponce, Yann LeCun, arXiv Jul 2023. Shared encoder learns optical flow and content features jointly; abstract claims "on-par" with unsupervised optical-flow benchmarks and SSL methods on segmentation. No numbers in abstract. **[MIXED]** (parity, not superiority) — [arXiv 2307.12698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12698).

**IWM** — "Learning and Leveraging World Models in Visual Representation Learning", Quentin Garrido et al., arXiv Mar 2024. Extends JEPA from masked-patch prediction to predicting the latent effect of global photometric transformations. Finding: predictor conditioning and capacity control whether the representation is invariant (contrastive-like) or equivariant (MIM-like); a fine-tuned IWM predictor matches or surpasses prior SSL methods. **[SUPPORTS]** as a design lesson: the predictor is a tunable knob on abstraction level — [arXiv 2403.00504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00504v1). Numbers not retrieved.

**V-JEPA 2 and V-JEPA 2-AC** — "V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning", Mahmoud Assran et al. (FAIR at Meta, Mila), arXiv Jun 2025 — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1); [Meta blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/v-jepa-2-world-model-benchmarks/).
- Pretraining: over 1 million hours of internet video plus about 1M images (VideoMix22M, 22M samples); encoder scaled from ViT-L (300M) to ViT-g (just over 1B parameters).
- Understanding: 77.3 top-1 on Something-Something v2 (ViT-g384, attentive probe); Epic-Kitchens-100 action anticipation 39.7 recall@5 vs previous best 27.6 (44% relative improvement); with an 8B LLM: PerceptionTest 84.0, TempCompass 76.9.
- V-JEPA 2-AC: ~300M-parameter action-conditioned predictor trained on less than 62 hours of unlabeled robot video (Droid), no rewards or task labels; planning by cross-entropy method (CEM) minimising L1 distance between predicted and goal-image representations, receding horizon.
- Zero-shot on Franka arms in two labs not seen in training, 10 trials each, averages: reach 100%; grasp 65% (cup) / 25% (box); reach-with-object 75% / 75%; pick-and-place 80% / 65%. Octo (behaviour cloning) baseline: pick-and-place 15% / 10%, grasp 15% / 0%.
- Versus a generative video world model (Cosmos) in Lab 2: Cosmos 0% pick-and-place (cup and box), 4 minutes per action; V-JEPA 2-AC 80% / 50%, 16 seconds per action.
- Stated limitations: sensitivity to camera position (action axes inferred from an uncalibrated monocular camera; authors manually tried positions); long-horizon planning limited by rollout error accumulation and exponential growth of the search space; requires image goals (language goals are future work); pick-and-place needed image sub-goals.
- **[SUPPORTS]** latent prediction over pixel-generative world models for control; **[MIXED]** for manufacturing transfer because 16 s/action, horizon-1 planning and 25% grasp success are far from process-control reliability.

**DINO-WM** — "DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning", Gaoyue Zhou, Hengkai Pan, Yann LeCun, Lerrel Pinto, ICML 2025 — [arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25t.html).
- Frozen DINOv2 patch features; ViT predictor with frame-level causal attention, actions concatenated to patch tokens, teacher forcing, latent loss only (pixel decoder for visualisation only); MPC with CEM to a goal latent. Gradient-descent planning was tried and was worse than CEM.
- Offline, reward-free datasets of 1,000–18,500 trajectories.
- Success rate (higher better) / Chamfer distance (lower better): Maze 0.98, Wall 0.96, Reach 0.92, Push-T 0.90, Rope CD 0.41, Granular CD 0.26. DreamerV3: 1.00, 1.00, 0.64, 0.30, 2.49, 1.05. TD-MPC2: 0.00 on all four success tasks. IRIS: 0.74, 0.04, 0.18, 0.32, 1.11, 0.37.
- Stated limitations: needs offline data with sufficient state-action coverage; needs ground-truth actions; plans in raw action space only (no hierarchy).
- **[SUPPORTS]**. Caveat (inference): DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 are reward-driven online agents being evaluated in an offline goal-conditioned setting.

**JEPA world-model design study** — "What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?", Basile Terver, Tsung-Yen Yang, Jean Ponce, Adrien Bardes, Yann LeCun, arXiv Dec 2025 (v3 2026) — [arXiv 2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3); code [facebookresearch/jepa-wms](https://github.com/facebookresearch/jepa-wms).
- Findings: CEM with L2 cost best overall; gradient planners get stuck in local minima on navigation and real robot data; a 2-step rollout loss beats pure teacher forcing (6-step best on real DROID data, longer hurts in simulation); proprioception consistently helps; DINO encoders clearly beat V-JEPA encoders for planning; larger encoders/deeper predictors help only on real data, not simulation; planning context must not exceed training context.
- Results vs baselines (success %, proposed / DINO-WM / V-JEPA-2-AC): Maze 83.9 / 81.6 / –; Wall 78.8 / 64.1 / –; Push-T 70.2 / 66.0 / –; Metaworld Reach 58.2 / 44.8 / –; Robocasa Place 30.7 / 21.7 / 33.1; DROID action score 48.2 / 39.4 / 42.9.
- Stated limitation: the predictor is deterministic (MSE learns the conditional mean of possibly multimodal futures); "environments with aleatoric uncertainty would need stochastic extensions such as latent variable injection or latent diffusion".
- **[MIXED]**: confirms the recipe works and is tunable, but absolute success rates are 30–80% and the JEPA-pretrained encoder is not the best encoder.

**LeJEPA / SIGReg** — "LeJEPA: Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics", Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun, arXiv Nov 2025 — [arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544); [code](https://github.com/rbalestr-lab/lejepa).
- Claim: the isotropic Gaussian is the optimal embedding distribution for minimising downstream prediction risk; SIGReg (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization) enforces it by applying the Epps–Pulley characteristic-function test to random 1-D projections (default 256 slices, 1024 recommended; λ = 0.05).
- Removes stop-gradient, teacher–student/EMA and schedulers; one trade-off hyperparameter; linear time and memory; ~50 lines of code; validated on 10+ datasets and 60+ architectures.
- ImageNet-1k linear probe 79% with ViT-H/14.
- Training loss correlates with downstream accuracy: about 85% Spearman for the raw loss, close to 99% with a λ-scaling heuristic — i.e. label-free model selection.
- In-domain pretraining on Galaxy10 with small ConvNeXt-V2 Nano / ResNet-34 beats transfer from DINOv2/DINOv3 ViT-S (e.g. 82.72 / 83.28 vs 78.34 / 81.60 in one column of Figure 1; column semantics were ambiguous in the extracted page).
- Caveats visible in the paper: the Galaxy10 comparison confounds architecture with pretraining method; minibatch SIGReg gradient is biased at O(1/N); teacher–student still gives a small boost for ViTs; all visible experiments are images despite an "any domain" framing.
- **[SUPPORTS]** — this is the single most relevant result for a vertical-model thesis: small in-domain SSL can beat transfer from a giant generalist, and the collapse heuristics are no longer required. **[MIXED]** on strength of evidence: one small-data domain, architecture-confounded, 79% is below the best DINO-family linear probes (comparison not shown in the retrieved text).

**(2026) LeWorldModel (LeWM)** — "LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels", Lucas Maes, Quentin Le Lidec, Damien Scieur, Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero, arXiv Mar 2026; NeurIPS 2026 poster — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/150076).
- Two loss terms only (next-embedding prediction + SIGReg); no stop-gradient, EMA or pretrained encoder; tunable loss hyperparameters cut from six to one versus the only prior end-to-end alternative (PLDM).
- ~15M parameters (≈5M ViT-Tiny encoder + ≈10M predictor); trains on one GPU in a few hours; plans up to 48× faster than DINO-WM (under one second per plan) with about 200× fewer tokens per observation.
- Planning: beats PLDM by 18 points on Push-T and beats DINO-WM there; better than both on Reacher; DINO-WM slightly better on OGBench-Cube; PLDM and DINO-WM both better on Two-Room. (Numeric table is in a figure and was not extracted.)
- Latent probing on Push-T (linear probe Pearson r): agent location 0.974, block location 0.986, block angle 0.902. "Surprise" rises significantly (p < 0.01) for teleporting objects, not significantly for colour changes.
- Stated limitations: short planning horizons; needs offline data with good coverage; SIGReg struggles in low-diversity, low-intrinsic-dimension environments (Two-Room) because the data cannot fill a high-dimensional Gaussian; needs action labels.
- **[SUPPORTS]** feasibility for a small team; the low-diversity failure is **[UNDERMINES]**-relevant for batch manufacturing, where data are deliberately low-diversity.

**(2026) Evaluation problem** — "One-Step Prediction Fidelity Is a Poor Proxy for Planning Performance in JEPA World Models", Joshua Cheung and Zhang He, arXiv Oct 2026, NeurIPS 2026 WM-PAI Workshop — [arXiv 2610.19375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19375). Fine-tuning an end-to-end JEPA world model across three control environments and three seeds: in epochs 2–16 one-step error improves a further 15% while its correlation with planning success is r = -0.010; a 93% error reduction on TwoRoom gives -0.7 pp planning, a 40% reduction on PushT gives +4.9 pp; a matched continued-training control explains the entire gain previously attributed to an auxiliary objective. **[UNDERMINES]** (model selection by latent loss is unreliable for control; conflicts with LeJEPA's loss-tracks-accuracy claim, which was for classification probes).

**Collapse still an active research topic** — "Understanding Early Collapse in Predictive World-Model Pretraining" is listed at ICLR 2026; the search summary describes a collapse phase arising because EMA keeps the target encoder too close to the online encoder — [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10018046) (search summary only). LeWM states existing JEPA methods are "highly prone to collapse" — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312). **[UNDERMINES]** (for EMA-style JEPAs).

**LLM-JEPA** — "LLM-JEPA: Large Language Models Meet Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures", Hai Huang (Atlassian), Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero, arXiv Sep 2025; listed in ICLR 2026 proceedings — [arXiv 2509.14252](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14252v2); [ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/ac818ec2da1c976b267de32f59709c69-Abstract-Conference.html).
- Adds a JEPA term (cosine distance between predicted and actual embeddings of two views, e.g. natural-language description and its regex/SQL) to the next-token loss.
- Fine-tuning accuracy, baseline vs LLM-JEPA: Llama-3.2-1B NL-RX-SYNTH 57.29 → 71.46; GSM8K 32.36 → 36.36; Spider 47.52 → 50.55; Llama-3.1-8B NL-RX-SYNTH 35.77 → 63.57 (±16.81); OLMo-2-7B 87.26 → 87.75.
- Stated limitations: roughly 2× training compute; two extra hyperparameters with no clear optimum; requires datasets with natural paired views; pretraining results preliminary; with the generative loss removed the model outputs only empty text.
- **[MIXED]**: evidence that latent-prediction objectives add something to LLMs, but as an add-on to, not a replacement for, next-token training.

**VL-JEPA** — "VL-JEPA: Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for Vision-language", Delong Chen et al. (incl. LeCun, Fung), arXiv Dec 2025, revised Feb 2026 — [arXiv 2512.10942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10942). Predicts continuous text embeddings instead of generating tokens; 1.6B parameters; in a controlled comparison with token-space VLM training (same encoder and data) performs better with 50% fewer trainable parameters; selective decoding cuts decoding operations 2.85×; VQA comparable to InstructBLIP/QwenVL. **[SUPPORTS]** (controlled embedding-vs-token comparison).

**Intuitive physics** — "Intuitive physics understanding emerges from self-supervised pretraining on natural videos", Quentin Garrido et al. (FAIR), arXiv Feb 2025 — [arXiv 2502.11831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11831). V-JEPA reaches 98% zero-shot on IntPhys violation-of-expectation; pixel-prediction video models and multimodal LLMs are "around chance level" per the authors. Limitation stated: no action conditioning; not uniformly accurate. **[SUPPORTS]**.
- (2026) Counterpoint: "How Do Video Foundation Models Encode Intuitive Physics? Probing Across Pretraining Paradigms", Samuele Punzo et al., arXiv Jun 2026, NeurIPS 2026 workshop — [arXiv 2606.09646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09646). Frozen-feature probing of V-JEPA (latent-predictive), VideoMAE (masked reconstruction) and LTX-Video (diffusion): V-JEPA best on MVP (95.0% pair consistency), VideoMAE best on IntPhys2 (73.9%), diffusion model consistently lowest. Conclusion: accessibility of physics "depends more on the benchmark and readout than on the pretraining objective or model scale". **[MIXED]**.

**Other 2026 follow-ups surfaced (titles verified, contents not read)** — "Depth-Regularized JEPA World Models Learn More Transferable Representations from Real Outdoor Robot Data" — [arXiv 2607.16314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16314); "A Lightweight Library for Energy-Based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures" — [arXiv 2602.03604](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03604).

### Inferences
- The practical state of the art for a JEPA world model in late 2026 is: (a) encoder trained with prediction + SIGReg (no EMA) or a frozen pretrained encoder; (b) action-conditioned transformer predictor trained with teacher forcing plus a short multi-step rollout loss; (c) sampling-based MPC (CEM/MPPI) on latent distance to a goal embedding. All three pieces transfer conceptually to (state, manipulated-variable) process data; none has been shown there at scale (Section 3).
- The compute barrier is no longer the argument against a vertical model: LeWM is 15M parameters on one GPU, HEPA (Section 3) fine-tunes 198K parameters. The barrier is data coverage and evaluation.
- Three warning signs recur across the JEPA authors' own papers: deterministic predictors, short horizons, and dependence on action-space coverage in offline data. GMP batch records are the opposite of high-coverage data: operators hold set-points inside validated ranges.
- LeWM's weakness on low-diversity environments and Cheung and He's finding on one-step error are the two results most likely to bite a "JEPA for batch manufacturing" project.

### Gaps
- OpenReview reviews of LeJEPA and LeWM were not retrieved (search budget exhausted), so reviewer criticism of SIGReg is not reported here. No independent replication of LeJEPA's 79% or of the Galaxy10 result was found.
- A reported "V-JEPA 2.1" follow-up and LeCun's post-Meta venture were not verified in this session and are omitted.
- I-JEPA's ImageNet linear-probe number, IWM's numbers, and LeWM's numeric planning table were not extracted.
- V-JEPA 2's appendix on camera-position sensitivity was not read.

## 3. JEPA for time series, signals, tabular, graph and scientific/physical data

### Takeaway
Outside vision the evidence is real but thin and mixed: latent prediction usually beats contrastive and autoregressive pretraining for classification-type readouts and is usually ahead of masked reconstruction in medical signals (ECG, fMRI), but masked autoencoders win on a non-trivial share of datasets, autoregressive models are better at short-horizon forecasting, and a physics-specific operator-learning method beat JEPA on physical-parameter estimation. The only JEPA papers on industrial process signals are small (hundreds of samples) or per-dataset; nothing was found on pharma/bioprocess or spectroscopy data.

### Cited Findings

**General time series**

- TS-JEPA — "Joint Embeddings Go Temporal", Sofiane Ennadir, Siavash Golkar, Leopoldo Sarra (KTH / NYU / Flatiron Institute), arXiv Sep 2025; presented at a NeurIPS 2024 workshop — [arXiv 2509.25449](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25449v1); [NeurIPS 2024 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/103043). Setup: 1D-CNN patch tokeniser (10 patches), 70% masking, transformer encoder, EMA target (0.998), L1 latent loss; tiny model (dim 128, 2 heads), same backbone for all baselines.
  - Classification accuracy (TS-JEPA / MAE / TS2Vec contrastive / autoregressive): FordA 91.5 / 85.1 / 86.4 / 69.6; FordB 73.8 / 59.6 / 72.4 / 61.9; FaultDetectionA 85.8 / 90.4 / 83.9 / 81.6; FaultDetectionB 50.6 / 54.3 / 53.9 / 51.4; ECG5000 89.5 / 91.6 / 86.9 / 87.5.
  - Short-term forecasting MSE (autoregressive vs JEPA): ETT-Small 0.009 vs 0.017; Electricity 0.010 vs 0.014; Weather 0.022 vs 0.015. Long-horizon rollouts: JEPA better on ETT and Electricity, not Weather (figure only).
  - Stated limitations: not consistently best; AR stronger short-term; no architecture ablation; no scaling study.
  - **[MIXED]**: JEPA wins 2 of 5 classification sets outright, MAE wins 2 (both of them the fault-detection and ECG sets), AR wins short-horizon forecasting.
- LaT-PFN — "LaT-PFN: A Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for In-context Time-series Forecasting", Stijn Verdenius, Andrea Zerio, Roy L.M. Wang, arXiv May 2024 (preprint). JEPA + prior-data fitted network; in-context learning in latent space on a normalised abstract time axis; claims "superior zero-shot predictions compared to established baselines". No numbers in abstract. **[MIXED]** (unverified magnitude) — [arXiv 2405.10093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10093).
- (2026) Pythia — "Pythia: Toward Foundation World Models for Multimodal Time Series", Xilin Dai et al., arXiv 4 Oct 2026. JEPA core with a stop-gradient numerical reference; a separate probabilistic decoder reads forecasts from the frozen representation; uses entity descriptions, events and covariates. Pythia-Tiny on the MUSE benchmark: normalised MASE 0.6879, WSQL 0.4269, about 6.26% and 5.00% better than the strongest published leaderboard entry. **[SUPPORTS]** and directly relevant: it separates latent-predictive representation learning from a probabilistic read-out, which is the pattern a process model with uncertainty needs — [arXiv 2610.05240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05240). Abstract only; no independent replication.
- (2026) Phys-JEPA — "Phys-JEPA: Physics-Informed Latent World Models for Multivariate Time-Series Forecasting", Weizhi Nie et al., arXiv Jun 2026 (preliminary v1). Splits latent state into physical and residual components and applies physical-consistency constraints to latent states and transitions. Gains over its own supervised baseline are small: Jena Climate H=24 MSE 0.12482 → 0.12273; Traffic H=192 0.800784 → 0.773873. No external baselines named in the abstract. **[MIXED]** — [arXiv 2606.16076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16076).

**Industrial / process / event-prediction sensor data**

- (2026) HEPA — "HEPA: A Self-Supervised Horizon-Conditioned Event Predictive Architecture for Time Series", Jonas Petersen et al. (ETH Zurich, Forgis, University of Vienna), arXiv May 2026, spotlight at an ICML 2026 workshop — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130).
  - Setup: causal transformer (d=256, 2 layers), patches of 16, JEPA pretraining with weight-shared target encoder and SIGReg (no EMA), L1 latent loss; horizon-conditioned MLP predictor; downstream the encoder is frozen and ~198K parameters are fine-tuned to output a monotone survival CDF over horizons. Same hyperparameters on all 14 datasets.
  - Benchmarks include C-MAPSS 1–4 (turbofan degradation), TEP (Tennessee Eastman chemical process), BATADAL (water-network cyberattack), GECCO (water quality), SMAP, PSM, MBA, ETTm1, Weather.
  - h-AUROC at 100% labels (HEPA / Chronos-2 / PatchTST / iTransformer / MAE): C-MAPSS-1 .81 / .66 / .80 / .70 / .69; C-MAPSS-3 .84 / .73 / .79 / .76 / .78; TEP 1.00 / n.r. / .99 / .93 / .96; GECCO .88 / .74 / .65 / .64 / .81; SMAP .59 / .54 / .49 / .47 / .64 (MAE wins); ETTm1 .81 / .74 / .53 / .79 / .87 (MAE wins); BATADAL .57 / .56 / .66 (PatchTST wins). HEPA best or tied on 10 of 14.
  - Label efficiency: C-MAPSS-1 keeps 98% of full-label h-AUROC with 10% of labels and 92% with 2% (2 of 85 engines).
  - Failure cases the authors report: wins drop to 6 of 14 at 10% labels (Chronos-2 .80 vs HEPA .39 on GECCO); RUL RMSE far worse than a supervised specialist on C-MAPSS (28.5 vs 12.2; 40.8 vs 20.0; 34.7 vs 12.7); chance-level (0.497) on seizure prediction; sensor-localised events are diluted by channel-fusion tokenisation; pretrained predictor weights add at most +0.003, i.e. the value is in the encoder; probability scale is distorted (calibration issue); each dataset gets its own encoder, so no cross-domain "foundation" transfer is shown.
  - Notes that point-adjusted F1 inflates anomaly-detection scores and uses a threshold-free metric instead.
  - **[SUPPORTS]** (a small latent-predictive model beats a general time-series foundation model, Chronos-2, on most industrial event tasks) but **[MIXED]** on precision tasks and calibration.
- "Learning Actionable World Models for Industrial Process Control", Peng Yan et al. (ZHAW, ETH/UZH, Kistler Instrumente AG), arXiv Mar 2025 (preprint) — [arXiv 2503.01411](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01411v1).
  - Real plant data: plastic injection moulding cavity-pressure curves. Datasets of 270 samples each (27 parameter settings × 10 cycles) for two products, plus 460 samples for pretraining.
  - Architecture: weight-shared CNN encoders to a 10-d latent; JEPA-style latent predictor ẑ_y = P(z_x, a); linear action predictor â = P_a(z_y − z_x); no negatives.
  - Results: training on the 8 corner settings and testing on the 19 interior settings gives an action-direction error of 18.22° / 12.14° (two products); pretraining roughly halves the error on one product but hurts on the other (negative transfer); the JEPA latent predictor contributes only a 2–5% gain; injection speed stays entangled with holding pressure.
  - Stated limitations: tailored to one machine setup; signal→parameter mapping is non-bijective; no closed-loop deployment; some corrective actions are not parameter changes (e.g. cleaning a sensor).
  - **[MIXED]** — the closest published analogue to "JEPA for batch manufacturing with control inputs", and it shows both feasibility with tiny data and how marginal the JEPA component can be at that scale.
- (2026) Fertig et al., JEPA-style self-supervised embeddings for anomaly detection on automotive object-state time series (nuScenes), arXiv Feb 2026 — [arXiv 2602.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09985) (search summary only). Not process data.

**Biomedical signals (the most mature non-vision JEPA evidence)**

- Brain-JEPA — "Brain-JEPA: Brain Dynamics Foundation Model with Gradient Positioning and Spatiotemporal Masking", Zijian Dong, Ruilin Li et al. (NUS), NeurIPS 2024 (spotlight per search summary) — [arXiv 2409.19407](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19407); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9c3828adf1500f5de3c56f6550dfe43c-Abstract.html). Pretrained on resting-state fMRI from 40,162 UK Biobank participants (450 ROIs × 160 timesteps), ViT-B, EMA target, cross-ROI / cross-time / double-cross masking. Versus BrainLM (a masked autoencoder), fine-tuned: UKB age ρ 0.718 vs 0.632, sex accuracy 88.17% vs 86.47%; external HCP-Aging sex accuracy 81.52% vs 74.39%; ADNI amyloid ± accuracy 71.00% vs 67.00%; Flanker MSE essentially tied (0.972 vs 0.971). Also better in linear probing (figure only). Authors' argument: reconstructing every value of a noisy signal makes it hard to separate noise from signal. **[SUPPORTS]**.
- ECG-JEPA — "Learning General Representation of 12-Lead Electrocardiogram With a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture", Sehun Kim (Seoul National University), arXiv Oct 2024 (preprint) — [arXiv 2410.08559](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08559v1). Pretrained on ~174K ECGs; ViT-B with EMA teacher; latent smooth-L1 loss. PTB-XL AUC (linear multi-label / fine-tune multi-class): ECG-JEPA 0.912 / 0.937–0.944 at 100 epochs vs ST-MEM (masked autoencoder, 800 epochs) 0.896 / 0.929; SimCLR 0.866 / 0.905; CMSC contrastive 0.802 / 0.877; CPC 0.620. Caveats: some baselines copied from another paper; no variance reported. **[SUPPORTS]** (better with 8× fewer epochs). A second ECG JEPA paper exists — [arXiv 2410.13867](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13867v1).
  - Independent check: a third-party benchmark ("Benchmarking ECG FMs: A Reality Check Across Clinical Tasks", arXiv Sep 2025) finds ECG-JEPA and ST-MEM close on MIMIC-based labels, e.g. sex AUROC about 0.904 vs 0.903 — [arXiv 2509.25095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25095) (search summary only). **[MIXED]**: the JEPA-over-MAE margin shrinks to noise under independent evaluation.
- S-JEPA (EEG), Guetschel, Moreau, Tangermann — [arXiv 2403.11772](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11772v2) (search summary only): spatial block masking for cross-dataset EEG transfer; spatial filtering and pretraining-window length matter, mask size does not. Headline accuracy not retrieved.

**Tabular, graph, point cloud**

- T-JEPA — "T-JEPA: Augmentation-Free Self-Supervised Learning for Tabular Data", Hugo Thimonier et al., ICLR 2025 — [arXiv 2410.05016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05016). Predicts the latent of one feature subset from another; introduces "regularization tokens" described as critical for training JEPAs on structured data; claims improvement over raw-feature training and that some T-JEPA-augmented deep models match or beat gradient-boosted trees. No head-to-head against a masked autoencoder in the retrieved text. **[SUPPORTS]** (augmentation-free SSL on the data type closest to batch-record features).
- Graph-JEPA — "Graph-level Representation Learning with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures", Geri Skenderi et al., TMLR (v3 Jan 2025) — [arXiv 2309.16014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16014). Predicts latents of masked subgraphs (targets placed on a 2-D unit hyperbola). Numbers not in abstract.
- Point-JEPA — "Point-JEPA: A Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for Self-Supervised Learning on Point Cloud", Ayumu Saito, Prachi Kudeshia, Jiju Poovvancheri, arXiv 2024–2025 — [arXiv 2404.16432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16432). Claims "competitive" results without input-space reconstruction. Numbers not in abstract. **[MIXED]** (parity).

**Physics / PDE**

- (2026) "Representation Learning for Spatiotemporal Physical Systems", Helen Qu, Rudy Morel, Michael McCabe, Alberto Bietti, François Lanusse, Shirley Ho, Yann LeCun, ICLR 2026 Workshop on AI & PDE — [arXiv 2603.13227](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13227v1). The cleanest head-to-head of objectives on physical data. Task: estimate governing physical parameters from frozen representations (attentive probe), three systems from The Well. MSE (JEPA with VICReg loss / VideoMAE / MPP autoregressive physics foundation model, fully fine-tuned / DISCO operator meta-learning): active matter 0.079 / 0.160 / 0.230 / 0.057; shear flow 0.38 / 0.67 / 0.59 / 0.13; Rayleigh–Bénard 0.13 / 0.18 / 0.08 / 0.01. With 10% of fine-tuning data on shear flow: JEPA 0.57 vs VideoMAE 0.98. Caveats: 6 pretraining epochs; three systems; one task. **[MIXED]**: latent prediction beats pixel reconstruction on all three and the autoregressive next-frame model on two of three (**supports** native latent objectives), but a physics-structured operator method beats JEPA on all three (**supports** "vertical/physics-informed" even more strongly than "JEPA").
- (2026) PDE-JEPA — "PDE-JEPA: Predictive Representation Learning of Latent Dynamics Modeling for Parametric PDEs", Zhentao Tan et al., arXiv Sep 2026 — [arXiv 2609.34715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34715). Finding: predictive representations keep rich physical information but "do not by themselves ensure accurate field evolution"; adds a geometry projector and a physics-structured predictor (parameter-independent evolution + parameter-dependent response). Claims average 33.4% in-distribution and 51.4% parameter-extrapolation improvement over prior state of the art on nine PDE benchmarks. Abstract only; baselines not named. **[SUPPORTS]** with an important caveat: the plain JEPA was not enough.
- (2026) "Toward Goal-Agnostic Joint-Embedding Predictive Control of Partial Differential Equations", Jonathan Gallagher and Roberto Guglielmi, arXiv Jul 2026 — [arXiv 2607.21644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21644). JEPA world model (small ViT + action-conditioned latent dynamics) trained offline without rewards, frozen, used by an MPPI controller on controlled 2D Navier–Stokes. A linear kinetic-energy probe on the frozen latent (R² = 0.989) can be used as the control objective: on three withheld targets, late-field RMSE falls 53% (0.0220 vs 0.0469) versus latent-L2 tracking; steady-state stabilisation at 2.7% mean relative error. Limitations: probe is brittle to measurement noise and missing pixels; one benchmark; only baseline is its own latent-L2 variant. **[SUPPORTS]** as an architecture template (control a physically meaningful probe of the latent, not the raw latent distance); weak as comparative evidence.
- (2026) PI-JEPA (operator-split latent prediction for multiphysics surrogates), Brandon Yee and Pairie Koh — claimed 1.9× lower error than FNO with 100 labelled runs, but the arXiv listing is **withdrawn** with the comment "Substantial Revision Required" — [arXiv 2604.01349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01349). Do not rely on it.
- (2026) SJEPA (symbolic-neural predictors; pendulum experiments) — [arXiv 2608.04060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04060) (search summary only).

**Theory relevant to noisy signals**

- "Learning by Reconstruction Produces Uninformative Features For Perception", Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun, arXiv Feb 2024 — [arXiv 2402.11337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11337). On TinyImageNet, the top pixel subspace explaining 90% of variance yields 45% accuracy while the bottom subspace explaining 20% of variance yields 55%; reconstruction spends capacity on high-variance, low-information directions; additive Gaussian-noise denoising never helps, masking helps depending on shape/ratio. **[SUPPORTS]**.
- "How JEPA Avoids Noisy Features: The Implicit Bias of Deep Linear Self Distillation Networks", Etai Littwin et al. (Apple), NeurIPS 2024 — [arXiv 2407.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03475); [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/implicit-bias). In deep linear networks JEPA objectives prioritise "influential" features (high regression coefficient) whereas masked reconstruction prioritises high-variance ones; the bias appears only with deep encoders. **[SUPPORTS]**.
- "Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures Focus on Slow Features", Vlad Sobal et al. (incl. Cho, LeCun), NeurIPS 2022 workshop — [arXiv 2211.10831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10831). JEPA (VICReg or SimCLR objectives) matches or beats pixel reconstruction when distractor noise changes every step but "fail[s] when the noise is fixed": static or slowly varying distractors are the most predictable thing and dominate the representation. **[UNDERMINES]** — see Inferences.

### Inferences
- A fair scorecard of head-to-heads on non-vision data: latent prediction vs masked reconstruction is roughly "JEPA ahead on fMRI and ECG (margin shrinking under independent benchmarking), split on generic time-series classification (2–2 with one loss to contrastive-level in TS-JEPA), ahead on 10 of 14 event-prediction sets (HEPA), ahead on physical-parameter estimation (Qu et al.)". Versus autoregressive pretraining: ahead for representation/classification, behind for short-horizon forecasting. This supports "the objective matters and next-step prediction is not the best representation learner for signals", not "JEPA dominates".
- The slow-features failure mode maps directly onto process data: sensor offsets, calibration drift, equipment identity, campaign/lot effects and slowly varying ambient conditions are the most predictable components of a batch trajectory. A JEPA trained naively on batch data may learn "which bioreactor and which probe" rather than the biology. Mitigations seen in the literature are action-conditioning (IWM, V-JEPA 2-AC), per-context instance normalisation (HEPA) and physics-structured latents (PDE-JEPA, Phys-JEPA).
- The Qu et al. and PDE-JEPA results point the same way: the winning recipe on physical systems is latent prediction plus domain structure (operators, parameter/evolution factorisation), which is an argument for vertical models but not for an off-the-shelf JEPA.
- HEPA's channel-fusion weakness (sensor-localised events get diluted) is a concrete design warning for multivariate plant data with hundreds of tags.

### Gaps
- No JEPA or latent-predictive paper was found on bioreactor, chromatography, crystallisation, tableting, or NIR/Raman spectroscopy data. The one chemical-process result is HEPA's Tennessee Eastman score (a 1990s simulator benchmark, saturated at 1.00).
- "MTS-JEPA" (codebook-regularised JEPA for multivariate anomaly detection) is cited by HEPA according to a search summary, but its paper was not located or read.
- No study was found that handles multi-rate data (fast online sensors + sparse offline assays) in a JEPA; Pythia's use of covariates and events is the nearest, abstract only.
- Numeric results for LaT-PFN, Graph-JEPA, Point-JEPA, S-JEPA and T-JEPA were not extracted.

## 4. Latent world models for control, and what has actually run on real plants

### Takeaway
Learned world models are state of the art for control in simulation and games (DreamerV3: 150+ tasks with one configuration; TD-MPC2: 104 tasks; Dreamer 4: Minecraft diamonds from offline data), and both reconstruction-based and decoder-free variants work. What has run on real industrial equipment is much plainer: neural-network MPC with heavy safety wrapping (Google data centres), simulator-trained RL transferred to hardware (tokamak, a distillation column), vendor closed-loop optimisers trained on historian data (Imubit), and one 8-day RL trial on a photobioreactor. No Dreamer/TD-MPC/JEPA-style latent world model was found running in pharmaceutical or chemical production.

### Cited Findings

**Algorithms (simulation / games / lab robots)**

- DreamerV3 — "Mastering diverse control tasks through world models", Danijar Hafner et al., Nature 2025 (arXiv 2023 as "Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models"). One fixed hyperparameter configuration outperforms specialised methods across over 150 tasks; first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula (first diamond at about 30M environment steps, roughly 17 days of play time) — [project page](https://danijar.com/project/dreamerv3/); [arXiv 2301.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104). **[MIXED]**: supports "learned world models for control", but DreamerV3's world model is trained with observation reconstruction (not re-verified on the fetched page), so it is evidence against "you must avoid generative objectives".
- Dreamer 4 — "Training Agents Inside of Scalable World Models", Danijar Hafner, Wilson Yan, Timothy Lillicrap, arXiv Sep 2025 — [arXiv 2509.24527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24527). World model trained mostly from unlabeled video with a "shortcut forcing" (diffusion-family) objective, real-time on one GPU; first agent to obtain Minecraft diamonds purely from offline data (sequences of over 20,000 mouse/keyboard actions from pixels); learns action conditioning from a small labelled fraction. Secondary summaries (unverified against the paper) give a 0.7% diamond success rate, 100× less data than OpenAI VPT, and 4 sampling steps matching 64-step diffusion forcing. **[UNDERMINES]** the JEPA-specific claim (a generative world model is the one that scaled to long-horizon offline control); **[SUPPORTS]** "offline world model + learn the policy inside it", which is the regime a GMP plant needs since exploration on the plant is not allowed.
- TD-MPC2 — "TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control", Nicklas Hansen, Hao Su, Xiaolong Wang, ICLR 2024 — [arXiv 2310.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828v2). Local trajectory optimisation in the latent space of an implicit, decoder-free world model; improves over baselines on 104 online RL tasks across DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, MyoSuite with one hyperparameter set; a single 317M-parameter agent handles 80 tasks across embodiments and action spaces; capability grows with model and data size. **[SUPPORTS]** (no reconstruction, continuous control, MPC — the closest algorithmic match to process control). Counter-note: scored 0.00 on DINO-WM's offline reward-free tasks — [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2).
- DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC, LeWM, JEPA-WMs: see Section 2 (real-robot zero-shot results exist only for V-JEPA 2-AC and the DROID evaluations).
- Dreamer and world-model methods in process control: a targeted search found no Dreamer or latent-world-model paper on a distillation column, reactor or bioreactor with plant deployment; 2025–2026 process-control RL benchmarks (e.g. a May 2026 PC-Gym study on a CSTR, four-tank system and extraction column versus nonlinear MPC) appear to use model-free PPO/SAC/DDPG/TD3 — [arXiv 2605.21211](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21211v1) (search summary only).

**What ran on real equipment**

- Google data-centre cooling (production). "Safety-first AI for autonomous data centre cooling and industrial control", DeepMind blog, 17 Aug 2018: a cloud AI reads sensors every five minutes, uses neural networks to predict the effect of candidate actions on energy use and selects actions that minimise energy subject to safety constraints, verified by a local control system before implementation; "now safely delivering energy savings in multiple Google data centres"; about 12% savings at launch rising to around 30% after nine months; eight safety mechanisms including uncertainty estimation (only high-confidence actions are executed), two-layer action verification, automatic failover and human override — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/safety-first-ai-for-autonomous-data-centre-cooling-and-industrial-control/). **[SUPPORTS]** (a learned predictive model + optimisation in closed loop on real plant; no LLM) — and shows the price: uncertainty estimates and a rule-based safety envelope.
- Commercial building cooling (live trial). "Controlling Commercial Cooling Systems Using Reinforcement Learning", Jerry Luo et al. (DeepMind/Google with Trane Technologies), arXiv Nov 2022: ensemble of neural networks plus a safety module, alternated with Trane's heuristic controller for three months in two buildings; approximately 9% and 13% energy savings while satisfying constraints — [arXiv 2211.07357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07357). **[SUPPORTS]**.
- Tokamak plasma (real hardware, simulator-trained). "Magnetic control of tokamak plasmas through deep reinforcement learning", Jonas Degrave et al., Nature 602, 2022: a single neural-network policy commands all 19 magnetic coils on TCV, trained entirely in a physics simulator and run "zero shot" on hardware in real time; produced elongated, negative-triangularity and snowflake shapes and two simultaneous "droplets" — [Nature / DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04301-9); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8850200). A later review notes the policy must act within 50 μs — [arXiv 2206.03846](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.03846) (search summary). Follow-up on another machine: "Reconstruction-free magnetic control of DIII-D plasma with deep reinforcement learning" — [arXiv 2506.13267](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13267) (title only). **[MIXED]**: non-LLM learned control works on a hard physical system, but the model was a first-principles simulator, not a learned latent world model.
- Chemical plant distillation (real plant, simulator-trained, model-free RL). Yokogawa and JSR, press release 22 Mar 2022: reinforcement-learning algorithm FKDPP (Factorial Kernel Dynamic Policy Programming) autonomously controlled a distillation column for 35 days (17 Jan – 21 Feb 2022, 840 hours), holding level and product quality while maximising waste-heat use; product met quality standards and was shipped; the controller was built on a plant simulator, validated against past operating data, and run with existing interlocks — [Yokogawa press release](https://www.yokogawa.com/news/press-releases/2022/2022-03-22/); later trade coverage: [Chemical Processing](https://www.chemicalprocessing.com/processing-equipment/separations-technology/article/33001627/full-scale-distillation-control-project-demonstrates-process-autonomy-using-artificial-intelligence). Vendor-reported. **[MIXED]** (no LLM, but no learned world model either).
- Refineries (vendor claims). Imubit, Sept 2024 launch announcement: "more than 90 closed-loop AI applications deployed" in large plants; a "Foundation Process Model" coupled with reinforcement learning; claims margin gains of US$0.30–0.50 per barrel and up to 30% lower natural gas use in rotary kilns; named operators include Marathon Petroleum, HF Sinclair, Citgo, Oxbow Calcining, Delek US — [Hydrocarbon Processing](https://www.hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2024/09/imubit-launches-closed-loop-ai-optimization-solution-powered-by-reinforcement-learning/). Trade case studies (vendor-authored): a debutaniser at Big West with a 2% throughput increase after an RL optimiser was trained on a learned process model — [PTQ Q3 2025](https://ptqmagazines.digitalrefining.com/PTQ-Q3-2025/59/); an FCC adaptive policy — [PTQ Catalysis 2026](https://ptqmagazines.digitalrefining.com/Catalysis-2026/48/) (search summaries). **[SUPPORTS]** commercially (a vertical, non-LLM, learned-dynamics-plus-RL product in closed loop) but with no independent audit and no published architecture.
- Bioprocess (real plant, short trial). "Reinforcement learning meets bioprocess control through behaviour cloning: Real-world deployment in an industrial photobioreactor", Juan D. Gil, Antonio Del Rio Chanona, José L. Guzmán, Manuel Berenguel, arXiv Sep 2025: RL agent for pH control pre-trained offline by behaviour cloning on PID trajectories, then fine-tuned online daily; in simulation IAE 8% lower than PID and 5% lower than standard off-policy RL, control effort 54% lower than PID; 8-day experimental validation; the authors call it, to their knowledge, the first industrial validation of an RL controller in a bioprocess — [arXiv 2509.06853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06853). **[MIXED]**: single-loop pH on an open algae reactor, model-free, improvements are single-digit over PID.
- Bioprocess (model-plus-RL, state of the art is unsettled). A 2025 proceedings paper quotes Monteiro and Kontoravdi that it is unclear how best to integrate models and RL and how to account for the plant-model mismatch that characterises bioprocesses — [CEUR-WS Vol-3970](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3970/SHORT4.pdf). A review of ML in biopharma process development describes fed-batch optimisation with differential dynamic programming and an MPC + learned value function approach validated on an industrial-scale penicillin bioreactor [whether real or the standard simulator benchmark was not verified] — [arXiv 2310.09991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991). General review: RL for chemical process control, Processes 2025 — [MDPI](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/processes/processes-13-01791/article_deploy/processes-13-01791.pdf).
- Crystallisation (simulation and bench only). Model-based RL for paracetamol cooling crystallisation, batch and continuous, compared against MPC — simulation (Benyahia et al., Loughborough, ESCAPE-31) — [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/chapter/Control_of_batch_and_continuous_crystallization_processes_using_reinforcement_learning/14995170/1); [USP CMKC](https://cmkc.usp.org/resources/503). Multi-agent RL and RL-tuned adaptive PID for aspirin and KDP crystallisation — [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/conference_contribution/Multi-agent_reinforcement_learning_and_RL-based_adaptive_PID_control_of_crystallization_processes/24378271). A bench-scale semi-batch antisolvent crystalliser (sodium chloride) controlled by RL with a CNN image sensor is attributed by a search summary to Manee, Baratti, Romagnoli (LSU, 2022) — [LSU repository](https://repository.lsu.edu/chem_engineering_pubs/481) (URL-to-claim mapping not individually verified). **[MIXED]**.

### Inferences
- Production pattern across every real deployment found: (1) a learned or first-principles predictive model, (2) an optimiser or policy on top, (3) a hard safety envelope and human override, (4) either a high-fidelity simulator or years of historian data. None uses an LLM in the loop, which supports the reader's "LLM + harness is not the control layer" intuition. But none uses a JEPA either; the world-model objective is not what made these work.
- The closest regime to GMP manufacturing is "offline data only, no exploration, policy learned inside the model" (Dreamer 4, DINO-WM, LeWM, Imubit's description). Every paper in that regime flags state-action coverage as the limiting factor.
- The size of real-world gains is modest where a tuned baseline exists (5–13% over PID/heuristics; 30% only against an un-optimised historical baseline). A founder should expect value from a vertical model to come from soft-sensing, early deviation detection and set-point advice rather than from displacing validated PID/MPC loops.
- For pharma specifically the evidence stops at bench-scale crystallisers and one photobioreactor pH loop.

### Gaps
- PlaNet, MuZero and Genie-type models were in scope but were not verified in this session (search budget exhausted); they are omitted rather than cited from memory.
- No public, peer-reviewed description of Imubit's model architecture or independent verification of its economics was found.
- No evidence was found of RL or learned-world-model control running in a GMP continuous pharmaceutical line or commercial-scale mammalian bioreactor. Absence of evidence here reflects a handful of searches, not an exhaustive review.
- DreamerV3's reconstruction-based RSSM design was not confirmed on the page fetched.

## 5. Objective and representation choices head-to-head: latent prediction vs reconstruction vs contrastive vs autoregressive vs diffusion, and tokenisation of continuous data

### Takeaway
Two things are well supported: (i) wrapping numeric series in a text LLM adds nothing measurable to forecasting, and (ii) discretising continuous values into a token vocabulary is being abandoned even by the groups that introduced it, in favour of patches with continuous or quantile heads. Whether the training objective should be latent-predictive rather than generative is less settled: latent prediction tends to give better frozen representations on noisy signals, but continuous-valued autoregression with diffusion heads and diffusion-style world models are state of the art for generation and for long-horizon offline control.

### Cited Findings

**LLMs on numeric time series**

- "Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?", Mingtian Tan, Mike Merrill, Vinayak Gupta, Tim Althoff, Thomas Hartvigsen, NeurIPS 2024 (spotlight). Ablating three LLM-based forecasters (OneFitsAll, Time-LLM, CALF) on eight datasets: removing the LLM or replacing it with a basic attention layer does not degrade forecasting and in most cases improves it; pretrained LLMs do no better than training from scratch, do not represent sequential dependencies in the series, and do not help few-shot; simple patching + attention performs similarly at far lower cost. Authors caution this does not mean LLMs will never be useful — [arXiv 2406.16964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96085). **[SUPPORTS]** the "LLM is not the right numeric backbone" half of the thesis.

**Tokenising continuous values**

- Chronos (2024) tokenised real values by scaling and quantisation into a fixed vocabulary and trained a T5 model with cross-entropy; its successors dropped that: Chronos-Bolt uses input patching and directly predicts quantiles (output patch length 64), and Chronos-2 (arXiv Oct 2025) is a patch-based encoder-only model with group attention unifying univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting; a 2026 paper states Chronos-2 and Chronos-Bolt "do not use discretization" or next-token prediction. TimesFM and Moirai are also patch-based — [Chronos-2, arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1); [UniCA, arXiv 2506.22039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.22039); [arXiv 2606.09861](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09861v1) (search summaries). **[SUPPORTS]** native continuous representations; note Chronos-2's encoder-only, non-next-token design is closer to masked/latent modelling than to an LLM.
- "Autoregressive Image Generation without Vector Quantization" (MAR), Tianhong Li, Yonglong Tian, He Li, Mingyang Deng, Kaiming He, NeurIPS 2024 (spotlight): replaces the categorical cross-entropy over VQ tokens with a per-token "Diffusion Loss" (a small MLP denoiser conditioned on the autoregressive model's output vector); best model approaches FID 1.55 on ImageNet 256×256; a fast configuration runs under 0.3 s per image with FID below 2.0 — [arXiv 2406.11838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11838); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/66e226469f20625aaebddbe47f0ca997-Abstract.html). **[MIXED]**: undermines "autoregression is the problem" (the problem was discrete tokens); supports "native continuous-valued representation with a distributional head".

**Latent prediction vs reconstruction vs contrastive vs autoregressive (signals and physical data)** — summarised from Sections 2 and 3:

- Hybrids are appearing rather than one side winning: Pythia pairs a JEPA representation with a separate probabilistic decoder — [arXiv 2610.05240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05240); PDE-JEPA adds geometry alignment and structured predictors because predictive latents alone did not give accurate field evolution — [arXiv 2609.34715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34715); JEPA-WM authors suggest "latent diffusion" predictors for stochastic environments — [arXiv 2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3).

### Inferences
- The strongest sub-claim in the reader's thesis is "native (non-text-token) representations": it is supported from three independent directions (Tan et al.; the Chronos lineage abandoning quantised vocabularies; MAR). It does not depend on JEPA being right.
- The "objective" sub-claim should be stated narrowly: for learning a state representation from noisy multivariate signals that will feed classification, soft-sensing, anomaly/event prediction or planning, latent prediction is at least competitive with and often better than reconstruction and clearly better than contrastive or next-step objectives; for producing calibrated numeric forecasts or sampling trajectories, a generative/distributional head is still needed.
- A sensible architecture for process data therefore has two parts: latent-predictive (JEPA/SIGReg) pretraining of the state encoder and action-conditioned dynamics, plus a distributional read-out (quantile, diffusion or flow-matching head) for observable quantities — which is what Pythia, MAR-style heads and Chronos-2's quantile outputs each implement in part.

### Gaps
- No single study was found that compares JEPA, MAE, contrastive, autoregressive and diffusion pretraining on the same industrial process dataset; TS-JEPA (four objectives, tiny models, generic datasets) and Qu et al. (physics) are the nearest.
- No ablation isolating quantised tokens versus continuous patches on identical time-series data was found.
- Flow-matching and diffusion heads for time-series/process data were in scope but not searched before the budget ran out.

## 6. Honest gaps: what would be hard about a "JEPA for batch manufacturing", including uncertainty quantification

### Takeaway
The hard parts are not compute but (a) low-diversity, low-coverage data, where SIGReg-style regularisers and offline world models are known to struggle; (b) slow/static nuisance features that latent prediction preferentially learns; (c) evaluation, since latent loss does not track control performance and there is no reconstruction to inspect; and (d) uncertainty, since standard JEPAs are deterministic point predictors — probabilistic JEPA variants only appeared in 2026 and are validated on MNIST-scale or tabular tasks.

### Cited Findings

**Small data and low diversity**

- LeWM's own stated failure: in low-diversity, low-intrinsic-dimension environments SIGReg cannot match the high-dimensional Gaussian prior and latents become less structured; planning also depends on offline datasets with sufficient coverage — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- DINO-WM: "requires offline datasets with sufficient state-action coverage" — [arXiv 2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v2). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- Industrial JEPA with 270 samples per product: negative transfer from pretraining on one of two products; latent predictor adds only 2–5%; the signal→parameter map is non-bijective — [Yan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01411v1). **[MIXED]**.
- HEPA: with 10% of labels wins drop from 10/14 to 6/14 and a general time-series foundation model (Chronos-2) wins by a wide margin on one dataset (.80 vs .39); encoders are trained per dataset, so cross-plant transfer is undemonstrated — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130). **[MIXED]**.
- Counter-evidence that small in-domain SSL can work: LeJEPA in-domain pretraining on Galaxy10 beats DINOv2/v3 transfer (architecture-confounded) — [arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3); HEPA keeps 92% of performance with 2 labelled engines out of 85 on C-MAPSS-1 — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130); PDE fine-tuning with 10% data favours JEPA over MAE (0.57 vs 0.98 MSE) — [arXiv 2603.13227](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13227v1). **[SUPPORTS]**.

**Non-stationarity and nuisance structure**

- JEPA focuses on slow features and fails with fixed distractors — [Sobal et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10831). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- Sensor-localised events are diluted by channel-fusion tokenisation (HEPA's explanation for its losses) — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- V-JEPA 2-AC is sensitive to observation geometry (camera placement), an analogue of sensor placement/calibration changes — [arXiv 2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1). **[UNDERMINES]**.

**Lack of augmentations / views**

- Masking-based JEPAs need no hand-crafted augmentations (I-JEPA, T-JEPA, ECG-JEPA) — [I-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243); [T-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05016). **[SUPPORTS]**. But LeJEPA's recipe uses multiple views (2 global + 6 local crops) — [arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3) — and LLM-JEPA requires naturally paired views — [arXiv 2509.14252](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14252v2); T-JEPA needed special "regularization tokens" — [arXiv 2410.05016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05016). **[MIXED]**: for process data the views must be designed (time blocks, sensor groups, future-from-past with actions).

**Evaluation**

- One-step latent prediction error does not predict planning success (r = -0.010 after the first epoch) — [Cheung and He 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19375). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- HEPA: correlation between pretraining loss and downstream score holds within some datasets but is r = -0.05 pooled across datasets; point-adjusted F1 inflates anomaly scores — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- LeJEPA claims training loss tracks linear-probe accuracy (about 85% Spearman, ~99% with scaling) — [arXiv 2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08544v3). **[SUPPORTS]** for representation quality, not control.
- A latent loss "does not guarantee consistency with observations" (Theorem 2) — [Xing et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05169v3). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- Physical-quantity probes on frozen latents are the working evaluation tool: linear probes recover position/angle in LeWM (r 0.90–0.99) — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312); a kinetic-energy probe (R² 0.989) serves as a control objective in PDE control, but is brittle to measurement noise and missing data — [arXiv 2607.21644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21644). **[MIXED]**.

**Uncertainty quantification**

- Standard JEPA world models are deterministic: the MSE-trained predictor "learns the conditional mean of potentially multimodal futures"; stochastic environments "would need stochastic extensions such as latent variable injection or latent diffusion" — [Terver et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3). **[UNDERMINES]**.
- (2026) Var-JEPA — "Var-JEPA: A Variational Formulation of the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture", Göğl and Yau (Oxford / Health Data Research UK), arXiv Mar 2026: derives JEPA's predictor as a learned conditional prior in a latent-variable model whose ELBO also discourages collapse; per-sample uncertainty from the target-latent posterior variance tracks input noise on MNIST, and rejecting high-uncertainty samples improves accuracy (risk–coverage curves); a tabular variant (Var-T-JEPA) "consistently improves over T-JEPA" — [arXiv 2603.20111](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20111) (search summary only). **[SUPPORTS]** in principle; evidence is small-scale.
- (2026) VJEPA — "VJEPA: Variational Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures as Probabilistic World Models", Yongchao Huang, arXiv Jan 2026, ICML 2026 poster: learns predictive distributions over future latents with a latent-space variational objective, no observation likelihood; links to Bayesian filtering and predictive state representations; credible intervals by sampling; BJEPA extension composes learned dynamics with goals/constraints — [arXiv 2601.14354](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14354); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61579) (search summary only). **[SUPPORTS]** in principle; no industrial validation.
- (2026) Pythia separates predictive representation learning from a probabilistic forecasting decoder and reports a probabilistic metric (WSQL 0.4269) — [arXiv 2610.05240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05240). **[SUPPORTS]**.
- Real deployments needed explicit uncertainty: Google's data-centre controller executes only high-confidence actions (uncertainty estimation is one of its eight safety mechanisms) and the Trane system used an ensemble of networks — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/safety-first-ai-for-autonomous-data-centre-cooling-and-industrial-control/); [arXiv 2211.07357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07357).
- HEPA reports that its training loss distorts the probability scale (calibration) — [arXiv 2605.11130](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11130). A paper titled "Beyond Accuracy: Are Time Series Foundation Models Well-Calibrated?" exists — [arXiv 2510.16060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16060) (title only, not read).

**Collapse and heuristics**

- EMA/stop-gradient JEPAs are prone to collapse (LeWM's statement; ICLR 2026 "early collapse" paper); SIGReg removes the heuristics but adds a Gaussian-latent assumption — [arXiv 2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312); [ICLR 2026 listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10018046); [LeJEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544). **[MIXED]**.

### Inferences
- A concrete adaptation blueprint that is consistent with the evidence above (inference, not a tested design):
  1. Tokenise per sensor-group and per time-patch, keeping channel identity (HEPA's channel-fusion failure) and using per-context normalisation; give sparse offline assays and events their own tokens with time stamps (Pythia's covariate/event idea).
  2. Train the encoder with future-latent prediction + SIGReg (LeJEPA/LeWM/HEPA) rather than EMA; condition the predictor on manipulated variables and recipe set-points (V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM), with a 2–6-step rollout loss (Terver et al.).
  3. Counter the slow-feature bias explicitly: condition on or adversarially remove equipment/lot identity, and include physics-structured latent components where a mechanistic model exists (PDE-JEPA, Phys-JEPA, DISCO's win in Qu et al.).
  4. Attach a separate probabilistic read-out (quantile/diffusion head, or a variational predictor as in VJEPA/Var-JEPA) for CQAs and soft-sensor targets; use ensembles for epistemic uncertainty as the deployed DeepMind systems did.
  5. Evaluate by probing physically meaningful quantities and by downstream decision metrics, never by latent loss alone (Cheung and He).
  6. For control, use the model for advisory MPC inside validated ranges behind the existing control and interlock layer; every real deployment found kept that envelope.
- The biggest unaddressed risk is coverage: GMP batches cluster tightly around a validated set-point, so the action-conditioned predictor sees almost no action variation. Development/DoE runs, scale-down models and mechanistic simulators are the only places where the necessary variation exists — which pushes toward hybrid (simulator-pretrained, plant-fine-tuned) world models, as in the tokamak and Yokogawa cases.
- Regulatory explainability is easier for a latent model with linear probes onto named physical quantities than for raw latent distances, but harder than for a reconstruction model whose predictions can be overlaid on sensor traces; a decoder head is therefore worth having even if it is not used for training the encoder (DINO-WM trains one only for visualisation).

### Gaps
- No paper was found that discusses JEPA or latent world models in the context of GMP validation, ICH Q8–Q14, process analytical technology or regulatory explainability; the regulatory points above are inferences.
- No paper was found on conformal prediction or calibration specifically for JEPA latents.
- No study was found on JEPA under distribution shift from raw-material lot changes, scale-up or equipment transfer.
- Var-JEPA and VJEPA details come from search summaries; their numeric results were not read.

## 7. Other non-LLM paradigms worth knowing (brief evidence only)

### Takeaway
None of these alternatives has evidence comparable to latent world models for control. Energy-based transformers are the most interesting conceptually (verification-by-optimisation at inference, ICLR 2026 oral) but cost several times more to train and are shown only at small scale; KANs lose fair comparisons to MLPs except on symbolic-formula fitting; continuous-time models (neural CDEs, closed-form liquid networks) are well matched to irregular, multi-rate sensor data but have not scaled into foundation models.

### Cited Findings

- **Energy-Based Transformers.** "Energy-Based Transformers are Scalable Learners and Thinkers", Alexi Gladstone et al. (10 authors incl. Yilun Du, Heng Ji), arXiv Jul 2025; ICLR 2026 (oral listing). Learns an energy over (context, candidate prediction) pairs and predicts by gradient descent on the energy; claims up to 35% higher scaling rate than Transformer++ across data, batch size, parameters, FLOPs and depth; 29% more improvement from extra inference-time "thinking" on language tasks; outperforms Diffusion Transformers on image denoising with 99% fewer forward passes (ICLR abstract) — [arXiv 2507.02092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02092); [ICLR 2026 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/oral/10008833). Criticism: training requires 3.3–6.6× the FLOPs of a standard transformer; results are at relatively small model sizes with no comparison to leading reasoning models — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/?p=25321) (search summary). **[MIXED]**: supports "alternatives to plain autoregression exist and scale", but unproven beyond small models and not tested on sensor/process data.
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks.** "KAN or MLP: A Fairer Comparison", Runpeng Yu, Weihao Yu, Xinchao Wang (NUS), arXiv Jul 2024: with parameters and FLOPs matched, MLP generally outperforms KAN on machine learning, vision, audio and NLP tasks; KAN is ahead only on symbolic formula representation; KAN's claimed continual-learning advantage does not hold — [arXiv 2407.16674](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16674v2). Further critiques: "Critical Assessment of Claims, Performance, and Practical Viability" — [arXiv 2407.11075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11075); "MLPs and KANs for data-driven learning in physical problems: A performance comparison" (MLPs benefit from depth, stacked KAN layers do not gain comparably) — [arXiv 2504.11397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11397); (2026) KAN vs MLP/GNN for aerodynamic prediction finds MLP slightly but systematically better — [arXiv 2606.27126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27126) (search summaries). **[UNDERMINES]** KAN as a foundation for a vertical model; possibly useful for interpretable symbolic sub-models.
- **Neural controlled differential equations.** "Neural Controlled Differential Equations for Irregular Time Series", Patrick Kidger, James Morrill, James Foster, Terry Lyons, NeurIPS 2020 (spotlight): continuous-time analogue of an RNN that can incorporate later observations (unlike a neural ODE), operates directly on partially observed, irregularly sampled multivariate series, uses memory-efficient adjoint backpropagation, is a universal approximator, and reports state-of-the-art results against ODE- and RNN-based baselines — [arXiv 2005.08926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08926). **[SUPPORTS]** (a native representation for multi-rate, irregular plant data), small-scale evidence.
- **Liquid / closed-form continuous-time networks.** "Closed-form Continuous-time Neural Models", Ramin Hasani et al. (MIT; Daniela Rus), Nature Machine Intelligence 4, 992–1003 (2022): closed-form approximation of liquid time-constant dynamics removes the ODE solver and is "between one and five orders of magnitude faster" in training and inference than ODE-based counterparts, with strong time-series modelling versus advanced recurrent models — [arXiv 2106.13898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13898). Liquid AI's commercial LFM2 models are language/vision-language models evaluated on text benchmarks with the company's internal suite (e.g. self-reported 79.56% IFEval, 82.41% GSM8K for LFM2-2.6B); the Artificial Analysis listing marked its intelligence score as an estimate pending independent evaluation — [Liquid AI blog](https://liquid.ai/blog/liquid-foundation-models-v2-our-second-series-of-generative-ai-models); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/providers/liquid-ai) (search summaries). **[MIXED]**: the academic continuous-time idea fits sensor data; the commercial "liquid" foundation models are efficient LLMs, not process models.
- **State-space models (Mamba).** "Is Mamba Effective for Time Series Forecasting?" (S-Mamba), Wang et al., arXiv Mar 2024: per-variate tokenisation, bidirectional Mamba across variates; authors report leading accuracy on thirteen public datasets with lower GPU memory and training time — [arXiv 2403.11144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11144). Authors' claim, not independently checked. Counter-note: Mamba failed alongside Transformers on Bachmann and Nagarajan's planning task under teacher forcing — [arXiv 2403.06963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963). **[MIXED]**: a backbone choice, orthogonal to the objective question.

### Inferences
- These paradigms are better treated as components than as competing foundations: continuous-time encoders (neural CDE / CfC) for irregular multi-rate inputs, Mamba or transformers as the sequence backbone, energy-style inference for verification/planning. The choice of training objective and of physical structure (Sections 3 and 5) has more evidence behind it than the choice among these.
- Energy-based inference (optimise the prediction to minimise a learned compatibility score) is the same computational pattern as JEPA planning by optimisation and classical MPC; its cost profile (several times the training FLOPs, iterative inference) is acceptable for minute-scale process control but unproven there.

### Gaps
- Hyperdimensional computing and neuromorphic approaches were in scope but not searched before the budget ran out; no evidence is reported.
- No evidence was found of energy-based transformers, KANs or liquid networks applied to pharmaceutical or chemical process data in this session.
- Independent benchmarks for Liquid AI models on sensor/time-series tasks were not found.
