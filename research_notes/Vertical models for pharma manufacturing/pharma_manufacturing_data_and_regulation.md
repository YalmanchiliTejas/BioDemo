## 1. Data modalities and their properties: what must a model represent, and what is or is not token-like?

### Takeaway
The physical-process core of pharma manufacturing is multi-rate, multivariate, continuous trajectory data with sparse, delayed, noisy lab labels and very few batches per product ("Low-N"); the quality-system layer (deviations, CAPA, SOPs, batch-record narrative, recipes) is discrete and text-like. Published sources document the first group as badly matched to off-the-shelf sequence models and the second as a natural fit for language models; some knowledge (operator tacit knowledge) is not recorded at all.

### Cited Findings

Continuous process and PAT data (trajectories, spectra)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Bioprocess runs are long and expensive, with sparse labels: "For new biopharmaceutical products, there are limited number of bioreactor runs because of the high cost. Each cell culture experiment usually runs over a two weeks period, where off-line measurements such as nutrient and metabolite concentrations are daily measured." Offline measurements "are conducted infrequently (e.g., every 12-24 hours)". — Khuat, Bassett, Otte, Grevis-James, Gabrys (UTS / CSL Innovation), "Applications of Machine Learning in Biopharmaceutical Process Development and Manufacturing: Current Trends, Challenges, and Opportunities", arXiv:2310.09991, 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Multi-rate heterogeneity is a named obstacle: bioprocess data have a "high level of heterogeneity with regard to the measurement frequency. Many ML algorithms are not designed to work well on heterogeneous data collected over different sampling time intervals"; workarounds are algorithm modification or dynamic time warping. — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** PAT spectra are high-dimensional relative to the number of runs: with spectrometers "the number of dimensions of the obtained data can be thousands of variables as in the Raman and infrared spectroscopy data", producing the curse of dimensionality with "a limited number of training samples". — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Label sparsity example: in one cited simulation, labels were available only every eight hours while samples were taken every 0.5 h; the "Low-N" problem is when "the number of training samples is much smaller than the data dimensions". — Peng, Khuat, Musial, Gabrys, "Machine Learning Methods for Small Data and Upstream Bioprocessing Applications: A Comprehensive Review", Biotechnology Advances / arXiv:2506.12322, 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12322v2)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Data scarcity is called structural: "The high cost and time investment required for each bioreactor run restrict the amount of data"; "offline analysis is often performed infrequently due to the high labour cost." The same review cites the Chinchilla scaling result (roughly 20 text tokens per parameter) to argue bioprocess data are small relative to large-model appetites. — Peng et al. 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12322v2)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Reference measurements themselves are noisy: "VCD and TCD measurements from the cell counting instrument are notoriously bad, particularly towards the end of a run", which affects cross-scale model transferability. Datasets also show values missing at random because some parameters are offline. — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[MIXED]** Downstream is under-instrumented: "online or at-line monitoring tools are not widely used in downstream processing, especially in chromatography, and only a few examples have been presented in the literature". (Less native data exists to pretrain on; chromatography traces remain mostly offline/at-line.) — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Site transfers reset the data clock: "products are regularly moved to various production sites ... there is a limited number of historical experiments available at new manufacturing facilities." — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** A 2026 peer-reviewed review states that bioprocess development is "still limited by a lack of data and data heterogeneity" and that "data sets obtained at different scales or under varying operating conditions are often inconsistent, hindering data integration and model transferability across systems." — Barón Díaz, Drommershausen, Grünberger, Holtmann (KIT), "Transfer Learning Approaches in Bioprocess Engineering: Opportunities and Challenges", Biotechnology and Bioengineering, 2026, DOI 10.1002/bit.70186 — [Source](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000193214/181017391)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Regulators expect continuous-process dynamics to be represented explicitly: residence time distribution "is specific to the process, composition/formulation, material properties, equipment design and configuration", and "A process model is specific to system design and configuration and relevant material properties." ICH Q13 also defines soft sensors as "A model that is used in lieu of physical measurement to estimate a variable or attribute ... based on measured data". — ICH Q13 "Continuous manufacturing of drug substances and drug products", Step 5, EMA/6401/2023 — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/ich-guideline-q13-continuous-manufacturing-drug-substances-drug-products-step-5_en.pdf)

Data volume, raw-record retention and integrity
- **[MIXED]** FDA expects data volume and type to grow and flags record-keeping consequences: "The increase in data may be in terms of both the frequency and the types of data recorded ... applicants may need additional clarity for data sampling rates, data compression, or other data management approaches to ensure that an accurate record of the drug manufacturing process is maintained." It also notes "challenges in storing the data in a structured manner that enables retrieval and analysis". — FDA CDER, "Artificial Intelligence in Drug Manufacturing" discussion paper, 2023 — [Source](https://www.fda.gov/media/165743/download)
- **[MIXED]** Data integrity (ALCOA) applies across the whole CGMP data life cycle, "including in the creation, modification, processing, maintenance, archival, retrieval, transmission, and disposition of data"; an audit trail is "a secure, computer-generated, time-stamped electronic record that allows for reconstruction of the course of events relating to the creation, modification, or deletion of an electronic record"; "relationships between data and their metadata should be preserved in a secure and traceable manner." (Any model pipeline that resamples, compresses, tokenises or summarises GMP data sits under these rules; it constrains both LLM and non-LLM architectures.) — FDA, "Data Integrity and Compliance With Drug CGMP: Questions and Answers", December 2018 — [Source](https://www.fda.gov/media/119267/download)
- **[MIXED]** The EU draft revisions of Chapter 4 and Annex 11 (consultation 7 July to 7 October 2025) add provisions for automated data capture, metadata, version control and ALCOA+-aligned traceability, per a consultancy summary. — QbD Group (Ward Neefs), "Inside the EudraLex Annex 22...", 31 October 2025 — [Source](https://qbdgroup.com/en/blog/eudralex-annex-22-whats-new-for-ai-in-pharma); consultation page: European Commission — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/consultations/stakeholders-consultation-eudralex-volume-4-good-manufacturing-practice-guidelines-chapter-4-annex_en)

Structured, discrete, hierarchical data (recipes, batch records, events)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Recipes, equipment capabilities, procedures and batch production records already have standard discrete, hierarchical schemas: ISA-88 Part 2 defines how "recipes, equipment capabilities, and control procedures" are organised and exchanged, Part 3 general/site recipe models, Part 4 a standard model for batch production records. (Symbolic, schema-bound data of this kind is readily serialisable for language models.) — ISA, "ISA-88 Series of Standards" page — [Source](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-88-standards)
- **[UNDERMINES]** ISA-95 / IEC 62264 defines the Level 0–4 hierarchy (physical process, sensing/actuation, supervisory control, MES/operations management, ERP) and standard objects and attributes exchanged between Level 3 and Level 4. — ISA, "ISA-95 Standard" page — [Source](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-95-standard)
- **[MIXED]** Real batch records are frequently still paper or scans with handwriting: an LLM/VLM pipeline for batch manufacturing records reports handwritten entries at roughly 85% accuracy and step-level accuracy of 75.09–82.72% on three real records (15, 44, 66 pages), with hierarchy/sequence/calculation fidelity reported at 100%. — Agarwal, Bendre, Rojkova (MasterControl AI Research), "From Paper to Structured JSON: An Agentic AI Workflow for Compliant BMR Digital Transformation", arXiv:2601.04368 / EACL 2026 Industry Track (vendor-authored research) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04368v1); [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-industry.3.pdf)

Text (deviations, CAPA, complaints)
- **[UNDERMINES]** FDA itself lists text mining as a manufacturing AI use: "AI can be used to examine consumer complaints and deviation reports containing large volumes of text to identify cluster problem areas and prioritize areas for continual improvement." — FDA CDER discussion paper, 2023 — [Source](https://www.fda.gov/media/165743/download)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Lilly's Quality group describes deviation records as "a large volume of deviations with rich free-text descriptions — too many to manually review for hidden patterns", where "traditional trending relies on pre-defined categories selected by authors — subtle cross-category themes are missed." — Mary Coyne (Quality Advisor, Eli Lilly), "Applying AI/ML to Deviation Trend Analysis", Trinity College Dublin QP Forum 2026 slides — [Source](https://www.tcd.ie/media/tcd/pharmacy/images/qp-forums/qp-forum-2026/presentations/3.-Innovation--Advancing-GMP---Applying-AIML-to-Deviation-Trend-Analysis.pdf)

Silos
- **[MIXED]** One vendor case study describes a single biologics drug-substance facility whose relevant data sat in "3 siloed systems": DeltaV batch historian, IP21 time-series sensor data, SAP batch genealogy, plus lab sample readings and 1,000+ pages of operating documents. (vendor claim) — C3 AI, "Pharmaceutical Company Increases Yield in Batch Manufacturing with AI Insights" — [Source](https://c3.ai/customers/pharmaceutical-company-increases-yield-in-batch-manufacturing-with-ai-insights)
- **[MIXED]** DataHow's CEO: many organisations have siloed, inconsistent process data and "no model can compensate for that." (sponsored article, vendor claim) — AIChE ChEnected, "Data-Driven Bioprocessing: A Conversation With DataHow's Alessandro Butté", 28 April 2026 — [Source](https://chenected.aiche.org/2026/04/data-driven-bioprocessing-conversation-datahows-alessandro-butte)
- **[MIXED]** A vendor survey of 124 bioprocessing professionals (mid-2026) reports 68% citing poor data availability and 70% lack of internal expertise as barriers; 70% report a gap between expected and actual AI performance. (vendor claim; survey method not published) — Omri Schanin (Algocell), "Can GenAI transform biomanufacturing? It's complicated", Protein Production Technology International, 2 September 2026 — [Source](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/can-genai-transform-biomanufacturing-its-complicated)
- **[MIXED]** Invert claims bioprocess teams spend "over 50% of their time managing data." (vendor claim, no methodology) — Invert homepage — [Source](https://www.invertbio.com/)

Unrecorded knowledge
- **[MIXED]** Regulators at the EMA Annex 22 workshop noted "that GMP often uses tacit knowledge and experience to create controls beyond formally identified risks"; the industry answer was that risk assessments "are limited by available knowledge and must be updated through lifecycle learning, deviations and defects." (Tacit knowledge is unavailable to any model class unless captured; this cuts against both "more data" and "native model" claims.) — EMA, "Report - Multistakeholder workshop on expert contributions to artificial intelligence guidance development (Annex 22)", EMA/156789/2026, published 2 October 2026 — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)

### Inferences
Analyst classification of modalities (my synthesis; the properties come from the sources above, the "representation" column is inference):

| Modality | Natural structure | Token-like? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process historian (pH, DO, temperature, flows, agitation; setpoints vs measured vs manipulated) | Continuous multivariate trajectories, multi-rate, causal (input to state to output) | No | Setpoint/MV/PV roles and control-loop causality are not carried by a flat sequence; see TSFM evidence in section 7 |
| PAT spectra (Raman, NIR), FBRM distributions, chromatograms | Continuous functions / fields (thousands of channels per time point) | No | Calibrated against sparse offline references |
| Offline assays, CQAs, release tests | Sparse, delayed, noisy scalars with measurement uncertainty | Partly (as numbers) | Value is in uncertainty and timing, which text encodings lose unless explicitly modelled |
| MES/eBR events, alarms, audit trails, recipe execution | Discrete timestamped event sequences in ISA-88/95 hierarchy | Yes | Most "language-like" process data |
| Recipes, master batch records, specifications | Hierarchical symbolic structures | Yes | Schema-bound; good LLM fit with validation |
| Raw-material lots, genealogy, equipment topology/train | Graphs | Serialisable but graph-native | Queries are graph traversals; LLMs can call graph tools |
| Environmental monitoring, equipment state/maintenance | Mixed: time series plus event logs and work orders | Mixed | |
| Deviations, CAPA, change control, SOPs, validation documents, regulatory filings | Natural-language text | Yes | Clear LLM territory |
| Operator tacit knowledge | Unrecorded | N/A | Not addressable by any model without capture |

- The data-scarcity evidence cuts in two directions. It supports "general LLMs + more data is not enough" (there is not much more data, and it is not text). It also undermines a naive "vertical foundation model" bet, because a per-product corpus of tens of runs is far too small to pretrain on; a vertical model would need cross-company, cross-product data that the sources describe as siloed, inconsistent and legally bound to each manufacturer.
- The modalities where most GMP labour is spent on documents (deviations, batch record review, CAPA, change control) are text-native. The thesis is weakest there.
- ALCOA and raw-data requirements imply that any learned representation (embedding, token, latent) is a derived record; the original time-stamped data and metadata must remain the system of record and be traceable to model inputs. That is architecture-neutral.

### Gaps
- No verified source for typical number of commercial batches per product per year, or historian tag counts/sampling rates per unit operation. Khuat et al. and Peng et al. describe "Low-N" qualitatively only. A Deloitte document reportedly trained a yield model on 1,500 batches (search snippet only, vendor) — not opened.
- BioPhorum, ISPE Pharma 4.0 and digital-plant-maturity survey data on silos and paper batch records were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- No source retrieved specifically on FBRM, environmental-monitoring data structure, or equipment maintenance (CMMS) data in GMP plants.
- Tulsyan, Garvin, Ündey (Amgen) work on the "Low-N" problem (Biotechnology and Bioengineering, DOI 10.1002/bit.26605) is cited inside Khuat et al. but the publisher page returned HTTP 403; not independently verified here.

## 2. The incumbent non-LLM toolbox and how well it works (MSPC/PLS, chemometrics, DoE/design space, mechanistic and hybrid models, MPC, soft sensors, RTRT, CPV)

### Takeaway
The incumbent toolbox is explicitly embedded in regulatory guidance (PAT 2004, ICH Q8 design space and RTRT, ICH Q13 process models and soft sensors) and works well inside its calibration domain, but the literature documents recurring limits: poor extrapolation, scale/site/cell-line transfer failures for some analytes, recalibration after equipment change, and ongoing model maintenance. The best-documented remedy is hybrid (mechanistic + data-driven) modelling and transfer learning, not language models.

### Cited Findings

Regulatory embedding of the incumbent methods
- **[UNDERMINES]** FDA's PAT framework (2004) names "Multivariate tools for design, data acquisition and analysis", process analyzers, process control tools and knowledge-management tools as the PAT toolset, and defines PAT as "a system for designing, analyzing, and controlling manufacturing through timely measurements (i.e., during processing) of critical quality and performance attributes of raw and in-process materials and processes". (Twenty years of regulatory acceptance favours incumbents over any new model class.) — FDA, "PAT — A Framework for Innovative Pharmaceutical Development, Manufacturing, and Quality Assurance", 2004 — [Source](https://www.fda.gov/media/71012/download)
- **[MIXED]** ICH Q8(R2): design space is "The multidimensional combination and interaction of input variables (e.g., material attributes) and process parameters that have been demonstrated to provide assurance of quality. Working within the design space is not considered as a change." RTRT is "The ability to evaluate and ensure the quality of in-process and/or final product based on process data". (The regulatory object is a continuous multidimensional region, not a text artefact.) — ICH Q8(R2) Pharmaceutical Development, Step 4, August 2009 — [Source](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q8%28R2%29%20Guideline.pdf)
- **[MIXED]** ICH Q13 on process models: "Model validation assesses the fitness of the model for its intended use based on predetermined acceptance criteria using statistically sound approaches"; "During commercial manufacture, model maintenance and monitoring of model performance should occur on a routine and ongoing basis"; "Depending on the extent of a change and its impact on model performance, a model may need to be redeveloped and validated." It defers model-impact categories to the ICH Q8/Q9/Q10 Points to Consider. — ICH Q13, Step 5 (EMA/6401/2023); Step 4 adopted 16 November 2022 — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/ich-guideline-q13-continuous-manufacturing-drug-substances-drug-products-step-5_en.pdf)
- **[MIXED]** ICH Q14 (Step 4 on 1 November 2023; in effect in the EU 14 June 2024) has a section on multivariate analytical procedures and an annex example of multivariate model lifecycle components; the FDA version includes "Recalibration and Model Maintenance" (search snippet only; text not opened). — ICH Q14 Analytical Procedure Development — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/ich-q14-guideline-analytical-procedure-development-step-5_en.pdf)
- **[MIXED]** The ICH Q8/Q9/Q10 Points to Consider (R2) "in Section 5.1 provides an example of categorisation of models into 3 levels of impact on product quality", which ISPE asked EMA to reuse instead of a binary critical/non-critical split. — ISPE, "Submission of comments on Annex 22 Artificial Intelligence", 7 October 2025 — [Source](https://ispe.org/sites/default/files/regulatory/ISPE-Commenting/2025/ISPE_Comments_-_Annex%2022%20AI%20-%20EMA_PICs_Final%20.pdf)

Documented performance and limits
- **[SUPPORTS]** Scale transfer of Raman PLS models is analyte-dependent: Berry et al. (2014), across 3 L, 200 L and 2,000 L CHO bioreactors, found PLS models scaled well for some analytes, but models "for real-time prediction of glutamate and ammonium concentrations which were built from small scale data did not perform well on manufacturing scale batches", and VCD/TCD models "exhibited some scale dependencies". (As summarised in the review.) — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** A multi-company "generic" Raman PLS study (University of Hohenheim, Bayer, Sanofi, KTH, Rentschler, GSK) pooled 1,699 spectra from 61 cultivation runs at four industrial sites (6, 22, 4 and 7 batches) on two spectrometer types. Generic models reached SEP 4.6% (glucose), 6% (lactate), 5% (glutamine) on a dilution series, versus 11.7–22.1% glucose SEP for site-specific models; but on an independent CHO culture glucose SEP was 64% (about 1.5 g/L overprediction in a 2–4 g/L range) and glutamate was poor (16%). Authors warn PLS should not be used to extrapolate. (Pooling across sites helps, yet transfer to a new process still failed for the key control analyte.) — Yousefi-Darani et al., "Generic Chemometric Models for Metabolite Concentration Prediction Based on Raman Spectra", Sensors 22(15):5581, 2022 — [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9332195/)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Where incumbents are deployed in closed loop they deliver quality effects: Raman-based glucose feedback control in two CHO processes reduced glycation by 43.4% and 57.9% versus manual bolus feeding for one cell line and raised titer by up to 25% for the other (Gibbons et al., Biotechnology Progress, btpr.3371, as reported in a trade review). PLS is described as "the dominant chemometric method". — Technology Networks, "Raman Spectroscopy for Real-Time Bioreactor Monitoring", 15 June 2026 — [Source](https://www.technologynetworks.com/biopharma/articles/raman-spectroscopy-for-real-time-bioreactor-monitoring-413593)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Purely statistical models extrapolate poorly; hybrids do better: hybrid models show "better robustness and extrapolation abilities than statistical models which struggle with time alignment issues and have limitations in predicting beyond their training regions." — Khuat et al. 2023 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09991)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Quantified extrapolation gap: trained on low-titer runs (<580 mg/L) and tested on high-titer runs, a hybrid model's scaled RMSE rose 5–10% versus 50–80% for data-driven PLS (reported in a digital-twin review; search snippet only for the numbers). — Shahab, Destro, Braatz (MIT), "Digital Twins in Biopharmaceutical Manufacturing: Review and Perspective on Human-Machine Collaborative Intelligence", arXiv:2504.00286, 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00286)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Data-driven models "often fail to extrapolate to new strains, process conditions, or scales, necessitating costly retraining for each new system"; and "Negative transfer remains poorly understood and is typically identified only after the modeling effort has been completed". — Barón Díaz et al., Biotechnology and Bioengineering 2026 — [Source](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000193214/181017391)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Maintenance burden after change: "AI models are purely data based and as such lack any mechanistic insights"; models often need recalibration after equipment changes or scale-up (Anurag Rathore, IIT Delhi). "Few bioprocesses generate data of sufficient quality and quantity for generic AI software to be applicable" (Richard Braatz, MIT). — Mike May, "Tackling AI Bottlenecks in Bioprocessing", GEN, 9 October 2024 — [Source](https://genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/tackling-ai-bottlenecks-in-bioprocessing)
- **[MIXED]** Soft-sensor benchmarking on three cell-culture datasets (one in silico, two experimental): batch learning worked in homogeneous settings; just-in-time and online learning adapted better in cold-start; feed-media composition and control strategy are meta-features that govern transferability; combining Raman predictions with lagged offline measurements improved accuracy. — Peng, Khuat, Otte, Musial, Gabrys, "Learning From Limited Data and Feedback for Cell Culture Process Monitoring: A Comparative Study", arXiv:2512.03460, December 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03460)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Cross-product knowledge transfer is an active research line that uses learned product embeddings rather than text: DataHow proposed representing product identity "by embedding vectors that serve as input to a Gaussian Process regression model", learned from process data, to share information across cell lines (demonstrated on simulated data). — Hutter, von Stosch, Cruz Bournazou, Butté (DataHow AG), "Knowledge transfer across cell lines using Hybrid Gaussian Process models with entity embedding vectors", arXiv:2011.13863, 2020 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13863)
- **[MIXED]** Classic MSPC transfer literature exists and treats transfer as a modelling problem needing special methods: García-Muñoz, MacGregor and Kourti (2005), "Product transfer between sites using Joint-Y PLS"; Facco et al. (2012), "Transfer of Process Monitoring Models between Different Plants Using Latent Variable Techniques", Ind. Eng. Chem. Res.; a 2001 Trends in Biotechnology review gives an industrial fermentation MSPC case study; a 2020 review covers batch MSPC literature 1990–2018. (search snippets only; publisher pages returned 403.) — [Facco et al. index entry](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4MOq8yv9); [Trends Biotechnol. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016%2FS0167-7799%2800%2901528-6); [2020 batch MSPC review](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/21693277.2020.1871441)

### Inferences
- The incumbent stack already uses "native" representations (latent variables, spectra, state-space and kinetic models). The documented weakness is not representation type but generalisation across products, scales, sites and time, plus maintenance cost. That is the precise gap a vertical pretrained model would need to close; the evidence that it can is the hybrid/transfer-learning literature, which is small-scale and mostly development-stage.
- ICH Q13's statement that a process model "is specific to system design and configuration and relevant material properties" means a cross-process foundation model would still be validated per system and intended use. A foundation model can at most reduce the data and effort needed for each validated instance.
- Nothing in this literature argues for text tokens as the representation for process dynamics; equally, nothing shows a large pretrained non-text model outperforming PLS/hybrid models on GMP manufacturing data.

### Gaps
- Primary texts of Wold, Nomikos/MacGregor, Kourti and SIMCA-based industrial case studies were not opened; only bibliographic existence was confirmed for the items listed.
- No quantitative industry data on model-maintenance cost (hours per recalibration, revalidation frequency) was found.
- No verified published figures on how many RTRT or MPC applications are approved or running commercially.

## 3. Regulatory constraints and what they imply for model architecture (status at 19 November 2026)

### Takeaway
EU draft Annex 22 (July 2025) does say what was reported: for critical GMP applications it covers only static, deterministic models and states that dynamic models, probabilistic-output models, generative AI and LLMs "should not be used in critical GMP applications". It is still a draft: the EudraLex Volume 4 page lists no Annex 22 as of today, and EMA's workshop report published 2 October 2026 shows the drafting group actively considering a technology-neutral, risk-based scope that would admit adaptive, probabilistic and generative models under a documented control strategy. FDA has no binding AI-in-manufacturing rule; its 2025 credibility guidance remains draft, and its 2026 enforcement signal is human review and quality-unit accountability, not a ban on any architecture.

### Cited Findings

EU: draft Annex 22 text (verbatim from the consultation PDF)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Scope, static: "The document applies to static models, i.e. models that do not adapt their performance during use by incorporating new data. The use of dynamic models which continuously and automatically learn and adapt performance during use, is not covered by this document, and should not be used in critical GMP applications." — European Commission, EudraLex Volume 4, draft "Annex 22: Artificial Intelligence" (consultation version, July 2025) — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Scope, deterministic: "The document applies to models with a deterministic output which, when given identical inputs, provide identical outputs. Models with a probabilistic output which, when given identical inputs, might not provide identical outputs are not covered by this document and should not be used in critical GMP applications." — draft Annex 22 — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Scope, generative AI: "the document does not apply to Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLM), and such models should not be used in critical GMP applications. If used in non-critical GMP applications, which do not have direct impact on patient safety, product quality or data integrity, personnel with adequate qualification and training should always be responsible for ensuring that the outputs from such models are suitable for the intended use, i.e. a human-in-the-loop (HITL)". — draft Annex 22 — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- As drafted in 2025, Annex 22 structurally favours frozen, deterministic, discriminative models with feature attribution and confidence scores (classical ML, chemometrics, vision classifiers) for critical decisions, and confines LLMs to non-critical, human-reviewed use. That would support the "control" leg of the thesis in the EU. But the October 2026 workshop report shows the scope under active reconsideration toward technology neutrality, so a founder should not treat an LLM ban as a durable moat.
- Requirements that look stable across FDA, EMA and ISPE regardless of architecture: defined intended use / context of use; pre-specified acceptance criteria; independent test data; life-cycle monitoring and change control; evidence access from suppliers; documented human accountability in the quality unit. These favour systems whose weights, versions and logs the manufacturer controls. That supports "own or pin your model" (vertical or open-weight) over a rented frontier API that changes underneath the user, though it does not by itself require a non-text architecture.
- "Deterministic" in the draft is a property of the deployed system (identical inputs give identical outputs), not of an architecture family; a frozen sequence model with fixed decoding could satisfy the wording, while a stochastic classical model would not. The draft's separate, explicit exclusion of "Generative AI and Large Language Models" is what makes it architecture-specific.
- The draft's testing concept (characterise the "input sample space", stratified subgroups, confusion-matrix metrics) presumes bounded-input predict/classify tasks. Open-ended generation has no enumerable sample space, which is a real validation obstacle for LLMs in critical roles and the reason ISPE proposed alternative metrics.
- FDA's posture is risk-based and architecture-agnostic: lower model influence (independent verification, human sign-off) lowers the credibility burden. That makes "LLM proposes, validated model/human disposes" designs the regulatory path of least resistance in the US.

## 5. Where LLMs are working in pharma manufacturing and quality, and where they have failed or been restricted

### Takeaway
LLMs are in real use on the text layer: deviation drafting and retrieval, deviation/CAPA trend clustering, batch-record digitisation, document Q&A and natural-language access to data. Published evaluations are small and mostly qualitative, always human-in-the-loop, and the hard numbers that exist show useful but imperfect accuracy. Restrictions come from regulators (draft Annex 22; FDA's 2026 warning letter) and from evidence that LLMs and time-series foundation models underperform on numeric, causal and control tasks.

Working (with evidence quality noted)

- **[UNDERMINES]** LLM as interface over validated analytics is the pattern several practitioners endorse: LLMs "may add value as a chat-based interface that orchestrates existing validated analytical pipelines" (Mewburn Ellis summary of the 2026 Global Bioprocessing & Biotechnology Summit, Berlin); "GenAI acts as the common interface connecting them" (Schanin). — Anja Koller, Mewburn Ellis, 4 June 2026 — [Source](https://www.mewburn.com/forward/ai-in-bioprocessing-whats-behind-the-buzzword); Schanin 2026 — [Source](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/can-genai-transform-biomanufacturing-its-complicated)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Regulatory room for this use: draft Annex 22 itself allows generative AI/LLMs in non-critical GMP applications with a qualified human responsible for outputs; a trade summary lists "summarising deviation reports, searching SOPs, or drafting maintenance notes" as permitted non-critical uses. — draft Annex 22 — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf); Adam Cross (Octave, sponsored), European Pharmaceutical Review, 15 June 2026 — [Source](https://www.europeanpharmaceuticalreview.com/what-annex-22-spells-for-ai-in-gmp-manufacturing/2135686.article)

Failing or restricted
- **[SUPPORTS]** FDA warning letter (April 2026) over unreviewed AI-generated specifications, procedures and master production/control records; FDA position that AI outputs "must be reviewed and cleared by an authorized human representative" of the quality unit. — Epstein Becker Green, 28 May 2026 — [Source](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/fda-warns-against-over-reliance-on-ai-pharmaceutical-manufacturing-but-how-much-reliance-is-too-much)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Draft Annex 22 excludes generative AI/LLMs from critical GMP applications (still draft; see section 3). — [Source](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTS]** A preprint on LLM assurance in regulated life sciences reportedly documents a triage LLM that hallucinated a plausible but non-existent document even though retrieval returned the correct record (search snippet only; only the title was confirmed on the page). — "Establishing AI State of Control: An Error Taxonomy and Assurance Framework for Transformer-Based Probabilistic Systems in Regulated Life Sciences", Research Square preprint — [Source](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10476545/v1)
- **[SUPPORTS]** LLMs on industrial sensor telemetry: FactoryBench (70,000+ Q&A items over about 15,000 episodes of robot telemetry from a UR3 cobot and KUKA KR10 arm) finds that across six frontier LLMs zero-shot, no model exceeds 50% on structured levels or 18% on decision-making. (Discrete manufacturing/robotics, not pharma.) — Merzouki et al., "FactoryBench: Evaluating Industrial Machine Understanding", arXiv:2605.07675, 2026 (NeurIPS 2026 workshop oral per arXiv comments) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07675)
- **[SUPPORTS]** LLM-based time-series forecasters add nothing over simple components: removing the LLM or replacing it with a basic attention layer "does not degrade forecasting performance"; pretrained LLMs "do no better than models trained from scratch", do not represent sequential dependencies, and do not help in few-shot settings. — Tan, Merrill, Gupta, Althoff, Hartvigsen, "Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?", NeurIPS 2024 (Spotlight), arXiv:2406.16964 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- **[SUPPORTS]** LLMs lack physical constraints and reliable multi-step numerics: an LLM "does not inherently enforce conservation laws, mathematical balances, or equipment constraints"; without a validated physical model a recommendation "remains quantitatively unverified"; standalone LLMs designing feeding strategies "will only repeat the failures of the last decade." (vendor-authored opinion) — Schanin (Algocell), 2 September 2026 — [Source](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/can-genai-transform-biomanufacturing-its-complicated)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Expectation gap: 70% of 124 surveyed bioprocessing professionals report a gap between expected and actual AI performance (vendor survey). — Schanin 2026 — [Source](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/can-genai-transform-biomanufacturing-its-complicated)

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence against the thesis is on the document layer: general LLMs with retrieval and human review are already adequate for drafting, search, trending and extraction, and both FDA and the draft Annex 22 leave that space open provided a qualified human owns the output.
- The strongest evidence for the thesis is on the numeric/physical layer: general LLMs and even generic pretrained time-series models do not reliably model dynamics, causality or control; nobody in the retrieved sources proposes an LLM as the predictive model for a CPP-to-CQA relationship.
- The practical architecture implied by practitioners is a split: language model for intent, retrieval, orchestration and narrative; validated numeric model (PLS, hybrid, MPC, vision classifier) as source of truth. Whether the numeric component needs to be a "vertical foundation model" or a set of small per-process models is the open question.
- Quantified, peer-reviewed evaluations of LLMs on GMP tasks are nearly absent; most positive claims are qualitative or vendor-reported. Claims of "X% faster investigations" should be treated as unverified.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benchmark of LLM deviation classification or batch-record review against QA reviewers on GMP data was found.
- No verified evidence on LLM use for regulatory (CMC) writing, tech-transfer documents or SOP Q&A with accuracy figures.
- Katalyze AI's sponsored Fierce Pharma piece ("Why a deviation investigation still takes two weeks in the age of AI") returned 403; not used.

## 7. Published practitioner and academic arguments: will LLMs/agents suffice, or are specialised models needed?

### Takeaway
Credible chemical-engineering and bioprocess voices mostly argue that LLMs alone are insufficient for the physics-bound core and that the durable answer is hybrid, knowledge-infused models, with LLMs as the interface. But recent benchmarks also show that generic pretrained time-series foundation models are not yet reliably better than simple linear baselines for control, so "native representation + pretraining" is unproven; the position best supported by sources is "specialised numeric models + LLM interface", which is neither pole of the thesis.

### Cited Findings
- **[SUPPORTS]** Venkatasubramanian and Chakraborty: LLM "success is limited in highly scientific domains as they cannot yet reason, plan, or explain"; "the long-term success of AI in scientific and engineering domains would depend on developing hybrid AI systems" combining first principles and technical knowledge, which they call "Large Knowledge Models (LKMs)". — "Quo Vadis ChatGPT? From Large Language Models to Large Knowledge Models", arXiv:2405.19561 (related DOI 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2024.108895, Computers & Chemical Engineering), 2024 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19561)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Braatz (MIT): "Few bioprocesses generate data of sufficient quality and quantity for generic AI software to be applicable." Rathore (IIT Delhi): "hybrid models—combinations of mechanistic and data based—would be preferred over purely data-based models." Xie (Northeastern): "Black-box modeling and AI/ML approaches do not incorporate bioprocessing mechanisms". — GEN, 9 October 2024 — [Source](https://genengnews.com/topics/bioprocessing/tackling-ai-bottlenecks-in-bioprocessing)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Schanin (Algocell, vendor): the hybrid model is the source of truth and "GenAI is only as useful as the intelligence sitting behind its interface." — [Source](https://proteinproductiontechnology.com/post/can-genai-transform-biomanufacturing-its-complicated)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Koller (Mewburn Ellis, reporting a 2026 industry summit): "'AI' in current and cutting edge bioprocessing is rarely about artificial intelligence in the popular sense"; "progress in bioprocessing is driven more by digitalisation than by AI in the narrow sense"; AI in bioprocessing "is not a disruptive break". (Implies the binding constraint is data plumbing and workflow, not model class.) — [Source](https://www.mewburn.com/forward/ai-in-bioprocessing-whats-behind-the-buzzword)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Butté (DataHow, sponsored): data readiness, not model type, is the barrier; siloed, inconsistent process data means "no model can compensate for that." — [Source](https://chenected.aiche.org/2026/04/data-driven-bioprocessing-conversation-datahows-alessandro-butte)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Generic time-series foundation models as process simulators: on noiseless linear processes, "the tested TSFMs do not consistently outperform a history-validated linear baseline" in prediction or closed-loop MPC; diagnostics show direction, timing and magnitude errors in response to control inputs; prediction ranking does not predict control ranking. (Undermines the "pretrained native model will win on accuracy and control" half of the thesis as of 2026; also shows generic pretraining is not enough, which a vertical-model advocate could read as support for domain-specific pretraining.) — Tu et al., "Can Time-Series Foundation Models Serve as Simulators for Process Control?", NeurIPS 2026 workshop "Foundation Models for Temporal Systems" — [Source](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175101)
- **[MIXED]** Open TSFMs in manufacturing: in a structured steel-plant energy setting "TSFMs consistently outperform classical baselines, even without task-specific training"; in a highly dynamic CNC milling process there were "limited TSFM gains without domain-specific signals, with simple models excelling once control covariates are provided." — Calisir and Leszek (TU Berlin), "A comparison of open time-series foundation models for industrial manufacturing applications", ESANN 2026 — [Source](https://www.esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/2026/ES2026-310.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTS]** Tan et al. (NeurIPS 2024): language pretraining gives no forecasting benefit (see section 5). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)
- **[MIXED]** Bioprocess transfer-learning review (2026): transfer learning and pretrained models are "a way forward for creating more data-efficient, generalizable, and interpretable models", but the field still lacks "standardized benchmark data sets", and future work "will most likely focus on integrating TL with hybrid and physics-informed modeling frameworks". Meta- and transfer learning are "relatively new for bioprocess applications" and not widely adopted by industry (Peng et al. 2025). — Barón Díaz et al. 2026 — [Source](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000193214/181017391); Peng et al. 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12322v2)
- **[MIXED]** Human factors: digital-twin work "often overlooks the critical role of human operators"; the authors propose human-machine collaborative intelligence for biopharma manufacturing. — Shahab, Destro, Braatz, arXiv:2504.00286, 2025 — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00286)
- **[UNDERMINES]** Industry associations' consolidated position to EMA (2026): adaptive, probabilistic and generative models "should not be excluded from GMP applications on the basis of a technology label alone"; layered controls (input, in-model, output; RAG with citation binding, conformal prediction, uncertainty quantification, confidence gates, fallback to validated states) can deliver a state of control. This is the "harness is enough for compliance" argument, made by industry to the regulator; EMA recorded it as input, not as an accepted conclusion. — EMA workshop report — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)
- **[SUPPORTS]** The same report records the limit of harnesses: "guardrails are specific controls for specific risks and ... unanticipated failure modes cannot be assumed to be covered by controls designed for known failure modes." — EMA workshop report — [Source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Evidence for the thesis: (a) process data are non-text, multi-rate and causal, and language pretraining does not help with them; (b) LLMs do not enforce conservation laws or do reliable numerics; (c) EU draft rules and FDA enforcement keep LLMs out of critical decisions or under mandatory human review; (d) regulators want evidence access, version control and fallback states, which favour owned, pinned, specialised models.
- Evidence against the thesis: (a) the text layer of GMP work is large and LLM-suitable today; (b) the regulatory trend is toward technology neutrality, so harness-plus-controls may become acceptable even for higher-risk uses; (c) generic pretrained numeric foundation models do not yet beat simple baselines for control, and no pharma-manufacturing vertical foundation model with published results was found; (d) data scarcity, silos and data rights limit any pretraining strategy; (e) practitioners locate the bottleneck in digitalisation and data readiness.
- The sources converge on a third position: small, specialised, mechanistically informed numeric models (not necessarily "foundation" scale) for the physical process, with a general LLM as retrieval, orchestration and documentation layer under human accountability. A founder testing the thesis should separate its two claims: "LLMs + harness are insufficient for the physical-process layer" (well supported) and "vertical foundation models will win" (plausible, backed by investor interest and transfer-learning research, but without a manufacturing proof point in the material retrieved).

### Gaps
- No BioPhorum, ISPE Pharma 4.0, or McKinsey position paper specifically arguing LLM sufficiency versus specialised models was retrieved. No peer-reviewed "foundation models for bioprocessing" position paper was found by the one search run on that phrase; its absence is not established.
- No head-to-head published comparison (LLM agent + tools versus hybrid/specialised model) on a real bioprocess prediction or control task was found.
- Evidence on TSFMs is from general industrial settings (steel, CNC, linear benchmark processes), not GMP pharma data.
