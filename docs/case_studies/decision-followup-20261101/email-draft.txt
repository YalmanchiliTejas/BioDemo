I'm sharing the latest evaluation of Sievance's hypothesis that adding mechanistic tools can improve an agent's manufacturing decisions.

We ran the same Modal GLM-5.3 agent with and without the tool on 24 prespecified simulated receiving-process scenarios. Both arms received identical evidence and the same constraint-first decision policy. The endpoint responses identify GLM-5.3; model requests, submissions, failures and outcome replays are retained.

We measured 10/24 correct decisions with GLM alone versus 23/24 with GLM plus the mechanistic model. Constraint-violating operating decisions were 14 and 1, respectively. The paired correct-decision difference was +54.2 percentage points (descriptive 95% interval +33.3 to +75.0). Model access improved constrained decisions in this simulated cohort.

The conventional model-only selector also scored 23/24; added agent value over that selector was not demonstrated. These are synthetic process outcomes using a shared mechanistic equation family, including deliberately mismatched receiving conditions. They do not establish real manufacturing performance, product quality, or avoided losses. The earlier real CHO forecast comparison did not show benefit from the selected model.
