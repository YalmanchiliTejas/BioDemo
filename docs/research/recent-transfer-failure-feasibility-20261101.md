# Recent manufacturing and scale-up setbacks: 2023–2024 shortlist

Research date: 2026-11-01. Supersedes the older-case shortlist for the user's requested recency. Research only; no implementation or paid agent runs. Product direction: `Main_Culture_andETHOS.md`.

## Finding

There are relevant recent incidents, but the inspected public record does not yet supply a case that simultaneously establishes a 2023–2024 technology-transfer/scale-up failure, an isolated multi-million-dollar loss, a known physical root cause and sufficient inputs to validate historical prevention. Do not relax those distinctions to produce an impressive financial headline.

Sanofi Framingham's bulk-transfer pressure/leakage problem documented in its 2024 inspection is a **conditional hydraulic-model lead**, not a demonstrated fit for the platform's existing biological/lyophilization models. This is fluid transfer, not evidence that a technology transfer caused the problem. The clearest recent **named scale-up/ramp** lead is Catalent Harmans in 2023. GenSight supplies a documented 2023 failed GMP batch; Abeona supplies a 2024 manufacturing-validation/regulatory setback. Mechanistic benefit remains a hypothesis in all four.

## 1. Sanofi/Genzyme Framingham: inspected June–July 2024

**Primary source:** [FDA warning letter, January 15, 2025](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/sanofi-690604-01152025), replacing a December 16, 2024 letter.

**Observed:** FDA describes three high-pressure events with leakage during bulk-product transfer. Separately, investigations of out-of-specification appearance events disregarded a contradictory engineering study; the letter does not establish that this study explained the pressure events. It identifies March 2024 contamination and June 2023 equipment-boundary concerns. Its approximately 20% rejected-bioreactor-run figure spans **January 2022–July 2024**, not 2024 alone. These are manufacturing-control and investigation failures; the letter does not establish a failed technology transfer or disclose an isolated dollar loss. Product identities, equipment and quantities are extensively redacted.

**Model/agent hypothesis:** A hydraulic pressure/flow model could test transient pressure, restrictions and receiving-bag constraints against instrument and equipment limits. The agent could reconcile batch records, the engineering study, deviation histories and CAPA rationales, then flag incompatible explanations. The documented pressure observations do not establish the actual hydraulic cause or prove that model recommendations would prevent leakage or contamination.

**Data gate:** Require piping/filter/component geometry, fluid properties, pump/valve states, pressure/flow traces, equipment ratings, the engineering study and batch-matched outcomes. Test pressure predictions independently and assess whether the agent retrieves counterevidence. Do not infer redacted specifications or estimate loss from company-wide sales.

## 2. Catalent Harmans/BWI: capacity ramp setback in early 2023

**Primary source:** [Catalent quarter-ended March 31, 2023 Form 10-Q, Recent Developments](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1596783/000159678323000038/ctlt-20230331.htm).

**Observed:** Gene-therapy capacity expansion/ramp was slower than planned, leaving batches unproduced and revenue shifted beyond the fiscal year. Site resources were also occupied by regulatory inspections. Catalent expected related revenue recovery in the second half of calendar 2023 and did not expect adverse product-quality or commercial-launch-quantity effects at BWI. This is a capacity/ramp execution setback, not proof that manufactured product failed.

**Money:** The same filing records an expected reduction of **more than $400m each in company-wide fiscal-2023 revenue and adjusted-EBITDA guidance** amid operational issues and forecasting problems. These are overlapping financial measures, not amounts to add; neither is an isolated BWI loss or preventable amount. Consumer-health goodwill impairment is separate and should not be attached to the gene-therapy ramp.

**Model/agent hypothesis:** Start with capacity/discrete-event scheduling, equipment availability, staffing and inspection constraints. Add biological process mechanics only if batch data establishes a biological bottleneck. Require planned-versus-actual operation timelines, yields, downtime, staffing and readiness records; the public filing does not supply them.

## 3. GenSight LUMEVOQ: terminated batch in March 2023

**Primary sources:** [April 2023 operational update](https://www.gensight-biologics.com/2023/04/21/gensight-biologics-reports-cash-position-as-of-march-31-2023-and-provides-business-update/), [2023 interim financial results](https://www.gensight-biologics.com/2023/09/15/gensight-biologics-reports-interim-financial-results-for-the-first-half-of-2023/), [2023 annual report, published April 2024](https://www.gensight-biologics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/GENSIGHT_URD_2023_2024-04-17_VDEF.pdf).

**Observed:** A GMP batch was terminated in March 2023 because of an operational issue at the US manufacturing partner. GenSight reported confirming the cause in April and increasing engineering involvement and continuous person-in-plant supervision. This is a new 2023 batch failure within a longer manufacturing history; do not present every earlier disruption as a 2023 incident.

**Money:** Company-wide 2023 R&D expense was approximately **€19.4m**. That is program expenditure, not the cost of the terminated batch. The interrupted early-access supply described in interim results began before this incident, so it cannot all be assigned to the March 2023 batch. Isolated failure cost remains unverified.

**Model/agent hypothesis:** Transfer/readiness checks and reconciliation of operating instructions, training, engineering support and batch histories are plausible. A particular kinetic, mixing or heat-transfer model cannot be selected from the inspected high-level description alone. First obtain the detailed root-cause presentation/report, process history and equipment/operating records.

## 4. Abeona pz-cel: April 2024 manufacturing-validation setback

**Primary sources:** [April 22, 2024 company announcement](https://investors.abeonatherapeutics.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/276/abeona-therapeutics-provides-regulatory-update-on-pz-cel), [2024 results published March 2025](https://investors.abeonatherapeutics.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/301/abeona-therapeutics-reports-full-year-2024-financial-results-provides-pz-cel-regulatory-update-and-commercial-launch-plans).

**Observed:** FDA's complete response requested additional CMC information, including manufacturing and release-testing validation. The company said no clinical efficacy/safety deficiencies or new clinical studies were identified/requested. This is not evidence that a specific physical scale-up mechanism or a batch failed.

**Money:** **$34.4m 2024 company R&D expense**, including manufacturing-capacity expansion activity; not an isolated cost of the CRL or a prevented-loss estimate.

**Model/agent hypothesis:** A source-linked validation-readiness assessment is more directly relevant than a physical simulator. Test whether the agent identifies missing validation evidence and links it to a reviewable action. It cannot substitute simulation for required assay validation or guarantee regulatory acceptance.

## Recent publication is not necessarily a recent incident

The [2023 freeze-drying transfer case-study paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1208/s12249-023-02553-4) describes incomplete primary drying after scale/load or vial changes, with observations and mechanistic interpretation. It is attractive for testing heat/mass-transfer calculations. However, the cases are anonymous, incident years are not established in the inspected descriptions, and dollar losses are not given. Use it as a recent technical report, **not as a verified 2023 incident with millions lost**.

## Decision before building

Retain **Sanofi pressure/leakage** only as a conditional data-discovery lead. It would require a separate hydraulic model and quantitative transfer records; no inspected evidence establishes that the current platform models can explain this failure. Do not prioritize it as a ready mechanistic demonstration based on the warning letter alone. Use **GenSight** for a commercialization/transfer-readiness story if its detailed failure records can be obtained. Keep Catalent for capacity planning; do not force a bioreactor simulator onto a scheduling problem.

Compare rules-only, agent-only, model-only and combined approaches using identical predecision records and independent measured outcomes. Show predicted/observed pressure charts, source-linked discrepancies and proposed actions requiring review. Until those inputs exist, describe the work as a literature-based risk hypothesis, not a reconstruction proving prevention or savings.

This fits the four pillars by starting with existing authorized records, preserving source evidence and uncertainty, converting gaps into specific reviewed actions, and retaining existing systems of record. No confidential plant data, company access or manufacturing changes were requested or performed in this research.
