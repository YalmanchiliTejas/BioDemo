### Takeaway

## 2. Does Annex 22 as drafted permit generative AI / LLMs or agents in critical GMP applications? What exactly does it say about non-critical use and human-in-the-loop?

### Takeaway
No. The draft says generative AI and LLMs "should not be used in critical GMP applications" and allows non-critical use only with a qualified person responsible for the outputs; it never mentions agents. EMA's October 2026 report shows the drafting group weighing a wider scope under a documented control strategy, with no decision.

### Cited Findings
Draft text, with the draft's line numbers — [A22 draft](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)

| Topic | § | Draft text |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | 1, l.5–8 | Systems "where Artificial Intelligence models are used in critical applications with direct impact on patient safety, product quality or data integrity" |
| Dynamic, probabilistic models | 1, l.12–19 | Dynamic models "which continuously and automatically learn and adapt performance during use" and "Models with a probabilistic output" are "not covered by this document" and "should not be used in critical GMP applications." |
| Generative AI, LLMs | 1, l.20–25 | "the document does not apply to Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLM), and such models should not be used in critical GMP applications. If used in non-critical GMP applications, which do not have direct impact on patient safety, product quality or data integrity, personnel with adequate qualification and training should always be responsible for ensuring that the outputs from such models are suitable for the intended use, i.e. a human-in-the-loop (HITL) and the principles described in this document may be considered where applicable." |
| Human-in-the-loop | 3.3; 10.5 | Where "the effort to test such model has been diminished", intended use states "the responsibility of the operator" and "the training and consistent performance of the operator should be monitored like any other manual process."; records "may imply a consistent review and/or test of every output from the model" |
| Validation | 3.1; 4.3; 6.1 | Intended use "documented and approved before the start of acceptance testing"; criteria "at least as high as the performance of the process it replaces"; test data "is not used during development, training or validation of the model" |
| Operation | 8.1; 9.1–9.2; 10.1; 10.3–10.4 | "capture and record the features in the test data that have contributed to a particular classification or decision"; log confidence scores and flag "the outcome as ‘undecided’"; "Any change to the model itself, the system, or the process in which it is used" is assessed for retest; monitor performance and input drift |

EMA workshop report (industry positions and session "key messages", not adopted text) — [EMA report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/report/report-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22_en.pdf)
- "The drafting group has therefore discussed the scope of Annex 22 to allow dynamic or adaptive models, probabilistic models, and Generative AI/LLMs in GMP applications, provided they comply with the requirements of the annex and are supported by a fully documented, robust, risk-based control strategy." "However, concerns remain about the elements needed for such a control strategy."
- "Human-in-the-loop is one possible control, not a default requirement for all AI uses."; "Accountability remains with qualified/responsible persons and cannot be delegated to AI."; "Oversight should be evidenced and periodically reviewed; it cannot compensate for inadequate validation."; "Supplier or API models must provide sufficient evidence and transparency for control."
- Regulators asked "how to manage automation bias"; proposed evidence included "audit trails, recording of AI outputs and human modifications". Use cases included "an agentic AI system supporting deviation management".

Stakeholder feedback
- ISPE (7 Oct 2025): allow "dynamic models, those with probabilistic outputs, Generative AI, and Large Language Models" in critical uses when justified; "a binary classification oversimplifies the spectrum of risk" — [ISPE comments](https://ispewebassets.org/files/regulatory/ISPE-Commenting/2025/ISPE_Comments_-_Annex%2022%20AI%20-%20EMA_PICs_Final%20.pdf)
- Associations including EFPIA asked for "generative AI and large language models (LLMs) in critical GMP applications if supported by a robust risk-based framework"; regulators: "There is no current regulatory position on the points raised by industry during the meeting." — [HMA-EMA notes](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/minutes/summary-notes-meeting-hma-ema-group-focused-ai-industry-stakeholders-february-2026_en.pdf)
- Reported regulator talk (9 Dec 2025): options are "Maintain the ban", "Human-in-the-loop", "Technical guardrails" — [ISPE PE 2026](https://ispe.org/pharmaceutical-engineering/may-june-2026/innovation-and-regulation-danish-medicines-agency-insights) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Under the draft as written, an LLM-based agent fits only uses documented as non-critical, with a qualified person answerable for each output; a reviewer who substitutes for model testing becomes a documented, monitored part of the process.

### Gaps
- "Critical" and "direct impact" are undefined, so the status of drafting, summarising or investigation support is unsettled.
- EFPIA and PDA written comments: could not verify. ## 3. Under Part 11 and EU rules, to whom must an agent's action be attributed, and what must an audit trail capture when software creates or changes a GMP record at a person's request or on its own schedule?

### Takeaway
Signatures attach to a unique individual; no text provides for a non-human signatory. Records must show who or which system acted, when, what changed and why. PIC/S, MHRA and draft Chapter 4 accept attribution to a "computerised system" or "original data source" for automatically generated data; none addresses AI agents or scheduled actions.

### Inferences
- An agent cannot be the signatory: each text ties a signature to an individual's identity and accountability.
- An agent-made change is best attributed twice: to a unique system identity and version, and to the person who requested or authorised it (for scheduled actions, the approved schedule and its approver). No text prescribes this.
- An agent using a person's login would blur attribution as the shared-login texts describe.

### Gaps
- No text says how to attribute autonomous or scheduled AI actions; draft Annex 11 §12.1 covers only "manual user interactions". Draft wording may change.

## 4. Which decisions must by regulation be made by a named human role (quality unit, Qualified Person), so that an agent may only prepare them?

### Takeaway
In the US the quality control unit approves or rejects materials, products, procedures and specifications and approves batch records before release. In the EU a Qualified Person certifies each batch, a duty delegable "only to other Qualified Person(s)". FDA's April 2026 warning letter applies this to AI output.

### Cited Findings
- The unit has "the responsibility and authority to approve or reject all components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, packaging material, labeling, and drug products" and approves or rejects "all procedures or specifications impacting on the identity, strength, quality, and purity of the drug product" — [211.22(a), (c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-12/title-21.xml?part=211&section=211.22); the unit is "any person or organizational element designated by the firm" — [210.3(b)(15)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-12/title-21.xml?part=210&section=210.3)
- Records "shall be reviewed and approved by the quality control unit to determine compliance with all established, approved written procedures before a batch is released or distributed" — [211.192](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-12/title-21.xml?part=211&section=211.192)
- Automation meets two-person rules only if "one person checks that the equipment properly performed the operation", and the batch record names that checker — [211.68(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-12/title-21.xml?part=211&section=211.68); [211.188(b)(11)](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-12/title-21.xml?part=211&section=211.188)
- Warning letter: "you utilized artificial intelligence (AI) agents"; "you must review the AI generated documents to ensure they were accurate and actually compliant with CGMP. Your failure to do so is a violation of 21 CFR 211.22(c)."; "any output or recommendations from an AI agent must be reviewed and cleared by an authorized human representative of your firm’s QU" — [FDA WL](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/purolea-cosmetics-lab-722591-04022026)
- "No batch of product is released for sale or supply prior to certification by a Qualified Person" — [Ch1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/e458c423-f564-4171-b344-030a461c567f_en?filename=vol4-chap1_2013-01_en.pdf) 1.9(vii)
- "The responsibilities of a Qualified Person may be delegated, but only to other Qualified Person(s)." — [Ch2](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/11f4f8e6-a6e9-4897-afe3-f21e1dc56cb8_en?filename=2014-03_chapter_2.pdf) 2.6
- "Certification can only be performed by a QP"; "The QP must personally ensure" the operational responsibilities; supporting tasks "may be delegated to appropriately trained personnel or third parties" where reliance is "well founded" — [A16](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/20c41532-33d5-4635-ae80-8735d3d09fe0_en?filename=v4_an16_201510_en.pdf) 1.1, 1.6, 1.7
- "the system should allow only Qualified Persons to certify the release of the batches"; "This should be performed using an electronic signature." — [A11 2011](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/8d305550-dd22-4dad-8463-2ddb4a1345f1_en?filename=annex11_01-2011_en.pdf) §15

### Inferences
- An agent may assemble evidence, draft documents and propose dispositions; approve/reject, certification, and approval of procedures and specifications stay with the named human role. Annex 16 §1.7 leaves room for agent-prepared review packages if the reliance is well founded.

### Gaps
- No text says how much AI-prepared material a reviewer may accept without re-deriving it; the letter says only "reviewed and cleared".

## 5. What do FDA and EMA currently say about validating or establishing the credibility of AI used in manufacturing, as distinct from AI used to produce evidence for submissions?

### Takeaway
Neither has final AI-specific manufacturing guidance. FDA's only detailed framework is the January 2025 draft, still not finalised, for AI that informs regulatory decisions; it reaches manufacturing by example and says CGMP and quality-unit responsibility still apply. In the EU, Annex 11 (2011) validation and the reflection paper's risk principles apply pending Annex 22.

### Cited Findings
FDA
- Seven steps: question of interest; context of use; model risk; credibility plan; execute; document results and deviations; decide adequacy. "Model risk is a combination of two factors: (a) model influence" and "(b) decision consequence". Out of scope: "operational efficiencies (e.g., internal workflows, resource allocation, drafting/writing a regulatory submission) that do not impact patient safety, drug quality, or the reliability of results" — [FDA AI draft](https://www.fda.gov/media/184830/download) IV.A; lines 47–49
- "The use of AI in manufacturing (e.g., production and process controls) must be implemented in accordance with current good manufacturing practice"; "The quality control unit is ultimately responsible for ensuring the overall quality of the final drug product". Evaluation should "consider the performance of the human-AI team"; changes go through "the manufacturer’s change management system" — same, fn 21; lines 448, 541
- Planned drafts, not marked published in the July 2026 agenda: "AI and ML Quality Considerations in Pharmaceutical Manufacturing"; "Computer Software Assurance for AI-Based Systems in Drug Manufacturing and Clinical Investigations; Supplemental Guidance" — [CDER agenda](https://www.fda.gov/media/185228/download)
- Joint principles cover "manufacturing phases" and call for "proportionate validation, risk mitigation, and oversight based on the context of use and determined model risk" — [Principles](https://www.fda.gov/media/189581/download)

EMA / EU
- Manufacturing models "should follow the quality risk management principles, taking patient safety, data integrity and product quality into account", with ICH Q8, Q9 and Q10 considered "awaiting revision of current regulatory requirements and GMP standards" — [EMA RP](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/reflection-paper-use-artificial-intelligence-ai-medicinal-product-lifecycle_en.pdf) 2.3.6
- In force: "The application should be validated; IT infrastructure should be qualified."; "There should be no increase in the overall risk of the process." — [A11 2011](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/8d305550-dd22-4dad-8463-2ddb4a1345f1_en?filename=annex11_01-2011_en.pdf). ### Inferences
- An agent whose output can affect product quality or a GMP record falls outside the FDA draft's "operational efficiencies" carve-out and inside CGMP validation and quality-unit oversight.
- FDA's "model influence" and the draft Annex 22's human review point the same way: fewer independent checks between output and decision call for more model evidence.
- The AI Act is unlikely to class a GMP documentation or analysis agent as high-risk unless it monitors workers or is a safety component of machinery or a device.
