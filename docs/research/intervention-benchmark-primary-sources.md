# Primary sources for an oxygen-transfer intervention benchmark

Research checked 2026-10-29. This note distinguishes published physical observations, simulator conventions, and proposed benchmark assumptions. It does not validate a receiving-site model or authorize a manufacturing intervention.

## Decision and product context

The job is to evaluate whether evidence and a coupled process model help someone distinguish an oxygen-transfer problem from biological variation, select an informative experiment, and choose a bounded corrective plan. Following [Main_Culture_andETHOS.md](../../Main_Culture_andETHOS.md), a useful benchmark should minimize setup (Start), expose provenance and uncertainty (Trust), connect a proposed experiment to its observed outcome (Act), and preserve ordinary historian/laboratory records and human authorization (Adopt). Observable measures include unsupported causal claims, experiments requested, assay waiting time, constraint failures, and outcome improvement; none is yet a demonstrated real-world saving.

A simple, supportable option is a declared synthetic PenSim intervention study plus a separate physical experimental case. An ambitious option is a receiver-calibrated, physical oxygen-transfer corrective-experiment benchmark. The latter needs characterization and matched intervention records not supplied by the simulator distribution. Do not substitute numerical admission for that missing evidence.

## What the original sources support

Goldrick et al. (2015), [*The development of an industrial-scale fed-batch fermentation simulation*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2014.10.029), developed IndPenSim against historical records from ten 100,000-L penicillin fermentations. This is evidence for the original model and historical operating domain, not validation of arbitrary parameter multipliers or the Python port. The author's [MathWorks distribution](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/49041-industrial-scale-penicillin-simulationv2) identifies version 2.0.1, updated July 2019. Its current download link redirects to sign-in; guessed direct-version ZIP endpoints tested here returned 404, so this session did not independently acquire that MATLAB ZIP.

The locally inspected [PenSimPy revision e08fc71](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/tree/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc) is a port, not the original author's distribution. Its [ODE](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/blob/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc/pensimpy/ode/indpensim_ode_py.py) calculates kLa from an effective coefficient, gas superficial velocity, power per volume, viscosity and oil addition. Multiplying `alpha_kla` scales kLa at otherwise identical states and commands. It does not represent a separately modeled impeller/sparger replacement, geometry, spatial mixing or a real new factory. Different source/receiver states can change the resulting kLa ratio even with a constant coefficient ratio.

## Parameter ranges: evidence classes must remain distinct

| Quantity | Direct source | Defensible interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `alpha_kla = 85 + 10 Z` | Pinned port [reset](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/blob/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc/pensimpy/peni_env_setup.py), standard-normal draw | Simulator batch variability, CV 11.76%. This coefficient is not itself kLa in h⁻¹. |
| Maximum growth `0.41 + 0.025 Z` h⁻¹ | Pinned port [initial conditions](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/blob/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc/pensimpy/data/batch_data.py) | Simulator convention, CV 6.10%; not a physical site-transfer confidence interval. |
| Maximum production `0.041 + 0.0025 Z` h⁻¹ | Same source | Simulator convention, CV 6.10%; preserve its model meaning rather than treating it as a directly assayed rate. |
| Water-characterization kLa 32.6 versus 38.8 h⁻¹ | Rastädter et al. (2023), Table 2 | Physical apparatus contrast with ring versus micro sparger at otherwise matched 2-L settings. Ratio 0.840 is calculated from rounded published values, not a universal transfer-loss distribution. |
| Mean kLa measurement SD 1.17 h⁻¹ | Same paper §3.2 | Repeat-characterization uncertainty reported across determinations; neither a row-specific confidence interval nor receiver-model uncertainty. |

For a synthetic parameter screen, nominal-relative ±2 SD in the port gives coefficient factors **0.765–1.235** and growth/production factors **0.878–1.122**. These are arithmetic descriptions of that simulator's normal draws, not empirical 95% intervals for a real receiver. If both source and receiver are randomized, their ratio is not the same as either nominal-relative interval; record the actual pairing and conditioning rule. Positivity truncation changes the distribution and must be declared.

A practical explicitly assumed screen could use oxygen-transfer factors **0.8, 1.0, 1.2** and biological factors **0.9, 1.0, 1.1** relative to the declared source model. Those are rounded synthetic design choices, broadly within the port variability, **not experimentally validated ranges**. A larger **0.6–1.4** oxygen-transfer or **0.8–1.2** biological range is an extrapolation stress test requiring its own numerical/constitutive admission. No reviewed primary source establishes those broader intervals as the distribution of physical source-to-receiver mismatch. Freeze ranges before agent evaluation; do not expand them until an advantage appears. Fit bounds such as kLa 0.5–1.5 used in previous local work are optimizer bounds, not scientific validation of those ranges.

## Measurements, noise and latency

Goldrick et al. (2019), [original published paper, Table 1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10082538/7/Goldrick_Modern%20day%20monitoring%20and%20control%20challenges%20outlined%20on%20an%20industrial-scale%20benchmark%20fermentation%20process_VoR.pdf), supports this **simulation measurement design**:

| Channel | Published availability |
|---|---|
| DO (mg/L), weight (kg), pH, temperature (K), O₂/CO₂ off-gas (%) | Every 12 minutes |
| Penicillin and biomass (g/L), PAA and nitrogen (mg/L), viscosity (cP) | Every 12 h, with a 4-h assay delay |
| Substrate | No offline measurements available |

Volume is not listed as a directly measured Table 1 channel. If derived from weight, density and balances, label it derived and expose assumptions. These timing rules do not establish that a real lab accepts off-cycle extra assays with guaranteed 4-h turnaround. Such an experiment-request interface may be a useful **declared benchmark affordance**, with separately specified cost, sampled time and result-release time.

The port's [offline sampling code](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/blob/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc/pensimpy/peni_env_setup.py) uses `k - delay - 1` with `delay=4`, although its [step](https://github.com/smpl-env/PenSimPy/blob/e08fc71fef193448f3ee9a304187dc92b88f3acc/pensimpy/constants.py) is 0.2 h. Four indices correspond to **0.8 h**, not the paper's 4 h. Preserve sampled and available timestamps explicitly rather than assuming this implementation matches the paper.

The paper describes sensor inaccuracies and synthetic Raman noise. Neither that fact, randomized initial DO, nor filtered process disturbances establishes a calibrated Gaussian error SD for DO, penicillin or biomass assays. A benchmark adding, for example, 2% assay noise or 0.1 mg/L DO noise must label these as assumed sensitivity settings and include a zero-added-noise reference. Distinguish instrument noise, bias/drift, assay replication, process variation and model discrepancy. P/DO-only calibration may confound growth, production and oxygen transfer; profile/sensitivity checks and held-out source-time checks are needed before claiming recovered biological parameters.

## Physical intervention data and actual access

**Strong adjacent candidate: assigned feeding experiments with measured failure and prevention.** Schäpertöns et al. (2026), [*Effects of feeding strategies on culture performance and product quality in NISTCHO*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-026-00686-3), compares seven feeding strategies in 20-mL TubeSpin cultures. Low glucose (4 g/L replenishment every 48 h) exhausted carbon and collapsed; 4 g/L every 24 h prevented full depletion. VCD, viability, glucose, lactate, antibody titer and glycosylation were measured. This is an actual intervention comparison, not merely observational CHO history. It is **prospective recipe assignment**, not evidence of rescuing the same already-failed culture, and does not test oxygen-transfer correction.

The paper links concept DOI `10.5281/zenodo.17046013`; the [repository API](https://zenodo.org/api/records/17046013) resolved here to [version 18609042](https://zenodo.org/records/18609042), public CC BY 4.0. Actual access was verified: HTTP byte ranges retrieved the ZIP directory and six culture-data members, which decompressed and passed the archive CRC checks. The 7,330,460,280-byte archive includes raw mass spectra; it was **not fully downloaded** and its full-file MD5 was not independently verified. Relevant members include `data/01_ViCell_growth_data.csv` (23,990 bytes), `data/03_titer.csv` (4,117), `data/04_lactate.csv` (3,241), and `data/05_glucose_data.xlsx` (29,639). Growth and titer files include condition, replicate and sampling-hour fields. Preserve the authors' condition mapping and experimental batches before forming train/test splits. Growth CSV SHA-256: `8342ee2bacbe86467464041deb7db4a90671c30ae3e78d6e8a80b393f164e7a1`; titer CSV: `d566393315de7eea193638729db83eb0d1090acae9dbd914e6966a26a887b05f`.

**Direct oxygen corrective evidence, summary-level access.** [Rastädter et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2023.1160012) observed oxygen limitation with the 2-L ring sparger. Increasing agitation 355→500 rpm raised characterized kLa 32.6→38.8 h⁻¹ and allowed omission of added oxygen; viability improved by almost 6 percentage points. This is useful physical corrective evidence, but characterization was in deionized water at 75°C. The [official Figshare API](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/23897628) supplies a DOCX supplement with figures and an equipment table, not a raw assay/DO trajectory workbook. Raw data are offered from authors. No public raw workbook was verified; authors were not contacted. Do not transfer this organism's kinetics, DO threshold or measurement SD into penicillin. Existing local [acquisition note](fermentation/sulfolobus-sources.md) records file hashes and missing 200-L viability/off-gas measurements.

**Physical pressure/feed intervention, no raw trajectory deposit verified.** [Liu et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep18439) reports 30-L Pichia pressure/oxygen comparisons and subsequent feeding optimization and scale-up. Outcomes are published tables/figures; the article does not provide a calibrated kLa/OUR response model. The separately optimized conditions confound attribution of the whole program to pressure alone. The accessible article and existing [acquisition record](fermentation/pichia-sources.md) establish physical experiments, not an open machine-readable counterfactual trajectory dataset.

**Public physical stress test, not sequential corrective rescue.** [Barczyk et al., DaRUS-6466](https://doi.org/10.18419/DARUS-6466) provides 3-L NISTCHO reference versus increased mixing-time configurations, with metabolite/VCD/product and quality outcomes. [Dataset metadata](https://darus.uni-stuttgart.de/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.18419/DARUS-6466) and actual [Figure5.csv](https://darus.uni-stuttgart.de/api/access/datafile/659820) were downloaded successfully. The latter contains time-resolved glucose/lactate comparison data. This supports physical perturbation comparisons; no claim of an already-failed vessel subsequently corrected follows from this access check.

## Data that must not be promoted into physical intervention evidence

The author's [Mendeley IndPenSim dataset v2](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/pdnjz7zz5x/2) explicitly says its 100 batches were **generated by simulation**, including control-strategy campaigns and fault batches. The original [author notebook](https://github.com/StephenGoldie/indpensim-notebook) is public and its download code was inspected; that does not change the synthetic origin. The 2015 historical validation records and 2019 generated benchmark must remain separate.

Similarly, the [Cambridge 163-culture CHO dataset](https://doi.org/10.17863/CAM.134450) describes anonymized, normalized historical development/production records. A title mentioning control does not establish that every record contains a deliberate corrective intervention with measured follow-up. The new NISTCHO feeding and mixing studies above are distinct physical experimental datasets and should not retroactively relabel earlier observational CHO benchmark results.

## Supported conclusion and remaining gap

There is enough evidence for a transparent **synthetic** oxygen-transfer/mismatched-biology benchmark with realistic measurement cadence and an independently scored physical feeding-intervention case. Public physical interventions with measured outcomes do exist. This search did **not** verify a public raw dataset containing the exact chain of penicillin receiver failure → diagnostic experiment → oxygen-transfer corrective action → measured receiving-process outcome with matched controls. The Sulfolobus paper is the closest direct corrective mechanism example, with summary/supplement evidence and author-held raw data. Any stronger claim requires additional data acquisition and qualification, not a wider guessed parameter range.

## Subsequent detailed-protocol qualification

The detailed archive member `data/Supplementary Data Application Fed Batch.xlsx` was also byte-range acquired and ZIP-CRC verified in temporary storage (SHA-256 `a9f26a29c74c71e0bf9dd06b230629536577099160c0726b6ced9931d6dd4245`). It is a calculation workbook, not an unambiguous executed dosing log. Main feed/Gal+ starts72h, not48h. Sampling withdrawals and calculated volume changes matter.

Examples requiring curation: FB4 low-glucose raw corrected glucose around5.128g/L at72h exceeds its4g/L target, and the73h export retains that value, while a shared-reference protocol formula calculates a positive glucose dose using an8g/L target. A FB2 HIP120h formula yields a negative calculated dose. The +1h rows often match replenishment targets and are not verified independent assay measurements. Raw LoG glucose conditionG also duplicates replicate1 IDs across13 timepoints while lacking replicate4; guessing that the second1 is4 would be an unsupported repair.

Consequently, a mechanistic fed-batch glucose intervention replay is not qualified without resolving replicate identities, actual feed amounts/times/composition, and sampled/derived values. Do not substitute a batch Monod model, assume workbook calculations were executed, or infer held-out future feed from held-out glucose. A descriptive grouped treatment comparison remains possible with these limits and campaign confounding. No author contact has been made.
